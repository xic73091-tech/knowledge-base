# Management

## Overview

Management is the process of dealing with or controlling things and people, particularly in organizations. It encompasses planning, organizing, staffing, leading, and directing organizational resources to achieve goals efficiently and effectively. It draws on psychology, economics, sociology, and strategy.

## Core Concepts

### Planning & Strategy
- **Strategic Planning**: Mission, vision, values; SWOT analysis; strategic goals
- **Competitive Strategy** (Porter): Cost leadership, differentiation, focus; five forces analysis
- **Business Model Canvas**: Value proposition, customer segments, channels, revenue streams, key resources, activities, partners, cost structure
- **Balanced Scorecard**: Financial, customer, internal process, learning & growth perspectives
- **OKRs**: Objectives and Key Results; goal-setting framework
- **Scenario Planning**: Exploring multiple future scenarios; strategic flexibility

### Organizational Structure
- **Structure Types**: Functional, divisional, matrix, flat, network, holacracy
- **Centralization vs Decentralization**: Decision-making authority distribution
- **Span of Control**: Number of direct reports; narrow vs wide
- **Organizational Design**: Alignment of structure with strategy; Mintzberg's configurations
- **Cross-Functional Teams**: Breaking silos; project-based collaboration

### Leadership
- **Leadership Styles**: Autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire, transformational, transactional, servant
- **Situational Leadership**: Adapting style to follower readiness (Hersey & Blanchard)
- **Emotional Intelligence**: Self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill (Goleman)
- **Authentic Leadership**: Self-awareness, relational transparency, balanced processing, moral perspective
- **Leadership vs Management**: Leaders do the right things; managers do things right (Bennis)
- **Power & Influence**: Positional power, personal power, political behavior

### Human Resource Management
- **Recruitment & Selection**: Job analysis, sourcing, interviewing, assessment, onboarding
- **Performance Management**: Goal setting, feedback, performance appraisals, 360-degree feedback
- **Compensation & Benefits**: Base pay, variable pay, equity, benefits, total rewards
- **Training & Development**: Learning needs assessment, programs, mentoring, coaching
- **Employee Relations**: Engagement, retention, discipline, grievance handling, union relations
- **Employment Law**: Anti-discrimination, labor standards, workplace safety, wrongful termination
- **Diversity, Equity, Inclusion (DEI)**: Bias awareness, inclusive practices, representation

### Decision-Making
- **Rational Model**: Define problem → generate alternatives → evaluate → choose → implement
- **Bounded Rationality** (Simon): Limited information, cognitive capacity, time; satisficing
- **Group Decision-Making**: Brainstorming, Delphi technique, nominal group technique; groupthink risks
- **Decision Frameworks**: Decision trees, cost-benefit analysis, Pareto analysis, Eisenhower matrix
- **Heuristics & Biases**: Confirmation bias, anchoring, availability, overconfidence

### Organizational Behavior
- **Motivation**: Maslow, Herzberg (two-factor), Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness), Expectancy Theory
- **Job Design**: Job enrichment, job rotation, job crafting; Hackman & Oldham model
- **Organizational Culture**: Values, norms, artifacts; Schein's model; culture assessment
- **Organizational Change**: Lewin's model (unfreeze-change-refreeze); Kotter's 8 steps; ADKAR
- **Conflict Management**: Avoiding, accommodating, competing, compromising, collaborating

### Operations & Project Management
- **Lean Management**: Waste elimination; value stream mapping; continuous improvement (kaizen)
- **Six Sigma**: DMAIC; reducing defects; statistical process control
- **Agile Management**: Scrum, Kanban; iterative development; sprints; retrospectives
- **Project Management**: PMBOK; scope, time, cost, quality; Gantt charts; critical path method
- **Risk Management**: Identification, assessment, mitigation, monitoring

### Communication
- **Business Writing**: Reports, proposals, emails; clarity, conciseness, persuasiveness
- **Presentations**: Structure, delivery, visual aids, audience engagement
- **Stakeholder Communication**: Managing expectations; transparency; feedback loops
- **Negotiation**: BATNA; principled negotiation (Fisher & Ury); win-win strategies

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Scientific Management | Taylor | Efficiency through standardization and measurement |
| Human Relations | Mayo | Social factors influence productivity (Hawthorne studies) |
| Theory X & Y | McGregor | Assumptions about workers shape management style |
| Five Forces | Porter | Industry structure determines competitive intensity |
| Core Competencies | Prahalad & Hamel | Sustainable advantage from unique capabilities |

- **Henry Fayol**: 14 principles of management; planning, organizing, commanding, coordinating, controlling
- **W. Edwards Deming**: Quality management; PDCA cycle; 14 points
- **Abraham Maslow**: Hierarchy of needs
- **Douglas McGregor**: Theory X and Theory Y

## Frontiers

- **Managing Remote & Hybrid Teams**: How to maintain cohesion, culture, and performance when teams are distributed across time and physical space
- **AI-Augmented Decision-Making**: The boundary between algorithmic recommendations and human judgment; when to defer to data versus intuition in managerial decisions
- **Organizational Resilience**: How firms build adaptive capacity to survive systemic shocks — pandemics, supply chain disruptions, geopolitical instability — rather than optimizing for efficiency alone
- **Gig Economy & Future of Work**: How management theory adapts when traditional employment relationships give way to contract, freelance, and platform-based labor
- **Purpose-Driven Leadership**: Whether stakeholder capitalism and ESG commitments represent a durable shift or remain subordinate to shareholder value pressures

## Applications

- **Consulting**: Strategy, operations, organizational design

- **Project Management**: IT, construction, event, product management
- **Entrepreneurship**: Startup management, scaling, pivoting
- **Change Management**: Digital transformation, mergers, restructuring

## Classic Works

- **"The Practice of Management"** by Peter Drucker — Established management as a discipline; introduced management by objectives (MBO) and the idea that businesses exist to create customers
- **"Organizations"** by James March & Herbert Simon — Foundational work on organizational decision-making, bounded rationality, and how firms actually behave versus idealized models
- **"Competitive Strategy"** by Michael Porter — Defined the five forces framework and generic strategies; the starting point for modern strategic management
- **"Good to Great"** by Jim Collins — Empirical study of companies that made sustained performance leaps; introduced Level 5 Leadership and the Hedgehog Concept
- **"The Human Side of Enterprise"** by Douglas McGregor — Introduced Theory X and Theory Y; reframed management assumptions about worker motivation
