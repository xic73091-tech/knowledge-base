# Operations & Supply Chain Management

## Overview

Operations management is the administration of business practices to create the highest level of efficiency possible within an organization. Supply chain management coordinates the flow of goods, services, information, and finances from raw material sourcing to final delivery. Together, they transform inputs into outputs and deliver value to customers.

## Core Concepts

### Operations Strategy
- **Competitive Priorities**: Cost, quality, flexibility, delivery speed, delivery reliability
- **Order Winners vs Qualifiers**: Distinguishing features that win orders vs minimum requirements
- **Capacity Planning**: Long-term (facilities), medium-term (workforce), short-term (scheduling)
- **Process Types**: Project, job shop, batch, mass, continuous; product-process matrix
- **Make vs Buy Decisions**: Outsourcing; core competencies; total cost of ownership

### Process Design & Analysis
- **Process Mapping**: Flowcharts, value stream mapping, swim lane diagrams
- **Bottleneck Analysis**: Theory of Constraints (Goldratt); throughput; cycle time
- **Little's Law**: L = λW (inventory = throughput rate × flow time)
- **Line Balancing**: Distributing work evenly across stations
- **Automation**: Robotic process automation (RPA); Industry 4.0; IoT in manufacturing

### Quality Management
- **Total Quality Management (TQM)**: Customer focus, continuous improvement, employee involvement
- **Six Sigma**: DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control); statistical quality control
- **Lean**: Waste elimination (8 wastes: TIMWOODS); value stream mapping; 5S; kaizen
- **ISO Standards**: ISO 9001 (quality), ISO 14001 (environmental), ISO 45001 (safety)
- **Statistical Process Control (SPC)**: Control charts; Cp, Cpk; process capability
- **Quality Tools**: Pareto charts, fishbone diagrams, scatter diagrams, histograms, check sheets

### Supply Chain Management
- **Supply Chain Design**: Network configuration; facility location; sourcing strategy
- **Procurement**: Supplier selection; strategic sourcing; e-procurement; supplier relationship management
- **Inventory Management**: EOQ (Economic Order Quantity); safety stock; ABC analysis; JIT; VMI
- **Demand Planning**: Forecasting methods (moving average, exponential smoothing, regression); S&OP
- **Logistics**: Transportation modes (road, rail, air, sea, pipeline); route optimization; last mile
- **Warehousing**: Storage; picking and packing; cross-docking; warehouse management systems (WMS)
- **Global Supply Chains**: Offshoring, nearshoring; customs, tariffs; trade compliance

### Lean Operations
- **Toyota Production System (TPS)**: Just-in-time, jidoka (autonomation), kaizen, standardized work
- **Value Stream Mapping**: Current state → future state; identifying waste
- **5S**: Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain
- **Pull Systems**: Kanban; demand-driven production; reducing overproduction
- **Continuous Improvement**: PDCA cycle; A3 problem solving; gemba walks

### Project Management
- **Methodologies**: Waterfall, Agile, Scrum, Kanban, PRINCE2, PMBOK
- **Triple Constraint**: Scope, time, cost; quality as fourth dimension
- **Scheduling**: Gantt charts, critical path method (CPM), PERT
- **Risk Management**: Identification, assessment, mitigation, monitoring
- **Stakeholder Management**: Communication plans; expectations management

### Service Operations
- **Service Design**: Service blueprinting; customer journey mapping; SERVQUAL
- **Queue Management**: Queue disciplines; waiting line models; reducing perceived wait time
- **Capacity Management**: Yield management; revenue management; demand shaping
- **Service Quality**: Reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, tangibles (SERVQUAL)

### Sustainability in Operations
- **Green Supply Chain**: Sustainable sourcing; carbon footprint reduction; circular economy
- **Reverse Logistics**: Returns management; recycling; remanufacturing
- **Triple Bottom Line**: People, planet, profit; ESG reporting
- **Life Cycle Assessment**: Cradle-to-grave environmental impact

## Frontiers

- **Supply Chain Resilience vs Efficiency**: How to redesign global supply chains for disruption readiness without sacrificing the cost advantages of lean, just-in-time systems
- **Digital Twins in Operations**: Whether virtual replicas of physical processes and supply chains can deliver predictive maintenance, scenario planning, and real-time optimization at scale
- **Autonomous Warehousing & Last-Mile Delivery**: The technical, regulatory, and economic barriers to fully autonomous logistics — from robotic picking to drone and self-driving delivery
- **Circular Economy Operations**: How to operationalize closed-loop supply chains — remanufacturing, recycling, product-as-a-service — at scale while remaining cost-competitive
- **Generative AI in Demand Planning**: Whether foundation models can outperform traditional statistical forecasting methods in volatile, multi-variate demand environments

## Classic Works

- **"The Goal"** by Eliyahu Goldratt — Introduced the Theory of Constraints through a novel format; made bottleneck thinking and throughput accounting accessible to practitioners
- **"The Toyota Production System"** by Taiichi Ohno — The origin text of lean manufacturing; explains just-in-time, kanban, and the philosophy of waste elimination from inside Toyota
- **"Operations Management"** by Jay Heizer & Barry Render — The standard university textbook; comprehensive coverage of both manufacturing and service operations
- **"The Machine That Changed the World"** by Womack, Jones & Roos — Documented the lean production system of Toyota and its superiority over mass production; catalyzed the lean movement in the West
- **"Factory Physics"** by Mark Spearman, Wallace Hopp & Michael VandeBerg — Rigorous treatment of production variability, queueing theory, and the mathematical laws governing operations
