# Supply Chain & Logistics

## Overview

Supply chain management (SCM) is the coordination of all activities involved in producing and delivering a product or service, from raw materials to the end customer. It encompasses procurement, manufacturing, warehousing, transportation, and distribution. Effective supply chain management reduces costs, improves quality, and creates competitive advantage — as the COVID-19 pandemic starkly demonstrated.

## Core Concepts

### Supply Chain Strategy
- **Push vs Pull**: Push (make-to-forecast) vs pull (make-to-order); lean vs agile supply chains
- **Responsive vs Efficient**: Fisher's framework; match supply chain type to product characteristics
- **Total Cost of Ownership**: Beyond purchase price — transportation, warehousing, obsolescence, quality costs
- **SCOR Model**: Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, Return — standard process reference framework
- **Bullwhip Effect**: Demand variability amplifies upstream; caused by forecasting, batching, rationing

### Procurement & Sourcing
- **Strategic Sourcing**: Category analysis, market research, supplier evaluation, negotiation
- **Supplier Selection**: Quality, cost, delivery, flexibility, risk; weighted scoring models
- **Supplier Relationship Management**: Transactional vs strategic partnerships; supplier development
- **Make vs Buy**: Core competency analysis; vertical integration decisions
- **Global Sourcing**: Offshoring, nearshoring, reshoring; total landed cost analysis

### Inventory Management
- **Inventory Types**: Raw materials, work-in-process (WIP), finished goods, safety stock
- **Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)**: Balancing ordering and holding costs
- **ABC Analysis**: Pareto principle; A items (high value, tight control), C items (low value, simple control)
- **Just-in-Time (JIT)**: Minimizing inventory; Toyota Production System; pull-based replenishment
- **Safety Stock**: Buffer for demand and supply uncertainty; service level targets
- **Inventory Metrics**: Inventory turnover, days of supply, fill rate, stockout rate

### Warehousing & Distribution
- **Warehouse Design**: Layout optimization; picking strategies (wave, batch, zone); slotting
- **Distribution Networks**: Centralized vs decentralized; hub-and-spoke; cross-docking
- **Order Fulfillment**: Pick, pack, ship; e-commerce fulfillment; last-mile delivery
- **Warehouse Management Systems (WMS)**: Inventory tracking, task management, labor optimization
- **Automation**: AS/RS (automated storage/retrieval), AGVs, robotic picking, goods-to-person systems

### Transportation & Freight
- **Modes**: Truck, rail, ocean, air, intermodal; trade-offs (cost, speed, capacity, reliability)
- **Route Optimization**: Vehicle routing problem; load consolidation; dynamic routing
- **Freight Management**: Less-than-truckload (LTL) vs full truckload (FTL); freight brokers
- **International Trade**: Incoterms, customs, tariffs, trade compliance, free trade zones
- **Last-Mile Delivery**: The most expensive leg; crowdsourced delivery, lockers, drones

### Demand Planning & Forecasting
- **Forecasting Methods**: Moving averages, exponential smoothing, ARIMA, causal models, machine learning
- **Demand Sensing**: Using real-time data (POS, weather, social media) to improve accuracy
- **Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP)**: Cross-functional alignment of demand, supply, and financial plans
- **Collaborative Planning**: CPFR (Collaborative Planning, Forecasting, and Replenishment)
- **Demand Shaping**: Pricing, promotions, allocation to influence demand patterns

### Supply Chain Risk & Resilience
- **Risk Categories**: Supply disruption, demand volatility, operational failures, geopolitical
- **Risk Assessment**: Probability × impact; scenario analysis; stress testing
- **Mitigation Strategies**: Dual sourcing, safety stock, nearshoring, flexible contracts
- **Resilience**: Ability to absorb, adapt, and recover from disruptions; agility and redundancy
- **Visibility**: End-to-end tracking; control towers; IoT and blockchain for traceability

### Technology & Digital Supply Chain
- **ERP Systems**: SAP, Oracle; integrated planning and execution
- **IoT & Sensors**: Real-time tracking of goods, equipment, and environmental conditions
- **Blockchain**: Supply chain transparency; provenance verification; smart contracts
- **AI & Machine Learning**: Demand forecasting, route optimization, anomaly detection, autonomous vehicles
- **Digital Twins**: Virtual replicas of supply chain for simulation and optimization
- **Control Towers**: Centralized visibility and decision-making across the supply chain

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Theory of Constraints (Goldratt) | System throughput limited by the bottleneck; focus improvement there |
| Lean Thinking (Womack & Jones) | Eliminate waste; continuous flow; pull-based production |
| Fisher's Supply Chain Match | Functional products → efficient chains; innovative products → responsive chains |
| Transaction Cost Economics | Make vs buy based on asset specificity, uncertainty, and frequency |

## Frontiers

- **Supply Chain Resilience**: Post-COVID redesign; balancing efficiency with robustness; nearshoring trends
- **Autonomous Logistics**: Self-driving trucks, delivery drones, robotic warehouses; regulatory and technical hurdles
- **Circular Supply Chains**: Reverse logistics, product reuse, recycling, remanufacturing; waste-to-value
- **AI-Native Supply Chains**: End-to-end autonomous planning and execution; human oversight for exceptions
- **Scope 3 Emissions**: Measuring and reducing supply chain carbon footprint; sustainable sourcing

## Applications

- **Manufacturing**: Raw material sourcing, production planning, just-in-time delivery
- **Retail**: Omnichannel fulfillment, inventory optimization, seasonal planning
- **Healthcare**: Pharmaceutical cold chain, medical device distribution, hospital supply management
- **E-Commerce**: Fulfillment center design, last-mile delivery, returns management
- **Food & Agriculture**: Cold chain management, food safety traceability, farm-to-fork logistics
- **Humanitarian**: Disaster relief logistics, prepositioning, last-mile distribution in remote areas

## Classic Works

- **"The Goal"** by Eliyahu Goldratt — Business novel introducing Theory of Constraints; the most influential operations management book
- **"Supply Chain Management: Strategy, Planning, and Operation"** by Chopra & Meindl — Comprehensive textbook; standard in business school curricula
- **"The Toyota Way"** by Jeffrey Liker — 14 principles of lean manufacturing from Toyota's system
- **"Designing and Managing the Supply Chain"** by Simchi-Levi, Kaminsky & Simchi-Levi — Network design, logistics, and quantitative models
- **"The Resilient Enterprise"** by Yossi Sheffi — How companies prepare for and recover from supply chain disruptions
