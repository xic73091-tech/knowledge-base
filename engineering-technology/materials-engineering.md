# Materials Engineering

## Overview

Materials engineering (or materials science and engineering) investigates the relationship between the structure of materials at atomic or molecular scales and their macroscopic properties. It covers the four major classes of materials — metals, polymers, ceramics, and composites — and develops new materials for specific applications.

## Core Concepts

### Structure of Materials
- **Atomic Bonding**: Metallic, covalent, ionic, van der Waals; bond energy curves
- **Crystal Structure**: Unit cells (FCC, BCC, HCP); Miller indices; crystallography
- **Amorphous Materials**: Glasses; polymers without crystalline order
- **Defects**: Point (vacancies, interstitials), line (dislocations), surface (grain boundaries), volume (voids)
- **Microstructure**: Grain size, phase distribution, precipitates; micrographs

### Metals & Alloys
- **Phase Diagrams**: Binary (eutectic, eutectoid, peritectic), ternary; lever rule
- **Heat Treatment**: Annealing, quenching, tempering; TTT/CCT diagrams
- **Ferrous Metals**: Carbon steel, stainless steel, cast iron; iron-carbon diagram
- **Non-Ferrous**: Aluminum, titanium, copper, nickel alloys; lightweight applications
- **Mechanical Properties**: Yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, hardness, toughness, ductility
- **Corrosion**: Electrochemical mechanisms; galvanic series; prevention (coatings, cathodic protection)

### Polymers
- **Polymerization**: Addition (free radical, ionic), condensation; copolymers
- **Structure**: Thermoplastics vs thermosets; crystallinity; molecular weight
- **Properties**: Viscoelasticity; glass transition temperature (Tg); melting temperature (Tm)
- **Engineering Plastics**: Nylon, polycarbonate, PEEK, polyethylene, ABS
- **Elastomers**: Rubber; vulcanization; natural and synthetic
- **Degradation**: UV, thermal, chemical; biodegradable polymers

### Ceramics & Glasses
- **Structure**: Crystalline ceramics (Al₂O₃, SiC, ZrO₂); amorphous glasses
- **Properties**: High hardness, high melting point, excellent wear resistance; brittle
- **Processing**: Sintering, hot pressing, sol-gel, chemical vapor deposition
- **Advanced Ceramics**: Piezoelectric, ferroelectric, bioceramics, transparent ceramics
- **Glass**: Silicate glasses; tempering; laminated; optical fibers

### Composites
- **Fiber-Reinforced**: Glass fiber (GFRP), carbon fiber (CFRP), aramid (Kevlar)
- **Matrix Types**: Polymer, metal, ceramic matrices
- **Rule of Mixtures**: Predicting composite properties from constituents
- **Manufacturing**: Layup, filament winding, pultrusion, resin transfer molding
- **Failure Modes**: Fiber breakage, matrix cracking, delamination, fiber-matrix debonding

### Electronic & Optical Materials
- **Semiconductors**: Silicon, germanium, GaAs; doping; p-n junctions
- **Superconductors**: Critical temperature; Type I and II; BCS theory
- **Optical Materials**: Refractive index; transparency; nonlinear optics
- **Photovoltaic Materials**: Silicon, thin-film (CdTe, CIGS), perovskite solar cells

### Nanomaterials
- **Nanoparticles**: Quantum dots; gold nanoparticles; catalytic properties
- **Carbon Nanomaterials**: Fullerenes, carbon nanotubes, graphene
- **Nanocomposites**: Enhanced mechanical, thermal, electrical properties
- **Surface Effects**: High surface-area-to-volume ratio; size-dependent properties

### Characterization Techniques
- **Microscopy**: Optical, SEM (scanning electron), TEM (transmission electron), AFM (atomic force)
- **Spectroscopy**: XRD (X-ray diffraction), EDS, XPS, Raman, FTIR
- **Mechanical Testing**: Tensile, compression, hardness, impact (Charpy), fatigue
- **Thermal Analysis**: DSC (differential scanning calorimetry), TGA (thermogravimetric)

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Phase Rule (Gibbs) | F = C - P + 2; degrees of freedom in phase diagrams |
| Hall-Petch Relationship | Yield strength increases with decreasing grain size |
| Griffith Theory | Fracture stress from crack length and surface energy |
| Band Theory | Electronic structure explains conductivity of metals, semiconductors, insulators |
| Boltzmann Distribution | Thermal activation of processes; diffusion |

## Important Figures

- **William Bragg & Lawrence Bragg**: X-ray crystallography; Bragg's law
- **Gibbs**: Phase rule; thermodynamics of materials
- **Alan Cottrell**: Dislocation theory; strengthening mechanisms
- **Richard Smalley / Robert Curl**: Fullerenes (C60)
- **Geim & Novoselov**: Graphene isolation (Nobel Prize 2010)

## Applications

- **Aerospace**: High-temperature alloys, composites, thermal protection
- **Electronics**: Semiconductor fabrication, thin films, packaging
- **Biomedical**: Implants, prosthetics, tissue engineering scaffolds
- **Energy**: Battery materials, fuel cells, photovoltaics, nuclear materials
- **Automotive**: Lightweight alloys, crash-resistant structures, coatings
- **Construction**: High-performance concrete, steel, timber engineering
