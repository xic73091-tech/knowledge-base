# Environmental Engineering

## Overview

Environmental engineering is the application of science and engineering principles to improve and protect the environment — air, water, and land — and to manage waste, remediate contaminated sites, and design sustainable systems. It integrates civil, chemical, and ecological engineering.

## Core Concepts

### Water & Wastewater Treatment
- **Drinking Water Treatment**: Coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, disinfection (chlorine, UV, ozone)
- **Wastewater Treatment**: Preliminary (screening), primary (settling), secondary (biological — activated sludge, trickling filters), tertiary (advanced)
- **Biological Processes**: Aerobic (activated sludge, MBR), anaerobic (digestion, UASB); BOD/COD removal
- **Membrane Technology**: Microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, reverse osmosis
- **Sludge Management**: Thickening, digestion, dewatering, disposal/reuse
- **Water Reuse**: Treated effluent for irrigation, industrial use, indirect potable reuse

### Air Pollution Control
- **Sources**: Stationary (factories, power plants) and mobile (vehicles)
- **Pollutants**: PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, SO₂, NOₓ, CO, O₃, VOCs, greenhouse gases
- **Control Technologies**: Scrubbers (wet/dry), baghouse filters, electrostatic precipitators, catalytic converters
- **Indoor Air Quality**: VOCs, radon, mold, ventilation standards
- **Air Quality Monitoring**: AQI; monitoring stations; satellite-based observations

### Solid Waste Management
- **Waste Hierarchy**: Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover → Dispose
- **Collection & Transfer**: Route optimization; transfer stations
- **Landfill Design**: Liner systems, leachate collection, gas collection, cover systems
- **Incineration**: Waste-to-energy; emissions control; ash management
- **Recycling**: Material recovery facilities; circular economy; extended producer responsibility
- **Hazardous Waste**: Classification; treatment (chemical, thermal, biological); RCRA/CERCLA regulations

### Soil & Groundwater Remediation
- **Contamination Sources**: Industrial spills, landfills, agricultural chemicals, mining
- **Remediation Technologies**:
  - Pump-and-treat for groundwater
  - Soil vapor extraction
  - Bioremediation (microbial degradation)
  - Chemical oxidation/reduction
  - Permeable reactive barriers
  - Phytoremediation (plants)
- **Risk Assessment**: Exposure pathways; toxicity; remediation goals

### Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)
- **Process**: Screening → scoping → baseline study → impact prediction → mitigation → monitoring
- **Methods**: Checklist, matrix, network, overlay (GIS-based)
- **Strategic Environmental Assessment**: Policy and plan-level assessment
- **Public Participation**: Stakeholder engagement; comment periods

### Sustainability & Green Engineering
- **Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)**: Cradle-to-grave environmental impact of products
- **Circular Economy**: Design for reuse, recycling, remanufacturing
- **Green Chemistry**: 12 principles; reducing hazardous substances in chemical processes
- **Sustainable Design**: LEED, BREEAM, Passive House; energy-efficient buildings
- **Carbon Footprint**: GHG accounting; Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions

### Climate Change Mitigation & Adaptation
- **Mitigation**: Renewable energy, energy efficiency, carbon capture and storage (CCS), reforestation
- **Adaptation**: Sea-level rise protection, heat resilience, drought-resistant infrastructure
- **Climate Modeling**: Global climate models (GCMs); regional downscaling
- **Policy Frameworks**: Paris Agreement; carbon pricing; emissions trading systems

### Environmental Regulations
- **Clean Air Act**: Ambient air quality standards; emission limits
- **Clean Water Act**: Discharge permits (NPDES); water quality standards
- **RCRA**: Hazardous waste management
- **CERCLA (Superfund)**: Contaminated site cleanup
- **Environmental Justice**: Equitable distribution of environmental burdens

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Mass Balance | Pollutant input - output = accumulation; basis of all environmental models |
| Michaelis-Menten Kinetics | Biological treatment rate modeling |
| Atmospheric Dispersion | Gaussian plume models for pollutant spreading |
| Darcy's Law | Groundwater flow through porous media |
| Carrying Capacity | Maximum population an environment can sustain |

## Frontiers

- **PFAS Remediation**: Developing cost-effective methods to destroy "forever chemicals" (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) in water and soil
- **Direct Air Capture at Scale**: Engineering sorbents and processes to remove CO2 from ambient air economically enough for gigaton-scale deployment
- **Microplastic Detection and Removal**: Building reliable monitoring systems and treatment technologies for nanoplastic and microplastic pollution
- **Nature-Based Solutions at Infrastructure Scale**: Quantifying and designing constructed wetlands, green roofs, and urban forests to replace gray infrastructure
- **Digital Environmental Monitoring**: Deploying IoT sensor networks and satellite analytics for real-time pollution tracking and early warning systems

## Applications

- **Municipal Services**: Water supply, wastewater treatment, waste collection
- **Industrial Compliance**: Emission controls, waste minimization, environmental auditing
- **Consulting**: EIA, remediation design, sustainability strategy
- **Government**: Environmental regulation, monitoring, enforcement
- **Research**: Climate science, pollution modeling, green technology development

## Classic Works

- **"Environmental Engineering: Fundamentals, Sustainability, Design"** by Mihelcic & Zimmerman — Modern textbook integrating sustainability principles with core treatment processes
- **"Water and Wastewater Engineering"** by Davis — Comprehensive reference for drinking water and wastewater treatment plant design
- **"Air Pollution Control: A Design Approach"** by Cooper & Alley — Detailed treatment of scrubbers, precipitators, and incinerator design
- **"Solid Waste Engineering"** by Worrell, Reckhow & Vesilind — Covers landfill design, waste-to-energy, recycling systems, and hazardous waste management
- **"Environmental Organic Chemistry"** by Schwarzenbach, Gschwend & Imboden — Foundational text on pollutant fate, transport, and transformation in natural systems
