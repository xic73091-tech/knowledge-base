# Computational Neuroscience

## Overview

Computational neuroscience is an interdisciplinary field that uses mathematical models, theoretical analysis, and computer simulations to understand how the nervous system processes information. It bridges neuroscience, mathematics, physics, computer science, and engineering to develop quantitative models of neural function at various levels—from ion channels and single neurons to neural circuits and entire brain regions. Computational neuroscience provides insights into neural coding, sensory processing, motor control, learning, memory, and cognitive functions, while also inspiring artificial intelligence and brain-inspired computing.

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Hodgkin-Huxley Model | Hodgkin & Huxley | Quantitative model of action potential generation based on ion channel conductances |
| Neural Coding Hypothesis | Various | Information is encoded in neural activity patterns (spikes, rates, timing) |
| Predictive Coding | Friston | Brain as hierarchical prediction machine minimizing prediction errors |
