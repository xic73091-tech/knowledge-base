# Earth Science

## Overview

Earth science (also known as geoscience) is the study of the Earth — its composition, structure, processes, and history. It encompasses geology, meteorology, oceanography, and environmental science, seeking to understand both the planet's past and predict its future behavior.

## Core Concepts

### Geology
- **Plate Tectonics**: Earth's lithosphere divided into plates; boundaries: divergent, convergent, transform
- **Rock Cycle**: Igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic; continuous transformation
- **Minerals**: Crystalline structures, hardness (Mohs scale), cleavage, luster
- **Volcanism**: Magma, lava types, volcanic landforms (shield, composite, cinder cone)
- **Earthquakes**: Seismic waves (P-waves, S-waves), Richter/moment magnitude scales, focus vs epicenter
- **Folds & Faults**: Structural geology; anticline, syncline, normal/reverse/strike-slip faults

### Stratigraphy & Geologic Time
- **Relative Dating**: Superposition, cross-cutting relationships, index fossils
- **Radiometric Dating**: Half-life of isotopes (C-14, K-40, U-238)
- **Geologic Time Scale**: Eons → Eras → Periods → Epochs
- **Key Events**: Formation of Earth (4.6 Ga), first life (3.8 Ga), Cambrian explosion, mass extinctions (5 major)

### Atmospheric Science
- **Atmosphere Composition**: N₂ (78%), O₂ (21%), Ar (0.9%), CO₂ (0.04%)
- **Layers**: Troposphere, stratosphere (ozone), mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere
- **Weather vs Climate**: Short-term atmospheric conditions vs long-term patterns
- **Greenhouse Effect**: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O trap heat; essential for life but excess causes warming
- **Climate Systems**: Hadley cells, jet streams, Coriolis effect, El Niño/La Niña

### Oceanography
- **Ocean Structure**: Surface currents (wind-driven), deep currents (thermohaline circulation)
- **Seafloor Features**: Mid-ocean ridges, trenches, continental shelf, abyssal plains
- **Tides**: Gravitational pull of Moon and Sun
- **Ocean Chemistry**: Salinity, pH, dissolved gases, carbon cycle

### Hydrology
- **Water Cycle**: Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff, infiltration, groundwater
- **Aquifers**: Confined vs unconfined; porosity, permeability
- **Erosion & Deposition**: Rivers, glaciers, wind shape landscapes

### Mineralogy & Petrology
- **Silicate Minerals**: Most abundant; quartz, feldspar, mica, olivine
- **Ore Deposits**: Metallic minerals; formation processes (magmatic, hydrothermal, sedimentary)
- **Gemstones**: Diamond, ruby, emerald, sapphire — geological formation and properties

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Plate Tectonics | Earth's surface is dynamic; plates move and interact |
| Uniformitarianism | Present processes explain past geological features (Lyell) |
| Continental Drift | Continents move; evidence from fossils and coastlines (Wegener) |
| Big Bang Cosmology | Origin of universe ~13.8 Ga; relevant to Earth's formation |

## Important Figures

- **Charles Lyell**: Uniformitarianism; Principles of Geology
- **Alfred Wegener**: Continental drift hypothesis
- **James Hutton**: Father of modern geology
- **Marie Tharp**: Mapped ocean floor; discovered mid-ocean rift
- **Charles Richter**: Earthquake magnitude scale
- **Milutin Milanković**: Orbital cycles and climate

## Applications

- **Resource Extraction**: Mining, petroleum geology, groundwater management
- **Natural Hazards**: Earthquake prediction, volcano monitoring, tsunami warning
- **Climate Science**: Understanding and mitigating climate change
- **Engineering**: Foundation geology, tunnel construction, dam sites
- **Archaeology**: Dating methods, paleoenvironment reconstruction
