# Physics

## Overview

Physics is the natural science that studies matter, its fundamental constituents, its motion and behavior through space and time, and the related entities of energy and force. It is one of the most fundamental scientific disciplines, with its main goal being to understand how the universe behaves.

## Core Concepts

### Classical Mechanics
- **Newton's Laws of Motion**: Inertia (F=ma), action-reaction, force equals mass times acceleration
- **Conservation Laws**: Energy, momentum, and angular momentum are conserved in isolated systems
- **Lagrangian & Hamiltonian Mechanics**: Reformulations using energy rather than force; foundation for modern physics
- **Oscillations & Waves**: Simple harmonic motion, wave equations, superposition, interference, diffraction

### Thermodynamics & Statistical Mechanics
- **Laws of Thermodynamics**: Zeroth (thermal equilibrium), First (energy conservation), Second (entropy increases), Third (absolute zero unattainable)
- **Entropy**: Measure of disorder; central to arrow of time
- **Heat Engines**: Carnot cycle, efficiency limits
- **Statistical Mechanics**: Microscopic explanation of macroscopic thermodynamic quantities; Boltzmann distribution

### Electromagnetism
- **Maxwell's Equations**: Unification of electricity, magnetism, and light
- **Electromagnetic Waves**: Light, radio, X-rays — all traveling at c
- **Coulomb's Law**: Force between charges
- **Electromagnetic Induction**: Faraday's law, basis for generators and transformers

### Quantum Mechanics
- **Wave-Particle Duality**: Light and matter exhibit both wave and particle properties
- **Schrödinger Equation**: Governs quantum state evolution
- **Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle**: Cannot simultaneously know exact position and momentum
- **Quantum Superposition**: Particles exist in multiple states until measured
- **Quantum Entanglement**: Correlated particles share states instantaneously
- **Quantum Field Theory**: Framework combining quantum mechanics with special relativity; basis of Standard Model

### Relativity
- **Special Relativity** (Einstein, 1905): Speed of light is constant; time dilation; E=mc²; mass-energy equivalence
- **General Relativity** (Einstein, 1915): Gravity as spacetime curvature; predicts black holes, gravitational waves, expansion of universe

### Particle Physics & The Standard Model
- **Quarks & Leptons**: Fundamental matter particles
- **Force Carriers**: Photon (EM), W/Z bosons (weak), gluons (strong), graviton (hypothetical)
- **Higgs Boson**: Gives particles mass; discovered 2012 at CERN
- **Four Fundamental Forces**: Gravity, electromagnetism, strong nuclear, weak nuclear

### Condensed Matter Physics
- **Crystal Structures**: Solid-state physics, band theory
- **Superconductivity**: Zero electrical resistance below critical temperature
- **Semiconductors**: Foundation of modern electronics
- **Topological Phases**: Quantum Hall effect, topological insulators

## Important Figures

- **Isaac Newton**: Laws of motion, universal gravitation, calculus
- **James Clerk Maxwell**: Electromagnetic theory
- **Albert Einstein**: Relativity, photoelectric effect
- **Niels Bohr**: Atomic model, Copenhagen interpretation
- **Werner Heisenberg**: Uncertainty principle, matrix mechanics
- **Erwin Schrödinger**: Wave equation, cat thought experiment
- **Richard Feynman**: QED, path integrals, Feynman diagrams
- **Marie Curie**: Radioactivity
- **Paul Dirac**: Relativistic quantum mechanics, predicted antimatter
- **Max Planck**: Quantum hypothesis

## Unresolved Problems

- **Quantum Gravity**: Reconciling general relativity with quantum mechanics
- **Dark Matter**: ~27% of universe; observed gravitationally but not directly detected
- **Dark Energy**: ~68% of universe; driving accelerated expansion
- **Matter-Antimatter Asymmetry**: Why universe has more matter than antimatter
- **Measurement Problem**: What constitutes a "measurement" in quantum mechanics

## Applications

- **Technology**: Semiconductors, lasers, nuclear energy, MRI, GPS (relativity corrections)
- **Engineering**: All branches depend on physics principles
- **Medicine**: Radiation therapy, medical imaging
- **Computing**: Quantum computing, transistor physics
