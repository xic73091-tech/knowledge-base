# Environmental Science

## Overview

Environmental science is an interdisciplinary academic field that integrates physical, biological, and information sciences to study the environment and find solutions to environmental problems. It encompasses ecology, chemistry, hydrology, geology, atmospheric science, and social sciences to understand natural systems, human impacts, and sustainable solutions. Environmental science addresses critical issues like climate change, pollution, resource depletion, biodiversity loss, and ecosystem degradation, seeking to balance human development with ecological preservation.

## Core Concepts

### Earth's Systems
- **Atmosphere**: Composition; layers (troposphere; stratosphere; mesosphere; thermosphere); greenhouse effect
- **Hydrosphere**: Water cycle; oceans; freshwater; glaciers; water distribution; availability
- **Lithosphere**: Earth's crust; rocks; minerals; soil formation; tectonic processes
- **Biosphere**: Living organisms; ecosystems; biogeochemical cycles; biodiversity
- **Cryosphere**: Ice sheets; sea ice; permafrost; glacial systems; climate feedback
- **Coupled Systems**: Interactions between spheres; climate system; Earth system science

### Ecology & Ecosystems
- **Ecosystem Components**: Biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) factors; energy flow
- **Food Webs & Chains**: Producers; consumers; decomposers; trophic levels; energy transfer efficiency
- **Biogeochemical Cycles**: Carbon; nitrogen; phosphorus; water; nutrient cycling
- **Ecological Succession**: Primary vs secondary succession; climax communities; disturbance
- **Ecosystem Services**: Provisioning; regulating; cultural; supporting services; valuation
- **Ecological Niche**: Fundamental vs realized niche; competition; resource partitioning
- **Population Ecology**: Population dynamics; carrying capacity; exponential vs logistic growth

### Biodiversity
- **Levels of Biodiversity**: Genetic; species; ecosystem diversity; alpha; beta; gamma diversity
- **Biodiversity Hotspots**: Regions with high endemism; conservation priorities
- **Threats to Biodiversity**: Habitat loss; climate change; invasive species; overexploitation; pollution
- **Extinction**: Background vs mass extinctions; extinction rates; sixth mass extinction
- **Conservation Biology**: Protecting species; habitat preservation; restoration ecology
- **Protected Areas**: National parks; reserves; marine protected areas; connectivity
- **Invasive Species**: Impacts; pathways; management; ecosystem disruption

### Climate Science
- **Climate vs Weather**: Distinguishing climate system from short-term weather
- **Climate System Components**: Atmosphere; hydrosphere; cryosphere; lithosphere; biosphere
- **Greenhouse Effect**: Natural vs enhanced greenhouse effect; greenhouse gases
- **Climate Feedbacks**: Positive and negative feedbacks; albedo; water vapor; clouds

- **Causes**: Fossil fuels; deforestation; agriculture; industrial processes; land use change

- **Mitigation**: Reducing emissions; energy transition; CCS; forests; technology

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Gaia Hypothesis | James Lovelock | Earth as self-regulating system maintaining habitable conditions |
| Tragedy of the Commons | Garrett Hardin | Shared resources face overexploitation without regulation |
| Ecological Footprint | Wackernagel & Rees | Measuring human demand on Earth's ecosystems |
| Planetary Boundaries | Stockholm Resilience Centre | Thresholds for safe human operation within Earth's system |
| Natural Capital | Various | Valuing ecosystem services and natural resources as capital |
| Intermediate Disturbance Hypothesis | Connell | Biodiversity maximized at intermediate levels of disturbance |
