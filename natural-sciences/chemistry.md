# Chemistry

## Overview

Chemistry is the scientific study of the properties and behavior of matter. It is a natural science that covers the elements that make up matter to the compounds composed of atoms, molecules and ions: their composition, structure, properties, behavior and the changes they undergo during a reaction with other substances.

## Core Concepts

### Atomic Structure
- **Atomic Model**: Nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electron clouds
- **Electron Configuration**: Aufbau principle, Hund's rule, Pauli exclusion principle
- **Periodic Table**: Organized by atomic number; periodic trends (electronegativity, ionization energy, atomic radius)
- **Isotopes**: Same element, different neutron counts

### Chemical Bonding
- **Ionic Bonds**: Electron transfer between metals and nonmetals
- **Covalent Bonds**: Electron sharing between nonmetals
- **Metallic Bonds**: Electron sea model in metals
- **Intermolecular Forces**: Van der Waals, hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole
- **Molecular Geometry**: VSEPR theory, hybridization (sp, sp2, sp3)

### Chemical Reactions
- **Stoichiometry**: Quantitative relationships in reactions
- **Reaction Types**: Synthesis, decomposition, single/double replacement, combustion, acid-base, redox
- **Chemical Equilibrium**: Le Chatelier's principle; equilibrium constants (K)
- **Reaction Rates**: Factors: concentration, temperature, catalysts, surface area
- **Activation Energy**: Energy barrier; Arrhenius equation

### Thermochemistry
- **Enthalpy (ΔH)**: Heat of reaction; exothermic vs endothermic
- **Hess's Law**: Total enthalpy change is path-independent
- **Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG)**: Predicts spontaneity; ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
- **Entropy (ΔS)**: Disorder measure

### Solutions & Mixtures
- **Concentration Units**: Molarity, molality, mass percent, ppm
- **Solubility Rules**: Like dissolves like; saturation
- **Colligative Properties**: Boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, osmotic pressure

## Branches

### Organic Chemistry
- **Carbon Chemistry**: Hydrocarbons, functional groups (alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, amines, esters)
- **Nomenclature**: IUPAC naming system
- **Isomers**: Structural, geometric (cis/trans), optical (enantiomers)
- **Reaction Mechanisms**: SN1, SN2, E1, E2, electrophilic addition, nucleophilic substitution
- **Polymers**: Plastics, proteins, DNA
- **Biochemistry Interface**: Amino acids, lipids, carbohydrates, nucleotides

### Inorganic Chemistry
- **Coordination Chemistry**: Ligands, crystal field theory, transition metal complexes
- **Acid-Base Chemistry**: Brønsted-Lowry, Lewis definitions; pH scale
- **Electrochemistry**: Galvanic cells, electrolysis, Nernst equation, corrosion
- **Solid-State Chemistry**: Crystal structures, ceramics

### Analytical Chemistry
- **Spectroscopy**: UV-Vis, IR, NMR, mass spectrometry
- **Chromatography**: HPLC, GC, thin-layer
- **Titration**: Acid-base, redox, complexometric

### Physical Chemistry
- **Quantum Chemistry**: Molecular orbital theory, computational chemistry
- **Kinetics**: Rate laws, reaction mechanisms
- **Thermodynamics**: Statistical mechanics applied to chemical systems

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Atomic Theory | Matter composed of atoms (Dalton → quantum model) |
| VSEPR | Molecular shape from electron pair repulsion |
| Molecular Orbital Theory | Electrons in molecules occupy delocalized orbitals |
| Transition State Theory | Reaction rates from activated complex formation |
| Collision Theory | Reactions require sufficient energy and proper orientation |

## Important Figures

- **Antoine Lavoisier**: Father of modern chemistry; conservation of mass
- **Dmitri Mendeleev**: Periodic table
- **Marie Curie**: Radioactivity
- **Linus Pauling**: Chemical bonding, electronegativity
- **Gilbert N. Lewis**: Lewis structures, acid-base theory
- **Friedrich Wöhler**: Synthesized urea; bridged organic/inorganic

## Frontiers

- **Ab Initio Reaction Prediction**: Using machine learning and quantum chemistry to predict reaction outcomes without empirical data
- **Artificial Enzyme Design**: Engineering enzymes for reactions not found in nature; bridging catalysis and synthetic biology
- **Plastic Degradation**: Developing chemical and biological methods to break down persistent polymers sustainably
- **Solid-State Battery Electrolytes**: Finding safe, high-conductivity solid electrolytes to replace flammable liquid ones in batteries
- **Origin of Life Chemistry**: Understanding how prebiotic molecules formed and self-assembled into the first living systems

## Applications

- **Pharmaceuticals**: Drug design and synthesis
- **Materials Science**: Polymers, nanomaterials, alloys
- **Energy**: Batteries, fuel cells, solar cells, petroleum refining
- **Agriculture**: Fertilizers, pesticides
- **Environmental**: Water treatment, pollution analysis, climate chemistry

## Classic Works

- **"Atkins' Physical Chemistry"** by Peter Atkins & Julio de Paula — The standard physical chemistry text; clear treatment of thermodynamics, kinetics, and quantum chemistry
- **"Organic Chemistry"** by Jonathan Clayden et al. — Widely regarded as the best modern organic chemistry textbook; mechanism-driven and well-organized
- **"Advanced Organic Chemistry"** by Francis Carey & Richard Sundberg — Two-volume graduate text covering structure, mechanism, and synthesis in depth
- **"Chemical Principles"** by Peter Atkins & Loretta Jones — Accessible introduction to fundamental chemistry concepts with rigorous foundations
- **"The Nature of the Chemical Bond"** by Linus Pauling — Landmark work on chemical bonding theory; introduced electronegativity scales and hybridization concepts
