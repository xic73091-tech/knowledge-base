# Astronomy

## Overview

Astronomy is the natural science that studies celestial objects and phenomena. It applies mathematics, physics, and chemistry to explain the origin and evolution of stars, planets, galaxies, nebulae, and the universe as a whole. It is one of the oldest sciences.

## Core Concepts

### Solar System
- **Sun**: G-type main-sequence star; fusion of H→He; 4.6 billion years old
- **Inner Planets** (terrestrial): Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — rocky, smaller
- **Outer Planets** (gas/ice giants): Jupiter, Saturn (gas); Uranus, Neptune (ice)
- **Dwarf Planets**: Pluto, Eris, Ceres, Haumea, Makemake
- **Small Bodies**: Asteroids (main belt, near-Earth), comets (Oort cloud, Kuiper belt), meteoroids

### Stars
- **Stellar Classification**: O, B, A, F, G, K, M (hot→cool); spectral types
- **Hertzsprung-Russell Diagram**: Luminosity vs temperature; main sequence, giants, white dwarfs
- **Stellar Evolution**: Nebula → protostar → main sequence → red giant/supergiant → white dwarf/neutron star/black hole
- **Nuclear Fusion**: pp chain (Sun), CNO cycle (massive stars)
- **Supernovae**: Type Ia (white dwarf), Type II (core collapse); seed heavy elements
- **Neutron Stars & Pulsars**: Ultra-dense remnants; pulsars emit radio beams
- **Black Holes**: Event horizon, singularity; Schwarzschild radius; Hawking radiation

### Galaxies
- **Milky Way**: Barred spiral; ~200-400 billion stars; supermassive black hole (Sagittarius A*)
- **Galaxy Types**: Spiral, elliptical, irregular, lenticular
- **Galaxy Clusters & Superclusters**: Large-scale structure; cosmic web
- **Dark Matter**: ~27% of universe; inferred from galaxy rotation curves, gravitational lensing
- **Active Galactic Nuclei**: Quasars, blazars — powered by accreting supermassive black holes

### Cosmology
- **Big Bang Theory**: Universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from a singularity
- **Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)**: Relic radiation from ~380,000 years after Big Bang
- **Expansion of Universe**: Hubble's law; galaxies receding; accelerating expansion (dark energy)
- **Dark Energy**: ~68% of universe; driving accelerated expansion
- **Cosmic Inflation**: Rapid expansion in first fraction of a second
- **Observable Universe**: ~93 billion light-years diameter; limit is speed of light × age

### Observational Methods
- **Telescopes**: Optical (visible light), radio, X-ray, infrared, gamma-ray
- **Spectroscopy**: Determine composition, temperature, velocity of celestial objects
- **Astrometry**: Precise measurement of star positions
- **Gravitational Wave Detection**: LIGO/Virgo; ripples in spacetime from massive events

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Big Bang | Universe originated from hot, dense state ~13.8 Ga |
| General Relativity | Gravity as spacetime curvature; predicts black holes |
| Stellar Nucleosynthesis | Stars forge heavy elements from lighter ones |
| Dark Matter/Energy | Inferred from gravitational effects and cosmic acceleration |

## Important Figures

- **Galileo Galilei**: Telescopic observations; moons of Jupiter
- **Johannes Kepler**: Laws of planetary motion
- **Edwin Hubble**: Expanding universe; galaxy classification
- **Carl Sagan**: Popularization; Voyager golden record
- **Stephen Hawking**: Black hole thermodynamics, Hawking radiation
- **Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar**: Stellar evolution limits (Chandrasekhar limit)
- **Vera Rubin**: Evidence for dark matter from galaxy rotation

## Applications

- **Navigation**: Celestial navigation, GPS (relativity corrections)
- **Space Exploration**: Robotic missions, human spaceflight, Mars colonization
- **Technology Spinoffs**: CCD sensors, memory foam, water purification
- **Astrobiology**: Search for extraterrestrial life; habitable zones
- **Satellite Systems**: Communication, weather, Earth observation
