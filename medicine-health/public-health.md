# Public Health

## Overview

Public health is the science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life, and promoting health through organized efforts and informed choices of society, organizations, public and private communities, and individuals. It focuses on population-level interventions rather than individual patient care.

## Core Concepts

### Epidemiology
- **Study Designs**: Cross-sectional, case-control, cohort, randomized controlled trials (RCTs)
- **Measures of Disease Frequency**: Incidence (new cases), prevalence (existing cases), mortality rate
- **Measures of Association**: Relative risk, odds ratio, attributable risk, hazard ratio
- **Confounding & Bias**: Selection bias, information bias, confounding variables; methods to control
- **Outbreak Investigation**: Steps: confirm diagnosis, define cases, describe by person/place/time, develop hypotheses, test, implement control
- **Surveillance**: Disease monitoring systems; syndromic surveillance; sentinel surveillance

### Disease Prevention
- **Primary Prevention**: Preventing disease before it occurs — vaccination, health education, sanitation, fluoridation
- **Secondary Prevention**: Early detection — screening programs (mammography, colonoscopy, blood pressure)
- **Tertiary Prevention**: Reducing impact of existing disease — rehabilitation, chronic disease management
- **Immunization Programs**: Vaccine schedules; herd immunity; eradication success (smallpox); ongoing efforts (polio)

### Health Promotion
- **Health Education**: Teaching healthy behaviors; community programs
- **Behavioral Change Models**: Health belief model, theory of planned behavior, transtheoretical model
- **Social Determinants of Health**: Income, education, housing, food security, social support, racism
- **Health Equity**: Reducing disparities; addressing structural inequalities

### Environmental Health
- **Air Quality**: Ambient and indoor air pollution; health effects (respiratory, cardiovascular)
- **Water Safety**: Waterborne diseases; sanitation; access to clean water
- **Food Safety**: Foodborne illnesses; HACCP; pasteurization; inspection
- **Occupational Health**: Workplace hazards; ergonomics; toxic exposures; OSHA regulations
- **Climate & Health**: Heat waves, vector-borne diseases, extreme weather, mental health impacts

### Health Policy & Systems
- **Health Systems**: Single-payer, multi-payer, universal coverage; WHO building blocks
- **Health Economics**: Cost-effectiveness analysis; quality-adjusted life years (QALYs); budget impact
- **Policy Process**: Agenda setting, policy formulation, implementation, evaluation
- **Global Health Governance**: WHO; International Health Regulations; pandemic preparedness

### Maternal & Child Health
- **Prenatal Care**: Folic acid, screening, nutrition, avoiding teratogens
- **Childbirth**: Safe delivery; skilled birth attendants; maternal mortality reduction
- **Child Health**: Growth monitoring, immunization, nutrition (breastfeeding), injury prevention
- **Adolescent Health**: Mental health, substance use, sexual health, injury prevention

### Global Health
- **Millennium/Sustainable Development Goals**: Health-related targets; universal health coverage
- **Neglected Tropical Diseases**: Dengue, Chagas, sleeping sickness; low research investment
- **Pandemic Preparedness**: Surveillance, stockpiling, rapid response, international coordination
- **Health Workforce**: Doctor/nurse shortages; task shifting; community health workers

### Mental Health in Public Health
- **Burden of Mental Illness**: Depression, anxiety, substance use disorders; DALYs
- **Suicide Prevention**: Risk factors; hotline programs; means restriction
- **Substance Abuse**: Tobacco, alcohol, opioids; harm reduction; treatment access
- **Stigma Reduction**: Anti-stigma campaigns; mental health literacy

### Biostatistics
- **Statistical Methods**: t-tests, chi-square, ANOVA, regression, survival analysis
- **Study Sample Size**: Power analysis; minimum detectable effect
- **Meta-Analysis**: Combining results from multiple studies; forest plots; heterogeneity
- **Causal Inference**: Directed acyclic graphs (DAGs); instrumental variables; natural experiments

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Epidemiologic Triad | Agent, host, environment interaction in disease |
| Social Determinants | Health shaped by social, economic, political conditions |
| Diffusion of Innovations | How new health behaviors spread (Rogers) |
| Risk Factor Epidemiology | Identifying modifiable risk factors for disease |

## Important Figures

- **John Snow**: Father of epidemiology; cholera investigation
- **Florence Nightingale**: Nursing reform; statistical analysis of mortality
- **Edward Jenner**: Smallpox vaccine
- **Rudolf Virchow**: Social medicine; "medicine is a social science"
- **Margaret Chan**: WHO Director-General; pandemic response
- **Paul Farmer**: Partners in Health; global health equity

## Applications

- **Disease Control**: Outbreak investigation, contact tracing, quarantine
- **Vaccination Programs**: Routine immunization, campaign vaccination
- **Health Education**: Anti-smoking, safe sex, nutrition, exercise campaigns
- **Environmental Regulation**: Clean air/water standards, toxic substance control
- **Health Policy**: Insurance reform, tobacco taxation, sugar tax, mask mandates
- **International Organizations**: WHO, UNICEF, CDC, Médecins Sans Frontières
