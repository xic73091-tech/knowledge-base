# Theater & Performing Arts

## Overview

Theater is the art of live performance — the collaborative creation of meaning through acting, directing, design, and text before a live audience. It is one of humanity's oldest art forms, from Greek tragedy to contemporary experimental performance. Performing arts also encompass dance, opera, musical theater, and performance art, all sharing the fundamental experience of presence and co-creation between performer and audience.

## Core Concepts

### Acting
- **Stanislavski System**: Emotional memory, given circumstances, objectives, super-objective; realistic acting foundation
- **Method Acting** (Strasberg): Affective memory, sense memory, "living the part"; associated with Brando, De Niro
- **Meisner Technique**: Repetition exercises, emotional truth, "living truthfully under imaginary circumstances"
- **Viewpoints** (Bogart & Landau): Composition and ensemble work through spatial relationships, tempo, gesture
- **Physical Theater**: Lecoq, Grotowski, Suzuki; body as primary expressive instrument
- **Improvisation**: Spolin, iO Theater, UCB; "yes, and..." principle; long-form, short-form, improvised musicals

### Directing
- **Director's Vision**: Conceptual interpretation of the text; unifying artistic choices
- **Blocking**: Staging actors' movements; composition on stage; picturization
- **Rehearsal Process**: Table work, blocking, run-throughs, tech rehearsals, dress rehearsals
- **Collaboration**: Working with designers, actors, stage management; director as conductor
- **Auteur Directors**: Peter Brook, Robert Wilson, Ariane Mnouchkine, Katie Mitchell; signature styles

### Playwriting & Dramaturgy
- **Dramatic Structure**: Exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution; Freytag's pyramid
- **Modern Structure**: Non-linear, episodic, fragmented; Brecht's epic theater; Caryl Churchill
- **Dialogue**: Subtext, rhythm, voice; how characters reveal themselves through speech
- **Dramaturgy**: Research, script analysis, contextual framing; new play development
- **Adaptation**: Reinterpreting classic texts; modernizing Shakespeare; cross-cultural adaptation

### Design
- **Set Design**: Scenography; creating physical environment; minimalist to immersive
- **Costume Design**: Character, period, concept; costume as storytelling
- **Lighting Design**: Mood, focus, time; LED, moving lights, projections; McCandless method
- **Sound Design**: Music, effects, atmosphere; reinforcement; spatial audio
- **Projection Design**: Video, mapping, multimedia integration; digital scenography

### Theater History & Traditions
- **Greek Theater**: Tragedy (Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides), comedy (Aristophanes); amphitheater; chorus
- **Shakespeare**: Globe Theatre; soliloquy; five-act structure; iambic pentameter
- **Modern Drama**: Ibsen (naturalism), Chekhov (subtext), Brecht (epic theater), Beckett (absurdism)
- **Non-Western Traditions**: Noh, Kabuki, Beijing Opera, Kathakali, commedia dell'arte
- **Contemporary**: Devised theater, immersive theater (Sleep No More), verbatim theater, site-specific

### Musical Theater
- **Golden Age**: Rodgers & Hammerstein; integrated musical; book, score, lyrics as unified whole
- **Concept Musical**: A Chorus Line; Company; form follows theme
- **Megamusical**: Les Misérables, Phantom; spectacle, through-composed, international franchising
- **Contemporary**: Hamilton, Dear Evan Hansen; hip-hop, pop influences; diverse casting
- **Collaboration**: Composer, lyricist, book writer, choreographer, director; the "workshop" process

### Dance & Movement
- **Ballet**: Classical (Petipa, Balanchine), neoclassical, contemporary; technique and artistry
- **Modern Dance**: Graham, Cunningham, Forsythe; rejecting ballet conventions; floor work, release
- **Contemporary**: Fusion of styles; contact improvisation; site-specific; interdisciplinary
- **World Dance**: Bharatanatyam, flamenco, butoh, West African, tango; cultural context and evolution
- **Choreography**: Compositional principles; motif, variation, structure; improvisation as method

### Business & Institutional Theater
- **Broadway & West End**: Commercial theater; producing, investing, marketing; Tony Awards
- **Regional & Nonprofit**: LORT theaters; subscription models; mission-driven programming
- **Fringe & Off-Off-Broadway**: Experimental, low-budget, artist-driven; Edinburgh Fringe
- **Funding**: Box office, subscriptions, donors, grants (NEA), corporate sponsorship
- **Touring**: National tours, international exchanges; logistics and adaptation

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Poetics | Aristotle | Tragedy as imitation of action; catharsis through pity and fear |
| Epic Theater | Bertolt Brecht | Alienation effect; audience should think, not just feel |
| Poor Theater | Jerzy Grotowski | Stripping theater to actor-audience relationship; removing spectacle |
| The Empty Space | Peter Brook | Theatre exists whenever someone walks across empty space watched by another |

## Important Figures

- **Aristotle**: Poetics; foundational dramatic theory
- **William Shakespeare**: Greatest playwright in English; 37+ plays
- **Konstantin Stanislavski**: System for realistic acting; Moscow Art Theatre
- **Bertolt Brecht**: Epic theater; alienation effect; political theater
- **August Wilson**: American Century Cycle; Black American experience on stage
- **Lin-Manuel Miranda**: Hamilton; revolutionizing musical theater with hip-hop and diverse casting
- **Pina Bausch**: Tanztheater; fusion of dance and theater; emotional intensity

## Frontiers

- **AI in Theater**: AI-generated scripts, virtual set design, motion capture for digital performance — what is lost without human authorship?
- **Immersive & Interactive Performance**: Audience agency, site-specific work, VR theater; redefining spectatorship
- **Accessibility**: Audio description, relaxed performances, deaf-led theater, neurodiverse-friendly practices
- **Decolonizing the Stage**: Diversifying canons, non-Western forms, indigenous storytelling; who gets to tell which stories?
- **Digital & Hybrid Performance**: Streaming, virtual theater, hybrid models post-COVID; economics and aesthetics of digital live art

## Applications

- **Entertainment**: Broadway, regional theater, touring productions, festivals
- **Education**: Drama in schools, theater for young audiences, applied theater
- **Therapy**: Drama therapy, psychodrama, theater in prisons and hospitals
- **Community**: Community theater, participatory art, Forum Theater (Boal)
- **Corporate**: Corporate training, role-playing, communication skills
- **Social Change**: Documentary theater, protest performance, Theater of the Oppressed

## Classic Works

- **"The Poetics"** by Aristotle — The foundational text of Western dramatic theory; analysis of tragedy
- **"An Actor Prepares"** by Constantin Stanislavski — The actor's bible; system for truthful, realistic performance
- **"The Empty Space"** by Peter Brook — Passionate argument for theater's essential purpose; deadly, holy, rough, immediate
- **"Theater of the Oppressed"** by Augusto Boal — Using theater as a tool for political and social transformation
- **"Hamilton: The Revolution"** by Lin-Manuel Miranda & Jeremy McCarter — Behind the scenes of the musical that changed Broadway
