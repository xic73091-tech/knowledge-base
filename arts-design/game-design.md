# Game Design

## Overview

Game design is the practice of creating interactive experiences — digital and analog — through the deliberate crafting of rules, systems, narratives, and aesthetics. It draws on psychology, art, engineering, storytelling, and mathematics to create experiences that engage, challenge, and transform players. From board games to open-world video games, game design is fundamentally about shaping meaningful choices and their consequences.

## Core Concepts

### Core Mechanics & Systems
- **Game Mechanics**: Rules and procedures that govern player interaction; actions, resources, constraints
- **Systems Design**: Interconnected mechanics creating emergent behavior; economy, progression, combat
- **Feedback Loops**: Positive (runaway leader) and negative (catch-up) loops; balancing dynamics
- **Randomness vs Determinism**: Dice, card draws, procedural generation; when luck enhances or frustrates
- **Economy Design**: Resources, currencies, sinks and sources; inflation control; crafting systems

### Player Experience
- **Flow** (Csikszentmihalyi): Optimal experience; balance between challenge and skill
- **Intrinsic Motivation**: Autonomy, competence, relatedness (Self-Determination Theory)
- **Player Types**: Achievers, explorers, socializers, killers (Bartle's taxonomy); more nuanced models
- **Onboarding**: Teaching through play; tutorials; progressive complexity; avoiding information overload
- **Difficulty Design**: Dynamic difficulty; adaptive AI; accessibility options; "Souls-like" challenge philosophy

### Level & World Design
- **Spatial Design**: Layout, flow, landmarks, sightlines; guiding player movement
- **Environmental Storytelling**: Narrative embedded in environment; objects, architecture, details
- **Procedural Generation**: Algorithmically created content; roguelikes, Minecraft, No Man's Sky
- **Open World**: Freedom of exploration; points of interest; emergent gameplay; pacing challenges
- **Dungeon Design**: Encounter pacing, resource management, puzzle integration, reward placement

### Narrative Design
- **Linear vs Branching**: Authorial control vs player agency; Telltale, BioWare, Disco Elysium
- **Environmental Storytelling**: Telling stories through world design rather than cutscenes
- **Emergent Narrative**: Stories arising from player interaction with systems; Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld
- **Dialogue Systems**: Dialogue trees, reputation systems, consequences that ripple across the game
- **Lore & Worldbuilding**: Internal consistency; depth without overwhelming; discoverable history

### Game Feel & Juice
- **Game Feel** (Swink): The tactile sensation of controlling an avatar; responsiveness, weight, impact
- **Juice**: Satisfying feedback — screen shake, particles, sound effects, animation exaggeration
- **Animation Principles**: Anticipation, follow-through, squash and stretch; applying Disney to games
- **Camera Design**: Fixed, follow, first-person, isometric; how camera shapes experience
- **Input Design**: Mapping actions to controls; accessibility; controller vs keyboard vs touch

### Balancing & Iteration
- **Playtesting**: Observing real players; identifying frustration, confusion, exploits; iterative design
- **Data-Driven Design**: Analytics, A/B testing, telemetry; balancing based on player behavior
- **Spreadsheet Balancing**: Damage formulas, XP curves, probability tables; mathematical modeling
- **Metagame**: The game outside the game; tier lists, community strategies, competitive balance
- **Live Service**: Ongoing updates, seasons, events; maintaining engagement over time

### Game Design Documents
- **GDD**: Game Design Document; comprehensive blueprint; audience, mechanics, story, art, technical
- **One-Page Pitch**: Concise summary of the core experience; hook, unique selling proposition
- **Prototyping**: Paper prototypes, grey-box levels, vertical slices; testing ideas cheaply and fast
- **Scoping**: Managing feature creep; minimum viable product; what to cut

### Board & Tabletop Design
- **Eurogames**: Strategic depth, low luck, indirect interaction (Catan, Agricola, Wingspan)
- **Ameritrash**: Theme-driven, direct conflict, high randomness (Risk, Betrayal)
- **Cooperative Games**: Pandemic, Gloomhaven; shared win/loss conditions
- **Legacy Games**: Permanent changes to the board and rules; narrative progression
- **Tabletop RPGs**: Dungeon World, Blades in the Dark; player-driven narrative; GM as designer

### Game Genres in Depth
- **Action Games**: First-person shooters (FPS), third-person shooters, fighting, platformers
- **Role-Playing Games (RPGs)**: JRPGs, Western RPGs, action RPGs, tactical RPGs
- **Strategy Games**: Real-time strategy (RTS), turn-based strategy (TBS), 4X, tower defense
- **Simulation Games**: Life sims (The Sims), city builders, vehicle simulators, farming sims
- **Puzzle Games**: Match-3, physics, logic, narrative puzzles
- **Adventure Games**: Point-and-click, walking simulators, escape rooms
- **Sports & Racing**: Realistic sims, arcade sports, racing simulators
- **Horror Games**: Survival horror, psychological horror, action horror
- **Rhythm & Music Games**: Pattern-matching, music creation, dance games
- **Battle Royale**: 100-player survival, shrinking play area, last-one-standing
- **Roguelikes & Roguelites**: Procedural generation, permadeath, run-based progression
- **MMOs & Online Games**: Persistent worlds, social play, economies
- **Sandbox Games**: Open creativity, modding, emergent play

### Game Systems & Mechanics in Depth
- **Combat Systems**: Real-time, turn-based, tactical, action; hit boxes, damage formulas
- **Progression Systems**: Leveling, skill trees, ability unlocks, talent points, prestige
- **Loot Systems**: Random loot tables, drop rates, rarity tiers, gambling mechanics
- **Crafting Systems**: Recipes, materials, complexity curves, player agency
- **Inventory Management**: Grid-based, weight-based, tetris-style, auto-sort
- **Quest Systems**: Main quests, side quests, dynamic quests, procedural quests
- **Dialogue Systems**: Branching dialogue, reputation systems, morality meters
- **Economy Design**: Currencies, sinks/sources, inflation control, market dynamics
- **Permadeath & Checkpoints**: Save systems, manual/auto-save, iron man modes
- **Difficulty Modes**: Easy/Normal/Hard, dynamic difficulty, accessibility options

### Game Design Process in Depth
- **Concept Development**: High concept, pitch documents, design pillars, target audience
- **Prototyping**: Paper, graybox, digital prototypes; throwaway vs. iterative prototypes
- **Iteration & Playtesting**: Internal playtests, external playtests, focus tests, blind playtests
- **Vertical Slice**: Polished segment showcasing the full experience
- **Pre-Production**: Concept validation, technical proof-of-concept, art direction
- **Production**: Full development with milestones, sprint planning, scope management
- **Alpha**: Feature-complete but content-incomplete; internal testing
- **Beta**: Feature and content complete; external testing; balancing
- **Gold Master**: Final version ready for release; certification (console)
- **Post-Launch**: Patches, DLC, live operations, sequels, community management

### Narrative Design in Depth
- **Three-Act Structure**: Setup, confrontation, resolution; cinematic storytelling
- **Hero's Journey**: Monomyth; common narrative template
- **Interactive Storytelling**: Agency, branching, consequence, reactivity
- **Lore Design**: Worldbuilding, history, mythologies, hidden lore
- **Quest Design**: Macro design (story arcs), micro design (individual quests)
- **Character Development**: Backstory, motivations, arcs, character growth
- **Environmental Storytelling**: Visual cues, environmental details, diegetic storytelling
- **Show, Don't Tell**: Revealing through action, environment, observation
- **Ludo-Narrative Dissonance**: Conflict between mechanics and story (Clint Hocking)
- **Choice Architecture**: Meaningful vs. cosmetic choices; false choices

### Player Psychology & Motivation
- **Self-Determination Theory**: Autonomy, competence, relatedness drive intrinsic motivation
- **Bartle's Player Types**: Achievers, explorers, socializers, killers; expanded taxonomies
- **Quantic Foundry's Gamer Motivation Model**: 12 motivations across 6 clusters
- **Loss Aversion**: Players feel losses more than equivalent gains
- **Variable Reward Schedules**: Skinner box mechanics, dopamine-driven engagement
- **Endowed Progress Effect**: Initial progress boosts completion; loyalty programs
- **Zeigarnik Effect**: Uncompleted tasks are remembered better; cliffhangers, open loops
- **Cognitive Flow**: Challenge-skill balance, clear goals, immediate feedback
- **Engagement Loops**: Short, medium, long-term loops; core loop design
- **Player Retention**: Daily rewards, FOMO mechanics, social bonds

### Game Audio
- **Music Composition**: Adaptive music, dynamic scores, leitmotifs, atmospheric music
- **Sound Design**: Foley, ambient sounds, UI sounds, weapon/character sounds
- **Voice Acting**: Casting, direction, recording, post-processing
- **Adaptive Audio**: Music that responds to gameplay state; vertical/horizontal re-orchestration
- **3D Audio**: Spatial sound, binaural audio, HRTF for immersive experiences
- **Audio Implementation**: Middleware (Wwise, FMOD), audio engines
- **Dynamic Mixing**: Real-time mix adjustments based on gameplay

### Game Art & Visual Design
- **Art Direction**: Defining visual style, mood boards, reference gathering
- **Character Design**: Silhouettes, proportions, costume, expression, appeal
- **Environment Art**: Set dressing, lighting, materials, atmosphere
- **Concept Art**: Ideation, exploration, visual problem-solving
- **UI/UX Design**: HUD design, menus, feedback, accessibility
- **3D Modeling**: Hard surface, organic, sculpting, retopology
- **Texturing**: PBR materials, hand-painted, procedural
- **Animation**: Keyframe, motion capture, facial animation, locomotion
- **Lighting**: Real-time lighting, lightmaps, baked lighting, ambient occlusion
- **VFX & Particles**: Explosions, magic, weather, environmental effects
- **Rendering Techniques**: Forward, deferred, ray tracing, rasterization
- **Shaders**: Vertex, fragment, geometry; stylized and realistic rendering

### Technical Game Development
- **Game Engines**: Unity, Unreal Engine, Godot; engine choice considerations
- **Programming Languages**: C++, C#, Rust, GDScript, Lua
- **Multiplayer Architecture**: Client-server, peer-to-peer, authoritative server, lag compensation
- **Networking**: TCP, UDP, WebSockets; matchmaking, replication
- **Physics**: Collision detection, rigid bodies, soft bodies, ragdolls
- **AI in Games**: Pathfinding (A*, navmesh), behavior trees, state machines, utility AI
- **Procedural Content Generation**: Perlin noise, L-systems, wave function collapse
- **Optimization**: LOD, occlusion culling, batching, draw calls, memory management
- **Save Systems**: Serialization, versioning, cloud saves
- **Tools Development**: In-house tools, editor extensions, debugging tools

### Game Production & Business
- **Project Management**: Agile, Scrum, Kanban for games; milestones; GDD tracking
- **Team Roles**: Designer, programmer, artist, animator, audio, QA, producer, writer
- **Studio Structure**: Indie, AA, AAA, mobile, services
- **Game Marketing**: Trailers, social media, influencer marketing, community building
- **Monetization Models**: Premium, free-to-play, subscription, season pass, cosmetic microtransactions
- **Game Analytics**: Player behavior tracking, A/B testing, retention metrics, LTV
- **Live Operations**: Seasonal content, events, balance patches, anti-cheat
- **Platforms**: PC (Steam, Epic), console (PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo), mobile (iOS, Android), cloud
- **Publishing**: Self-publishing, traditional publishing, platform exclusives

### Serious Games & Applied Game Design
- **Educational Games**: Learning objectives, assessment, engagement balance
- **Training Simulations**: Aviation, medical, military, corporate; fidelity considerations
- **Health Games (Games for Health)**: Exergames, cognitive training, rehabilitation, mental health
- **Games for Change**: Social impact, empathy, awareness, behavior change
- **Gamification**: Applying game mechanics to non-game contexts; Octalysis framework
- **Persuasive Games**: Behavior change, motivation, design ethics
- **Advergames & Branded Games**: Marketing through play
- **Newsgames**: Journalism through interactive media

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Flow Theory | Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi | Optimal experience when challenge matches skill |
| MDA Framework | Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek | Mechanics → Dynamics → Aesthetics; designer vs player perspective |
| Self-Determination Theory | Deci & Ryan | Autonomy, competence, relatedness drive intrinsic motivation |
| Bartle's Taxonomy | Richard Bartle | Player types: achievers, explorers, socializers, killers |
| Elemental Tetrad | Jesse Schell | Story, Mechanics, Aesthetics, Technology as four elements of game design |
| Ludo-Narrative Dissonance | Clint Hocking | Conflict between game mechanics and narrative; design problem |
| Octalysis Framework | Yu-kai Chou | 8 core drives for human motivation in gamification |
| PENS Model | Rigby & Ryan | Player Experience of Need Satisfaction; relatedness, competence, autonomy |
| Skinner Box / Variable Rewards | B.F. Skinner | Intermittent reinforcement drives engagement (slot machines) |
| Theory of Fun | Raph Koster | Fun comes from learning, mastery, and pattern recognition |

## Classic Works

- **"A Theory of Fun for Game Design"** by Raph Koster — Accessible exploration of why games are fun; connecting games to learning theory
- **"The Art of Game Design"** by Jesse Schell — Comprehensive textbook; 100+ lenses for examining game design from multiple perspectives
- **"Game Feel"** by Steve Swick — Deep dive into the tactile sensation of playing games; what makes controls feel good
- **"Rules of Play"** by Salen & Zimmerman — Academic foundation for game design; systems, play, and culture
- **"Designing Games"** by Sylvester Tynan — Practical guide to game design as an iterative, player-centered process
