# Architecture

## Overview

Architecture is both the process and product of planning, designing, and constructing buildings and other physical structures. It bridges art and engineering, combining aesthetic vision with structural integrity, functional requirements, and environmental responsibility. Architectural works are often perceived as cultural symbols and works of art.

## Core Concepts

### Design Principles
- **Form & Function**: Sullivan's "form follows function"; beauty and utility
- **Space**: Positive and negative space; spatial sequences; compression and release
- **Light**: Natural light; artificial lighting; light as material; shadow
- **Materiality**: Stone, brick, glass, steel, concrete, wood, timber; material expression
- **Proportion & Scale**: Golden ratio; human scale; monumental scale; modularity
- **Rhythm & Repetition**: Patterns, grids, facades; visual rhythm
- **Context**: Site, climate, culture, history; contextual vs autonomous design

### Building Systems
- **Structural Systems**: Load-bearing walls, frames, trusses, shells, tensile structures, geodesic domes
- **Foundation Systems**: Shallow (spread footings, slab-on-grade) and deep (piles, caissons)
- **Enclosure Systems**: Curtain walls, facades, cladding, roofing; thermal envelope
- **MEP Systems**: Mechanical (HVAC), electrical (power, lighting), plumbing (water, drainage)
- **Fire Protection**: Fire-rated assemblies, sprinklers, egress design, smoke control
- **Sustainability Systems**: Passive design, solar panels, green roofs, rainwater harvesting, greywater recycling

### Architectural Styles (Historical)
- **Ancient**: Egyptian (pyramids), Greek (temples, orders), Roman (arches, domes, concrete)
- **Medieval**: Romanesque (round arches), Gothic (pointed arches, flying buttresses, stained glass)
- **Renaissance**: Classical revival; symmetry; Brunelleschi, Palladio
- **Baroque**: Grandeur, drama, movement; Bernini, Borromini
- **Neoclassical**: Greek/Roman revival; civic architecture
- **Art Nouveau**: Organic forms; ornamentation; Gaudí, Horta
- **Modernism**: Bauhaus, International Style; Le Corbusier, Mies van der Rohe, Wright
- **Postmodernism**: Historical references; irony; Venturi; "less is a bore"
- **Deconstructivism**: Fragmentation; non-linear; Gehry, Zaha Hadid, Libeskind
- **Contemporary**: Parametric design; biomimicry; sustainability-focused; adaptive reuse

### Sustainable Design
- **Passive Design**: Orientation, natural ventilation, thermal mass, daylighting
- **Green Building Rating**: LEED, BREEAM, Passive House, Living Building Challenge
- **Net-Zero Energy**: Buildings that produce as much energy as they consume
- **Biophilic Design**: Connecting occupants with nature; green walls, natural materials
- **Circular Design**: Design for disassembly; material reuse; cradle-to-cradle
- **Resilience**: Climate adaptation; flood resistance; heat resilience

### Urban Design & Planning
- **Urban Morphology**: Street patterns, block sizes, density, mixed use
- **Public Space**: Plazas, parks, streetscapes; Jan Gehl's human-scale design
- **Transit-Oriented Development**: High density around transit nodes
- **New Urbanism**: Walkable neighborhoods; mixed-use; traditional neighborhood design
- **Smart Cities**: IoT, data-driven planning, digital infrastructure
- **Urban Regeneration**: Adaptive reuse; waterfront development; brownfield redevelopment

### Interior Architecture
- **Space Planning**: Programming; adjacencies; circulation; furniture layout
- **Materials & Finishes**: Flooring, wall treatments, ceilings, fabrics
- **Lighting Design**: Ambient, task, accent lighting; daylighting strategies
- **Acoustics**: Sound absorption, reflection, isolation; room acoustics
- **Universal Design**: Accessibility; ADA compliance; inclusive design
- **Biophilic Interiors**: Plants, natural materials, views, natural light

### Landscape Architecture
- **Site Design**: Grading, drainage, planting, hardscape
- **Parks & Recreation**: Urban parks, playgrounds, trails, sports facilities
- **Ecological Design**: Stormwater management, native planting, habitat creation
- **Cultural Landscape**: Heritage landscapes, memorial design, sacred spaces

### Digital & Computational Design
- **BIM**: Building Information Modeling; Revit, ArchiCAD; collaborative design
- **Parametric Design**: Grasshopper, Rhino; algorithmic form-finding
- **Generative Design**: AI-assisted design; optimization; exploration of design space
- **3D Printing**: Additive manufacturing in architecture; large-scale printing
- **Virtual Reality**: Immersive design review; client presentations

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Form Follows Function | Sullivan | Design should derive from purpose |
| Five Points of Architecture | Le Corbusier | Pilotis, free plan, free facade, ribbon windows, roof garden |
| Complexity and Contradiction | Venturi | Embrace ambiguity; "less is a bore" |
| Pattern Language | Alexander | Design patterns for human-centered environments |

- **Frank Lloyd Wright**: Organic architecture; Fallingwater; Guggenheim
- **Le Corbusier**: Modernism; Villa Savoie; Chandigarh; five points
- **Ludwig Mies van der Rohe**: "Less is more"; Barcelona Pavilion; Farnsworth House
- **Zaha Hadid**: Parametric design; fluid forms; MAXXI, Heydar Aliyev Center
- **Tadao Ando**: Concrete; light; Church of the Light
- **Bjarke Ingels**: Pragmatic utopian; BIG; sustainable innovation
- **Shigeru Ban**: Paper architecture; humanitarian design; disaster relief

## Applications

- **Residential Design**: Houses, apartments, housing developments
- **Commercial Architecture**: Offices, retail, hospitality, mixed-use
- **Cultural Buildings**: Museums, theaters, libraries, religious buildings
- **Educational**: Schools, universities, research facilities
- **Healthcare**: Hospitals, clinics, wellness centers
- **Infrastructure**: Airports, train stations, bridges
- **Humanitarian**: Disaster relief, refugee shelters, affordable housing
