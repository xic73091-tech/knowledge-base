# Film & Cinema

## Overview

Film (cinema) is the art of moving images — a medium that combines visual storytelling, sound, performance, and technology to create immersive narrative experiences. It encompasses fiction, documentary, animation, and experimental forms, and is both an art form and a global industry.

## Core Concepts

### Filmmaking Process
- **Development**: Script writing, storyboarding, financing, packaging
- **Pre-Production**: Casting, location scouting, scheduling, production design, rehearsals
- **Production**: Principal photography; directing actors; camera operation; sound recording
- **Post-Production**: Editing, color grading, visual effects (VFX), sound design, music scoring, mixing
- **Distribution**: Theatrical release, streaming platforms, film festivals, DVD/Blu-ray

### Direction
- **Director's Vision**: Aesthetic choices; thematic interpretation; tonal consistency
- **Working with Actors**: Method acting; rehearsal techniques; emotional direction; blocking
- **Mise-en-Scène**: Everything in the frame — set design, costumes, lighting, actor placement
- **Auteur Theory**: Director as primary creative author; personal style across films (Hitchcock, Kubrick, Kurosawa)

### Cinematography
- **Camera Work**: Shot types (extreme wide, wide, medium, close-up, extreme close-up); camera angles (high, low, eye level, Dutch)
- **Camera Movement**: Pan, tilt, dolly, tracking, crane, Steadicam, handheld, drone
- **Lighting**: Three-point lighting (key, fill, back); high-key, low-key; natural vs artificial; motivated lighting
- **Lens & Depth of Field**: Wide angle, normal, telephoto; shallow vs deep focus; anamorphic
- **Film vs Digital**: 35mm, 65mm, IMAX; RED, ARRI Alexa; aesthetic differences
- **Color**: Color temperature; color grading; color palettes for mood (teal and orange, desaturated)

### Editing
- **Continuity Editing**: Shot-reverse-shot, 180-degree rule, match cuts, eyeline matching
- **Montage**: Eisenstein's montage theory; Kuleshov effect; intellectual montage
- **Pacing & Rhythm**: Shot duration; editing rhythm; long takes vs rapid cutting
- **Nonlinear Editing**: Avid, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro
- **Transitions**: Cuts, dissolves, wipes, fades; motivated vs unmotivated transitions

### Screenwriting
- **Story Structure**: Three-act structure; Save the Cat; Hero's Journey; sequence method
- **Character Arc**: Flat vs dynamic characters; want vs need; internal conflict
- **Dialogue**: Subtext; exposition without "on the nose"; distinct character voices
- **Format**: Industry standard; slug lines, action, dialogue; software (Final Draft, WriterSolo)
- **Adaptation**: Book to screen; condensation; fidelity vs creative license

### Sound Design
- **Diegetic vs Non-Diegetic**: Sound within the story world vs added (score, narration)
- **Sound Effects**: Foley; ambient sound; hard effects; design effects
- **Dialogue**: Production sound; ADR (automated dialogue replacement); mixing
- **Film Score**: Original composition; temp tracks; leitmotifs; orchestration
- **Sound Mix**: Stereo, 5.1, 7.1, Dolby Atmos; dynamic range; loudness standards

### Genre Studies
- **Drama**: Character-driven; emotional depth; realism
- **Comedy**: Slapstick, romantic, dark, satirical, screwball, mockumentary
- **Thriller/Suspense**: Hitchcockian tension; plot twists; unreliable narrators
- **Horror**: Jump scares, psychological, slasher, supernatural, body horror, found footage
- **Science Fiction**: Dystopia, space opera, cyberpunk, time travel, hard sci-fi
- **Western**: Revisionist, spaghetti, classic; frontier mythology
- **Documentary**: Observational, participatory, expository, poetic, reflexive
- **Animation**: Traditional, CGI, stop-motion, rotoscoping; anime

### Film Theory & Criticism
- **Formalist Theory**: Eisenstein; montage; cinema as art (not just recording)
- **Realist Theory**: Bazin; long takes; deep focus; cinema as window on reality
- **Psychoanalytic Theory**: Lacan; the gaze; voyeurism; fantasy
- **Feminist Film Theory**: Mulvey's "Visual Pleasure"; male gaze; representation
- **Postcolonial Cinema**: Representation; resistance; Third Cinema movement
- **Cognitive Film Theory**: How viewers process narrative, emotion, attention
- **Auteur Theory**: Director's personal vision across body of work

### World Cinema
- **Hollywood**: Studio system; blockbusters; independent film
- **European Art Cinema**: Italian Neorealism, French New Wave, Dogme 95
- **Asian Cinema**: Japanese (Kurosawa, Ozu, Miyazaki), Chinese (Zhang Yimou, Wong Kar-wai), Korean (Bong Joon-ho, Park Chan-wook), Indian (Bollywood, Ray)
- **Latin American Cinema**: Cinema Novo; Mexican new wave; magical realism on screen
- **African Cinema**: Sembène; Nollywood; postcolonial narratives

## Important Figures

- **D.W. Griffith**: Narrative film grammar; montage; close-up
- **Sergei Eisenstein**: Montage theory; Battleship Potemkin
- **Alfred Hitchcock**: Suspense master; Psycho, Vertigo, Rear Window
- **Akira Kurosawa**: Seven Samurai; Rashomon; cross-cultural influence
- **Stanley Kubrick**: 2001, The Shining, A Clockwork Orange; technical perfection
- **Agnès Varda**: French New Wave; essay films; feminist cinema
- **Martin Scorsese**: Goodfellas, Taxi Driver; film preservation advocacy
- **Hayao Miyazaki**: Animation master; Studio Ghibli
- **Bong Joon-ho**: Parasite; genre-blending; global recognition

## Frontiers

- **AI in Filmmaking**: Generative tools for scriptwriting, storyboarding, VFX, and even synthetic actors — where does human authorship end and machine output begin?
- **Streaming vs Theatrical Distribution**: The pandemic accelerated streaming dominance; the industry still debates theatrical windows, box office relevance, and how release models shape what gets made.
- **Deepfakes & Digital Humans**: De-aging, posthumous performances, and fully synthetic actors raise ethical and legal questions about consent, likeness rights, and authenticity.
- **VR and Immersive Cinema**: 360-degree video and interactive narrative formats challenge traditional editing grammar and directorial control; no dominant storytelling language has emerged.
- **Digital Preservation & Format Obsolescence**: Digital files degrade and formats become unreadable; preserving 21st-century cinema is an unsolved archival problem.

## Classic Works

- **"Film Art: An Introduction"** by David Bordwell and Kristin Thompson — The standard university textbook on film analysis; covers form, style, and history with rigor and clarity
- **"What Is Cinema?"** by Andre Bazin — Foundational realist film theory; argues for long takes and deep focus as cinema's essential qualities
- **"Understanding Movies"** by Louis Giannetti — Accessible survey of film technique and theory; widely used in introductory courses
- **"Hitchcock/Truffaut"** by Francois Truffaut — landmark interview book where the French New Wave director dissects Hitchcock's craft film by film
- **"Sculpting in Time"** by Andrey Tarkovsky — The Russian auteur's meditation on cinema as art, memory, and spiritual experience
