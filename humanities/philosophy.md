# Philosophy

## Overview

Philosophy is the study of fundamental questions about existence, knowledge, values, reason, mind, and language. It employs rational argument and critical thinking to examine the most basic assumptions about reality, ethics, and human understanding. It is the oldest academic discipline and the foundation of all other fields.

## Core Concepts

### Metaphysics (Nature of Reality)
- **Ontology**: What exists? Categories of being; substance vs property
- **Free Will vs Determinism**: Is human action free or causally determined?
- **Mind-Body Problem**: Dualism (Descartes) vs physicalism vs functionalism; the hard problem of consciousness (Chalmers)
- **Personal Identity**: What makes you the same person over time? Memory, body, psychological continuity
- **Causation**: Humean regularity; counterfactual theories; causation and laws of nature
- **Time & Space**: Presentism vs eternalism; A-theory vs B-theory of time
- **Universals & Particles**: Do abstract properties (redness, justice) exist independently?

### Epistemology (Theory of Knowledge)
- **What is Knowledge?**: Justified true belief; Gettier problems
- **Sources of Knowledge**: Perception, reason, memory, testimony, introspection
- **Skepticism**: Can we know anything? Descartes' evil brain; brain in a vat; simulation hypothesis
- **Rationalism vs Empiricism**: Knowledge from reason alone (Plato, Descartes, Leibniz) vs experience (Locke, Hume)
- **A Priori vs A Posteriori**: Knowledge independent of experience vs dependent on it
- **Justification**: Foundationalism, coherentism, reliabilism
- **Truth Theories**: Correspondence, coherence, pragmatic, deflationary

### Ethics (Moral Philosophy)
- **Normative Ethics**:
  - **Deontology**: Duty-based; Kant's categorical imperative (act only on universalizable maxims)
  - **Consequentialism**: Outcome-based; utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill): maximize overall happiness
  - **Virtue Ethics**: Character-based; Aristotle's eudaimonia; virtues as means between extremes
  - **Care Ethics**: Relationships and responsibilities (Gilligan, Noddings)
- **Applied Ethics**: Bioethics, environmental ethics, business ethics, AI ethics
- **Meta-Ethics**: Moral realism vs anti-realism; moral relativism vs universalism; moral facts
- **Moral Psychology**: How do moral judgments work? Trolley problem; moral intuitions

### Logic
- **Formal Logic**: Propositional logic; predicate logic; validity, soundness
- **Informal Logic**: Fallacies (ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, slippery appeal to authority)
- **Modal Logic**: Necessity (□) and possibility (◇); possible worlds
- **Inductive Logic**: Arguments from patterns; problem of induction (Hume)
- **Paraconsistent Logic**: Tolerating contradictions; relevance logic

### Aesthetics (Philosophy of Art & Beauty)
- **What is Beauty?**: Subjective vs objective; Kant's aesthetic judgment
- **Art Theories**: Imitation, expression, formalism, institutional theory
- **Sublime**: Awe and terror in vastness; Burke and Kant
- **Art and Morality**: Does art have moral responsibilities? Censorship debates

### Political Philosophy
- **Justice**: Rawls' veil of ignorance; distributive justice; libertarian (Nozick) vs egalitarian
- **Rights**: Natural rights; human rights; positive vs negative rights
- **Authority & Legitimacy**: Social contract theory; anarchism; democracy
- **Freedom**: Positive (capability) vs negative (non-interference) liberty (Berlin)
- **Equality**: Equality of opportunity vs outcome; capabilities approach (Sen, Nussbaum)

### Philosophy of Mind
- **Consciousness**: Qualia; subjective experience; the hard problem
- **Functionalism**: Mental states as functional roles; multiple realizability
- **Eliminativism**: Folk psychology is false; neuroscience replaces mental talk
- **Panpsychism**: Consciousness is fundamental and ubiquitous
- **Artificial Intelligence**: Can machines think? Chinese Room argument (Searle); Turing test

### Philosophy of Language
- **Meaning & Reference**: Frege's sense and reference; Kripke's rigid designators
- **Speech Acts**: Locution, illocution, perlocution (Austin, Searle)
- **Truth and Meaning**: Davidson; Tarski's truth theory
- **Pragmatics**: Context-dependent meaning; implicature (Grice)

### Philosophy of Science
- **Scientific Method**: Hypothetico-deductive model; inference to best explanation
- **Falsificationism**: Popper; scientific theories must be falsifiable
- **Paradigms & Revolutions**: Kuhn; normal science vs revolutionary science
- **Underdetermination**: Evidence doesn't uniquely determine theory (Quine-Duhem)
- **Realism vs Anti-Realism**: Do scientific theories describe reality?

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Categorical Imperative | Kant | Act only on maxims you could will as universal law |
| Utilitarianism | Bentham, Mill | Maximize overall happiness |
| Virtue Ethics | Aristotle | Cultivate virtues for eudaimonia |
| Social Contract | Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau | Government legitimacy from consent |
| Phenomenology | Husserl | Study structures of consciousness |
| Existentialism | Sartre, Camus | Existence precedes essence; radical freedom |

## Important Figures

- **Socrates**: Dialectical method; "the unexamined life"
- **Plato**: Theory of Forms; The Republic; allegory of the cave
- **Aristotle**: Logic; virtue ethics; metaphysics; empiricism
- **René Descartes**: "I think therefore I am"; rationalism; dualism
- **David Hume**: Empiricism; problem of induction; is-ought gap
- **Immanuel Kant**: Critical philosophy; categorical imperative; epistemology
- **John Stuart Mill**: Utilitarianism; liberty; logic
- **Friedrich Nietzsche**: Will to power; death of God; eternal recurrence
- **Ludwig Wittgenstein**: Language games; private language argument
- **Simone de Beauvoir**: Existentialist feminism; The Second Sex
- **Hannah Arendt**: Political philosophy; banality of evil
