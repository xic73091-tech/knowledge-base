# Art History

## Overview

Art history is the study of visual art — painting, sculpture, architecture, photography, and other visual media — across cultures and time periods. It examines how art reflects, shapes, and responds to historical, social, political, and intellectual contexts. It combines close visual analysis with historical and theoretical interpretation.

## Core Concepts

### Prehistoric & Ancient Art
- **Cave Paintings**: Lascaux, Altamira; animals, hand stencils; symbolic behavior
- **Mesopotamian Art**: Ziggurats; cylinder seals; Lamassu; narrative reliefs
- **Egyptian Art**: Hieratic scale; tomb painting; pyramids; idealized human form; canon of proportions
- **Greek Art**: Archaic (kouros/kore), Classical (ideal beauty, contrapposto), Hellenistic (emotion, drama); Parthenon
- **Roman Art**: Realism (verism); portraiture; mosaics; architecture (Colosseum, Pantheon, aqueducts)
- **Indian Art**: Gandhara; Hindu temple sculpture; Mughal miniature painting; Ajanta caves
- **Chinese Art**: Landscape painting; calligraphy; jade carving; Tang ceramics; Song masters
- **Pre-Columbian Art**: Maya murals; Aztec sculpture; Inca textiles; Nazca lines

### Medieval Art (~500–1400)
- **Byzantine Art**: Icons; Ravenna mosaics; Hagia Sophia; gold backgrounds; frontal figures
- **Romanesque**: Round arches; barrel vaults; tympanum sculpture; illuminated manuscripts
- **Gothic**: Pointed arches; flying buttresses; stained glass (Chartres); sculpture; Sienese painting
- **Islamic Art**: Geometric patterns; arabesques; calligraphy; mosques (Alhambra, Sultan Ahmed)
- **African Art**: Benin bronzes; Nok terracottas; Yoruba sculpture; textiles (kente)

### Renaissance (~1400–1600)
- **Early Renaissance**: Brunelleschi (perspective); Masaccio; Donatello; Botticelli
- **High Renaissance**: Leonardo da Vinci; Michelangelo (Sistine Chapel); Raphael (School of Athens)
- **Northern Renaissance**: Van Eyck (oil painting); Dürer; Bosch; Bruegel
- **Venetian Renaissance**: Titian; colorito vs disegno; Giovanni Bellini
- **Key Innovations**: Linear perspective; chiaroscuro; sfumato; anatomical accuracy; humanism

### Baroque & Rococo (~1600–1750)
- **Baroque**: Caravaggio (tenebrism); Bernini (ecstasy); Rubens; Velázquez; Rembrandt; drama, movement, emotion
- **Rococo**: Watteau; Fragonard; Boucher; lightness, pastel colors, playful themes
- **Dutch Golden Age**: Vermeer; Hals; landscape, genre painting, still life; art market

### Neoclassicism & Romanticism (~1750–1850)
- **Neoclassicism**: David; Ingres; classical revival; reason, order, civic virtue
- **Romanticism**: Delacroix; Turner; Constable; Goya; emotion, nature, sublime, individualism
- **Orientalism**: Exotic depictions of "the East"; power dynamics; Said's critique

### Modern Art (~1850–1970)
- **Impressionism**: Monet, Renoir, Degas, Cassatt; light, color, everyday life; plein air painting
- **Post-Impressionism**: Cézanne (form), Van Gogh (emotion), Gauguin (symbolism), Seurat (pointillism)
- **Fauvism**: Matisse; wild beasts; pure color; expressive form
- **Expressionism**: Munch, Kirchner, Kandinsky; inner emotion; distorted form
- **Cubism**: Picasso, Braque; multiple perspectives; geometric fragmentation; Analytic vs Synthetic
- **Futurism**: Boccioni, Balla; speed, technology, modernity
- **Dada**: Duchamp (readymades); anti-art; absurdity; Cabaret Voltaire
- **Surrealism**: Dalí, Magritte, Ernst; dreams; automatism; the unconscious
- **Abstract Expressionism**: Pollock (drip), Rothko (color field), de Kooning; gesture, scale
- **Pop Art**: Warhol, Lichtenstein; mass culture, consumerism, celebrity
- **Minimalism**: Judd, Andre; geometric reduction; industrial materials; phenomenology
- **Conceptual Art**: Kosuth, Weiner; idea over object; art as language

### Contemporary Art (~1970–Present)
- **Installation Art**: Site-specific; immersive environments (Kapoor, Eliasson)
- **Performance Art**: Abramović, Acconci; body as medium; duration; audience participation
- **Video Art**: Viola, Hill; moving image; time-based media
- **Street Art & Graffiti**: Banksy, Basquiat, JR; public space; political commentary
- **Digital Art**: AI-generated art; NFTs; generative art; net art
- **Global Art World**: Biennials (Venice, Documenta); non-Western contemporary artists; decolonizing art

### Art Theory & Criticism
- **Formalism**: Art analyzed through form (Greenberg); medium specificity
- **Iconography & Iconology**: Panofsky; symbolic meaning in images
- **Semiotics**: Barthes; visual codes; mythologies
- **Feminist Art History**: Nochlin ("Why Have There Been No Great Women Artists?"); Guerrilla Girls
- **Postcolonial Critique**: Challenging Western canon; representation; Orientalism
- **Institutional Theory**: Danto, Dickie; art defined by art world context
- **Visual Culture**: Expanding beyond "fine art" to include media, design, popular culture

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Vasari's Lives | Vasari | Art progresses toward perfection (Renaissance narrative) |
| Hegelian Dialectic | Hegel | Art evolves through historical stages |
| Formalism | Greenberg | Medium-specificity defines artistic value |
| The Gaze | Lacan, Mulvey | Visual pleasure and power in looking |
| Aura | Benjamin | Mechanical reproduction diminishes art's uniqueness |

## Important Figures

- **Giorgio Vasari**: Lives of the Artists; first art historian
- **Erwin Panofsky**: Iconology; Renaissance and symbolism
- **E.H. Gombrich**: The Story of Art; accessible art history
- **John Ruskin**: Art criticism; social role of art
- **Clement Greenberg**: Modernist criticism; Abstract Expressionism
- **Linda Nochlin**: Feminist art history
