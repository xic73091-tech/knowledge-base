# Gender & Sexuality Studies

## Overview

Gender and sexuality studies is an interdisciplinary field examining how gender and sexuality are socially constructed, experienced, and contested. Drawing on feminism, queer theory, postcolonialism, and the social sciences, it analyzes how systems of power organize bodies, identities, and relationships. It is both a scholarly pursuit and a political project aimed at understanding and challenging inequality.

## Core Concepts

### Foundations of Feminist Thought
- **First Wave (19th-early 20th c.)**: Suffrage, legal rights; Wollstonecraft, Stanton, Anthony
- **Second Wave (1960s-80s)**: "The personal is political"; reproductive rights, workplace equality; Friedan, Millett, Firestone
- **Third Wave (1990s-2000s)**: Intersectionality, sex positivity, reclaiming derogatory terms; Butler, hooks, Crenshaw
- **Fourth Wave (2010s-present)**: Digital feminism, #MeToo, body positivity, trans inclusion
- **Ecofeminism**: Connections between domination of women and domination of nature (Shiva, Mies)

### Gender as Social Construction
- **Sex vs Gender**: Biological sex categories vs socially constructed gender roles and identities
- **Gender Performativity** (Butler): Gender is not expressed but constituted through repeated acts
- **Doing Gender** (West & Zimmerman): Gender as an ongoing accomplishment in social interaction
- **Gender Socialization**: How families, schools, media, and peers teach gender norms
- **Hegemonic Masculinity** (Connell): The dominant ideal of manhood that subordinates other masculinities and femininities

### Sexuality & Queer Theory
- **Heteronormativity**: The assumption that heterosexuality is natural, normal, and default
- **Queer Theory**: Challenges binary categories; destabilizes normative sexuality and gender (Sedgwick, Butler)
- **Coming Out**: As a cultural practice and political act; its limits and variations
- **Sexuality and Power**: How institutions (medicine, law, religion) regulate sexual behavior
- **LGBTQ+ History**: Stonewall, decriminalization, marriage equality, ongoing struggles

### Intersectionality
- **Crenshaw's Framework**: How race, gender, class, sexuality, and other axes of identity intersect to produce unique forms of oppression
- **Black Feminism**: The Combahee River Collective; hooks; Collins; centering Black women's experiences
- **Transnational Feminism**: How global power structures shape gender differently across contexts
- **Disability and Gender**: How ableism and sexism interact; crip theory
- **Masculinity Studies**: Examining men's experiences, toxic masculinity, and possibilities for transformation

### Bodies & Reproduction
- **Reproductive Rights**: Abortion, contraception, surrogacy; bodily autonomy debates
- **Bioethics and Gender**: Assisted reproduction, genetic selection, surrogacy markets
- **Body Politics**: Fat studies, beauty standards, body modification, disability
- **Medicalization**: How medicine has pathologized women's bodies, queer identities, and non-normative desires
- **Violence**: Domestic violence, sexual assault, femicide; structural and interpersonal dimensions

### Gender in Institutions
- **Labor**: Gender pay gap, occupational segregation, care work, glass ceiling
- **Education**: Gender gaps in STEM; hidden curriculum; sexual harassment in academia
- **Law**: Title IX, anti-discrimination law, marriage equality, sex work decriminalization
- **Religion**: Women's ordination, queer theology, religious feminism
- **Politics**: Women's representation, gender quotas, feminist policy-making

### Cultural Production
- **Women's Literature**: Woolf, Morrison, Adichie; the female bildungsroman; autofeminism
- **Feminist Art**: Guerrilla Girls, Judy Chicago, performance art; reclaiming representation
- **Queer Cinema**: New Queer Cinema; representation; camp as aesthetic strategy
- **Music**: Riot grrrl, queer hip-hop, feminist punk; gender in pop culture
- **Media Representation**: Bechdel test; stereotypes; trans visibility

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Gender Performativity | Judith Butler | Gender is constituted through repeated stylized acts |
| Intersectionality | Kimberlé Crenshaw | Multiple forms of oppression interact and cannot be separated |
| The Second Sex | Simone de Beauvoir | "One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman" |
| Heteronormativity | Michael Warner | Institutionalized assumption of heterosexuality as default |
| Standpoint Theory | Sandra Harding | Knowledge is situated; marginalized perspectives offer epistemic advantages |

## Important Figures

- **Simone de Beauvoir**: The Second Sex; existentialist feminism
- **Judith Butler**: Gender Trouble; performativity; queer theory
- **bell hooks**: Ain't I a Woman; intersectional feminism; pedagogy
- **Kimberlé Crenshaw**: Intersectionality; critical race feminism
- **Audre Lorde**: Sister Outsider; poetry and essays on race, gender, sexuality
- **Gloria Anzaldúa**: Borderlands/La Frontera; mestiza consciousness; queer theory
- **Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick**: Epistemology of the Closet; foundational queer theorist

## Frontiers

- **Trans Rights and Recognition**: Legal recognition, healthcare access, sports inclusion; evolving debates within feminism
- **AI and Gender Bias**: How algorithms perpetuate gender stereotypes in hiring, lending, and content moderation
- **Global Feminism**: Navigating cultural difference while maintaining solidarity; decolonizing feminist frameworks
- **Non-Binary and Gender-Expansive Identities**: How are categories of gender expanding beyond the binary? What are the implications for law, medicine, and culture?
- **Care Economy**: How should society value and distribute care work (childcare, eldercare, domestic labor)?

## Applications

- **Policy**: Gender mainstreaming; anti-discrimination legislation; parental leave; pay equity
- **Education**: Inclusive curricula; Title IX compliance; gender-inclusive pedagogy
- **Healthcare**: Reproductive health access; gender-affirming care; feminist bioethics
- **Workplace**: Diversity and inclusion; sexual harassment prevention; equal pay audits
- **Law**: Domestic violence legislation; marriage equality; sex work policy
- **Media**: Inclusive representation; feminist media criticism; content creation

## Classic Works

- **"The Second Sex"** by Simone de Beauvoir — The foundational text of second-wave feminism; "one is not born, but rather becomes, a woman"
- **"Gender Trouble"** by Judith Butler — Revolutionary work arguing gender is performative, not essential; foundation of queer theory
- **"Borderlands/La Frontera"** by Gloria Anzaldúa — Exploration of mestiza identity, queerness, and living at cultural borders
- **"Sister Outsider"** by Audre Lorde — Essential essays and speeches on race, sexuality, gender, and the power of difference
- **"Feminism Is for Everybody"** by bell hooks — Accessible manifesto arguing feminism must be anti-racist, anti-classist, and inclusive
