# History

## Overview

History is the study of the past — events, people, societies, and civilizations — through the analysis of written records, oral traditions, material culture, and other evidence. It is not merely a chronicle of events but an interpretive discipline that seeks to understand change over time, causation, and the human experience across cultures and eras.

## Core Concepts

### Historiography
- **What is History?**: Narrative reconstruction of the past; facts vs interpretation
- **Primary Sources**: Documents, artifacts, eyewitness accounts created at the time
- **Secondary Sources**: Scholarly works analyzing primary sources
- **Historical Method**: Source criticism (external, internal); corroboration; contextualization
- **Schools of Thought**: Annales (longue durée), Marxist (class conflict), Whig (progressive), postmodern (narrative), world-systems
- **Objectivity vs Subjectivity**: Can history be objective? Perspective and bias

### Ancient Civilizations (to ~500 CE)
- **Mesopotamia**: Sumerians, Babylonians, Assyrians; cuneiform; Code of Hammurabi
- **Ancient Egypt**: Pharaohs, pyramids, hieroglyphics; Nile-based civilization
- **Indus Valley**: Harappa, Mohenjo-daro; urban planning, drainage systems
- **Ancient China**: Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han dynasties; Confucianism, Legalism; Great Wall
- **Classical Greece**: City-states, democracy (Athens), philosophy (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), drama, Olympics
- **Roman Republic & Empire**: Republic (Senate, consuls), Empire (Augustus), law (Justinian Code), engineering (roads, aqueducts), fall of Rome (476 CE)
- **Mesoamerica**: Maya, Aztec, Inca civilizations

### Medieval Period (~500–1500)
- **Byzantine Empire**: Eastern Roman continuation; Justinian; Hagia Sophia; Orthodox Christianity
- **Islamic Golden Age**: Abbasid Caliphate; algebra, astronomy, medicine, philosophy (Avicenna, Averroes)
- **European Feudalism**: Lords, vassals, serfs; manorial system
- **Crusades**: Religious wars (1096–1291); cultural exchange; consequences
- **Mongol Empire**: Genghis Khan; largest contiguous empire; Pax Mongolica; trade routes
- **Song Dynasty China**: Technology (gunpowder, compass, printing), commerce, Neo-Confucianism
- **Renaissance**: Italy; humanism; art (Leonardo, Michelangelo); printing press (Gutenberg, ~1440)

### Early Modern Period (~1500–1800)
- **Age of Exploration**: Columbus (1492), Vasco da Gama, Magellan; Columbian Exchange
- **Reformation**: Martin Luther (1517); Protestantism; Counter-Reformation; Wars of Religion
- **Scientific Revolution**: Copernicus, Galileo, Kepler, Newton; empiricism and the scientific method
- **Enlightenment**: Reason, liberty, progress; Locke, Voltaire, Rousseau, Kant, Montesquieu
- **Colonialism**: European empires in Americas, Africa, Asia; mercantilism; Atlantic slave trade
- **American Revolution** (1776): Independence from Britain; Declaration of Independence; Constitution
- **French Revolution** (1789): Liberty, equality, fraternity; Reign of Terror; Napoleon

### Modern Period (~1800–1945)
- **Industrial Revolution**: Britain; steam power, factories, urbanization; social upheaval
- **Imperialism**: Scramble for Africa; British Raj; Opium Wars; unequal treaties
- **World War I** (1914–1918): Trench warfare; collapse of empires (Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian, Russian)
- **Russian Revolution** (1917): Bolsheviks; Lenin; Soviet Union; communism
- **Interwar Period**: Treaty of Versailles; Great Depression; rise of fascism (Mussolini, Hitler)
- **World War II** (1939–1945): Axis vs Allies; Holocaust; atomic bombs; United Nations founded
- **Chinese Revolution**: Qing dynasty fall (1912); warlord era; civil war; PRC established (1949)

### Contemporary Period (~1945–Present)
- **Cold War**: US vs USSR; NATO vs Warsaw Pact; nuclear deterrence; proxy wars (Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan)
- **Decolonization**: Indian independence (1947); African independence movements; Non-Aligned Movement
- **China's Transformation**: Mao era; Cultural Revolution; Reform and Opening Up (1978); economic rise
- **Globalization**: Trade liberalization; internet; cultural exchange; backlash
- **End of Cold War**: Fall of Berlin Wall (1989); dissolution of USSR (1991)
- **21st Century**: War on Terror; 2008 financial crisis; rise of populism; COVID-19 pandemic; climate crisis

### Thematic History
- **Economic History**: Trade routes, industrialization, globalization, financial crises
- **Social History**: Class, gender, race; everyday life; family structures
- **Cultural History**: Ideas, art, religion, intellectual movements
- **Military History**: Strategy, technology, wars, peace treaties
- **Environmental History**: Human-environment interaction; deforestation, pollution, climate
- **Gender History**: Women's rights, suffrage, feminism; masculinity studies
- **Digital History**: Computational methods; digital archives; data visualization

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |

## Important Figures

- **Herodotus**: Father of history; The Histories
- **Thucydides**: Critical historian; History of the Peloponnesian War
- **Sima Qian**: Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji); father of Chinese historiography
- **Ibn Khaldun**: Muqaddimah; cyclical theory of civilizations
- **Edward Said**: Orientalism; postcolonial criticism
- **Fernand Braudel**: Mediterranean world; Annales school
- **Howard Zinn**: A People's History of the United States

## Frontiers

- **Deep History and Big History**: Integrating archaeological, geological, and biological evidence into narratives that span millions of years, challenging the primacy of written records
- **Digital History and Computational Methods**: Using text mining, GIS, network analysis, and large-scale data to identify patterns invisible to traditional close reading
- **Decolonizing the Archive**: Whose voices are preserved and whose are silenced? Reconstructing subaltern histories from fragmentary and biased colonial records
- **Memory Studies and Contested Pasts**: How societies remember and forget; memorialization, historical trauma, and the politics of monuments and commemorations
- **Climate and Environmental History**: Understanding past human-environment interactions to inform responses to the current climate crisis; the Anthropocene as a historical category

## Applications

- **Policy Analysis**: Historical context for current decisions
- **Diplomacy**: Understanding historical grievances and alliances
- **Education**: Civic identity, critical thinking, cultural literacy
- **Museums & Heritage**: Preservation, interpretation, public history
- **Journalism**: Long-form reporting, investigative history
- **Genealogy**: Family history, DNA and archival research

## Classic Works

- **"The Histories"** by Herodotus — The foundational work of Western historiography; narrative account of the Greco-Persian Wars with ethnographic digressions
- **"The Muqaddimah"** by Ibn Khaldun — Pioneering treatise on the philosophy of history; cyclical theory of civilizations and the role of social cohesion (asabiyyah)
- **"The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II"** by Fernand Braudel — Landmark of the Annales school; demonstrates the longue durée approach to structural history
- **"The Oxford History of the French Revolution"** by William Doyle — Rigorous single-volume account that balances political, social, and cultural dimensions of 1789
- **"A People's History of the United States"** by Howard Zinn — Retells American history from the perspective of marginalized groups; influential for public history and social history
