# Media Studies

## Overview

Media Studies is an interdisciplinary field that examines media technologies, content, industries, audiences, and their social, cultural, political, and economic impacts. It encompasses the study of traditional media (newspapers, radio, television, film) as well as digital and social media. Media Studies analyzes how media shape our understanding of the world, influence public opinion, construct identity, and structure power relations in society.

## Core Concepts

### Media Theory
- **Media Ecology**: Media as environments shaping human thought and behavior (McLuhan, Postman)
- **Agenda-Setting Theory**: Media sets the public agenda through what it covers
- **Cultivation Theory**: Television cultivates perceptions of reality (Gerbner)
- **Uses and Gratifications**: Audiences actively use media to satisfy needs
- **Encoding/Decoding Model**: Producers encode messages, audiences decode them (Hall)
- **Political Economy of Media**: Media ownership, concentration, and power relations

### Media History
- **Print Media**: Gutenberg press, newspapers, magazines, book publishing
- **Telegraph & Telephone**: Electrical communication, early networks
- **Radio & Television**: Broadcasting, national audiences, advertising
- **Film & Cinema**: Silent era, studio system, independent film
- **Digital Revolution**: Personal computers, internet, World Wide Web
- **Social Media & Web 2.0**: User-generated content, participatory culture

### Media Industries
- **Ownership & Concentration**: Big Media, vertical/horizontal integration
- **Advertising & Commercial Media**: Commercial model, attention economy
- **Public Media**: PBS, BBC, non-commercial broadcasting
- **Independent Media**: Alternative press, independent film, grassroots
- **Global Media**: Transnational corporations, cultural imperialism, globalization
- **Media Policy & Regulation**: FCC, Ofcom, net neutrality, content regulations

### Media Content & Representation
- **Stereotyping**: Media representations of race, gender, class, sexuality, disability
- **Media Framing**: How issues are presented and contextualized
- **News Values**: Criteria for selecting and constructing news
- **Genre**: Film/TV genres, conventions, audience expectations
- **Narrative**: Storytelling in media, structure, conventions
- **Intertextuality**: How media references and builds on other media

### Digital & Social Media
- **Web 2.0 & User-Generated Content**: YouTube, blogs, wikis, social platforms
- **Social Media Platforms**: Facebook, Instagram, Twitter/X, TikTok, Snapchat
- **Platform Studies**: Algorithms, APIs, platform governance, business models
- **Digital Culture**: Memes, influencers, viral content, fandom
- **Surveillance Capitalism**: Data collection, targeted advertising, privacy
- **Digital Divide**: Access, skills, participation gaps

### Media Audiences
- **Audience Research**: Surveys, focus groups, ethnography, analytics
- **Active Audiences**: Users as producers, participatory culture, fandom
- **Fandom & Fan Culture**: Fan fiction, conventions, fan practices
- **Global Audiences**: Transnational reception, cultural hybridity
- **Media Effects**: Short-term vs long-term, intended vs unintended effects
- **Media Literacy**: Critical consumption, evaluation skills, digital literacy

### Journalism & News Media
- **Journalism Ethics & Norms**: Objectivity, balance, truth-telling
- **News Production**: Newsrooms, beats, sourcing, gatekeeping
- **Fake News & Misinformation**: Disinformation, misinformation, malinformation
- **Digital Journalism**: Online news, data journalism, multimedia
- **Citizen Journalism**: User-generated news, eyewitness reporting
- **Journalism & Democracy**: Fourth estate, watchdog role, media and politics

### Film & Moving Image Media
- **Film Form & Style**: Cinematography, editing, sound, mise-en-scène
- **Film Theory**: Auteur theory, feminist film theory, psychoanalytic film theory
- **Television Studies**: TV genres, seriality, flow, quality TV
- **Streaming Platforms**: Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, binge-watching culture
- **Documentary**: Non-fiction film, truth claims, documentary modes
- **World Cinema**: National cinemas, transnational film, film festivals

### Sound & Audio Media
- **Radio Studies**: Radio history, formats, radio drama, podcasting
- **Music & Media**: Music videos, music industry, streaming, radio
- **Podcasting**: Podcast formats, production, monetization, audiences
- **Sound Studies**: Acoustic culture, sound design, audio aesthetics
- **Voice Assistants & Smart Speakers**: Alexa, Siri, audio interfaces

### Games & Interactive Media
- **Game Studies**: Game design, mechanics, dynamics, aesthetics
- **Gamification**: Applying game elements to non-game contexts
- **Esports**: Competitive gaming, tournaments, professional leagues
- **Virtual & Augmented Reality**: VR, AR, immersive media
- **Interactive Storytelling**: Choice-based narratives, hypertext, interactive fiction

### Media & Identity
- **Race & Ethnicity in Media**: Representation, stereotypes, diversity
- **Gender & Sexuality**: Feminist media studies, queer media, LGBTQ+ representation
- **Class & Media**: Working-class representations, media and inequality
- **Disability & Media**: Disability representations, ableism, accessible media
- **National & Transnational Identity**: National cinema, global media flows
- **Intersectionality**: Multiple identities and their interactions in media

### Media & Politics
- **Political Communication**: Campaigns, advertising, news coverage
- **Media & Propaganda**: Historical and contemporary propaganda
- **Public Sphere**: Habermas, mediated public sphere, counterpublics
- **Social Movements & Media**: Activist media, hashtag activism, digital organizing
- **Media & War/Crisis**: War reporting, crisis communication, media and conflict
- **Censorship & Freedom of Expression**: Media censorship, free speech debates

### Media Ethics & Issues
- **Privacy & Surveillance**: Data privacy, surveillance, ethical issues
- **Intellectual Property**: Copyright, fair use, piracy, open access
- **Representation & Diversity**: Media diversity, inclusion, underrepresentation
- **Media Violence**: Effects of violent media, debates and research
- **Addiction & Mental Health**: Screen time, social media and well-being
- **Misinformation & Trust**: Fake news, fact-checking, media trust

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| The Medium is the Message | Marshall McLuhan | Media form shapes what and how we communicate |
| Agenda-Setting | McCombs & Shaw | Media doesn't tell us what to think, but what to think about |
| Cultivation Theory | George Gerbner | TV shapes our perception of the "real world" |
| Encoding/Decoding | Stuart Hall | Audiences decode media messages in different ways (dominant, negotiated, oppositional) |
| Uses & Gratifications | Katz, Blumler, Gurevitch | Audiences actively choose media to satisfy needs |
| Political Economy of Media | Herman & Chomsky, Schiller | Media ownership shapes content in service of power |
| Medium Theory | Harold Innis | Media biases shape civilizations |
