# Linguistics

## Overview

Linguistics is the scientific study of language — its structure (phonetics, phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics), its use in context (pragmatics, sociolinguistics), its acquisition (psycholinguistics), its historical change (historical linguistics), and its computational modeling (computational linguistics). It seeks to understand what all human languages have in common and how they differ.

## Core Concepts

### Phonetics & Phonology
- **Phonetics**: Physical production (articulatory), transmission (acoustic), perception (auditory) of speech sounds
- **IPA**: International Phonetic Alphabet; universal transcription system
- **Phonemes**: Contrastive sound units in a language; minimal pairs
- **Allophones**: Contextual variants of phonemes
- **Phonological Rules**: Assimilation, dissimilation, deletion, insertion; distinctive features
- **Syllable Structure**: Onset, nucleus, coda; sonority hierarchy
- **Suprasegmentals**: Stress, tone, intonation; pitch accent languages

### Morphology
- **Morphemes**: Smallest meaning-bearing units; free vs bound; roots, affixes
- **Word Formation**: Derivation (un- + happy), compounding (blackboard), conversion, clipping, blending
- **Inflectional Morphology**: Tense, number, case, gender, agreement
- **Agglutinative vs Fusional**: Turkish (agglutinative) vs Latin (fusional); isolating (Chinese)
- **Morphophonology**: Interaction between morphology and phonology

### Syntax
- **Constituent Structure**: Phrases (NP, VP, PP, AP); phrase structure rules
- **Grammatical Relations**: Subject, object, complement; word order typology (SVO, SOV, VSO)
- **Generative Grammar**: Chomsky; Universal Grammar; X-bar theory; movement
- **Dependency Grammar**: Head-dependent relations; valency
- **Construction Grammar**: Form-meaning pairings as basic units
- **Syntax Trees**: Phrase structure; constituency tests (substitution, movement, coordination)

### Semantics
- **Word Meaning**: Lexical semantics; polysemy, homonymy, synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy
- **Sentence Meaning**: Compositionality; truth conditions; logical form
- **Semantic Roles**: Agent, patient, theme, experiencer, instrument
- **Tense & Aspect**: Perfective, imperfective; past, present, future
- **Presupposition & Implicature**: What is assumed vs what is implied
- **Semantic Fields**: Networks of related meanings

### Pragmatics
- **Context & Meaning**: How context shapes interpretation
- **Speech Acts**: Locutionary, illocutionary, perlocutionary acts (Austin, Searle)
- **Grice's Maxims**: Quantity, quality, relation, manner; conversational implicature
- **Politeness Theory**: Face-threatening acts; positive/negative face (Brown & Levinson)
- **Deixis**: Context-dependent reference (I, here, now, this)
- **Discourse Analysis**: Cohesion, coherence; conversation analysis; turn-taking

### Sociolinguistics
- **Language Variation**: Dialects, registers, styles; idiolect, sociolect
- **Language & Social Identity**: Class, ethnicity, gender, age
- **Code-Switching**: Alternating between languages or varieties
- **Language Attitudes**: Prestige (overt/covert); standard vs non-standard
- **Language Policy & Planning**: Official languages; language revival; standardization
- **Pidgins & Creoles**: Contact languages; creolization process

### Historical Linguistics
- **Language Change**: Sound changes (Grimm's law); semantic change; grammaticalization
- **Language Families**: Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan, Afro-Asiatic, Austronesian, Niger-Congo
- **Comparative Method**: Reconstructing proto-languages; cognates
- **Etymology**: Word origins and history
- **Language Endangerment**: UNESCO Atlas; revitalization efforts

### Psycholinguistics & Language Acquisition
- **First Language Acquisition**: Babbling, one-word, two-word, telegraphic stages; critical period hypothesis
- **Second Language Acquisition**: Interlanguage; fossilization; input hypothesis (Krashen)
- **Language Processing**: Parsing; garden-path sentences; lexical access
- **Bilingualism**: Code-switching; bilingual advantage debate; language attrition
- **Language & Thought**: Sapir-Whorf hypothesis; linguistic relativity; color terms

### Computational Linguistics & NLP
- **Parsing**: Syntactic analysis; constituency and dependency parsing
- **Machine Translation**: Statistical, neural (transformer-based); BLEU scores
- **Speech Recognition**: ASR; acoustic models; language models
- **Corpus Linguistics**: Large-scale text analysis; frequency analysis; collocations
- **Large Language Models**: Transformers; BERT, GPT; pre-training and fine-tuning
- **Sentiment Analysis**: Opinion mining; aspect-based sentiment

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Universal Grammar | Chomsky | Innate linguistic capacity shared by all humans |
| Gricean Pragmatics | Grice | Communication relies on cooperative principle |
| Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis | Sapir, Whorf | Language influences thought and worldview |
| Grammaticalization | Various | Lexical items become grammatical markers over time |
| Optimality Theory | Prince, Smolensky | Grammar as constraint ranking |

## Frontiers

- **Language and Large Language Models**: How do models like GPT process and generate language? Do they learn grammar or just statistical patterns, and what does this tell us about human linguistic cognition?
- **The Origins of Language**: When and how did human language emerge? The lack of direct evidence makes this one of the hardest open questions in science
- **Language Endangerment and Revitalization**: Roughly half of the world's 7,000 languages may disappear this century; what are the most effective strategies for documentation and revitalization?
- **Universal Grammar Under Challenge**: Chomsky's innateness hypothesis faces mounting criticism from usage-based, emergentist, and typological approaches that emphasize language as a cultural and cognitive adaptation
- **The Neuroscience of Language**: How does the brain encode, process, and produce language? Advances in neuroimaging are refining — and complicating — classic models like Broca's and Wernicke's areas

## Applications

- **NLP & AI**: Machine translation, chatbots, voice assistants, text mining
- **Language Teaching**: Second language pedagogy; curriculum design
- **Speech Therapy**: Language disorders; aphasia; fluency disorders
- **Forensic Linguistics**: Author identification; threat analysis; trademark disputes
- **Lexicography**: Dictionary design and compilation
- **Translation & Interpretation**: Literary, technical, conference translation
- **Language Documentation**: Endangered language preservation

## Classic Works

- **"Course in General Linguistics"** by Ferdinand de Saussure — Foundational text of modern linguistics; introduces the concepts of the sign, langue vs parole, and synchronic analysis
- **"Aspects of the Theory of Syntax"** by Noam Chomsky — Establishes generative grammar and the competence/performance distinction; reshapes the field around formal syntax
- **"The Language Instinct"** by Steven Pinker — Accessible account of the nativist view of language acquisition; argues that language is a biological adaptation, not a cultural invention
- **"Language"** by Edward Sapir — Classic introduction to linguistics that emphasizes the relationship between language, culture, and thought; early formulation of linguistic relativity
- **"Sociolinguistic Patterns"** by William Labov — Establishes empirical methods for studying language variation and change in real communities; founding work of variationist sociolinguistics
