# Archaeology

## Overview

Archaeology is the scientific study of human activity through the recovery and analysis of material culture. The archaeological record includes artifacts, architecture, biofacts (eco-facts), and cultural landscapes. It spans from the earliest stone tools (3.3 million years ago) to contemporary material culture.

## Core Concepts

### Methods & Techniques
- **Survey**: Surface survey, aerial photography, LiDAR, satellite imagery; site prospection
- **Excavation**: Stratigraphic recording; single-context recording; open-area vs trench excavation
- **Recording**: Harris matrix; plan and section drawings; photogrammetry; 3D scanning
- **Sampling**: Flotation for botanical remains; soil sampling; sieving strategies

### Dating Methods
- **Relative Dating**: Stratigraphy (superposition); typology; seriation
- **Absolute Dating**:
  - Radiocarbon (C-14): Organic materials up to ~50,000 years
  - Potassium-Argon (K-Ar): Volcanic rocks; millions of years
  - Thermoluminescence (TL) / Optically Stimulated Luminescence (OSL): Ceramics, sediments
  - Dendrochronology: Tree-ring dating; calibration curve for radiocarbon
  - Uranium-series: Caves, corals; up to 500,000 years

### Material Culture Analysis
- **Lithic Analysis**: Stone tools; debitage; reduction sequences; typologies
- **Ceramics**: Pottery classification; temper, firing, decoration; seriation
- **Metalwork**: Copper, bronze, iron; metallurgical analysis; XRF
- **Organic Remains**: Textiles, leather, wood; preservation conditions
- **Art & Symbolism**: Rock art, figurines, personal ornaments; cognitive archaeology

### Bioarchaeology
- **Human Remains**: Osteology; age, sex, stature estimation; pathology; trauma
- **Diet & Subsistence**: Stable isotope analysis (C, N); dental calculus; paleodiet
- **Paleopathology**: Disease, malnutrition, injury in skeletal remains
- **Ancient DNA (aDNA)**: Population genetics; migration; kinship; Neanderthal genome
- **Zooarchaeology**: Animal bones; butchery marks; domestication; species identification
- **Paleoethnobotany**: Plant remains; seeds, pollen, phytoliths; agriculture origins

### Key Periods & Transitions
- **Paleolithic**: Oldowan tools → Acheulean hand axes → Middle Stone Age → Upper Paleolithic revolution
- **Neolithic Revolution**: Agriculture origins (~10,000 BCE); Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica; domestication
- **Urban Revolution**: First cities (Uruk, Mohenjo-daro); writing; state formation
- **Bronze Age**: Metallurgy; trade networks; palace economies (Minoan, Mycenaean)
- **Iron Age**: Iron smelting; alphabetic writing; expanding trade
- **Classical Civilizations**: Greece, Rome, Han China, Maurya India
- **Medieval Archaeology**: Castles, monasteries, towns; Viking, Islamic archaeology
- **Historical Archaeology**: Post-1500; colonial contexts; industrial archaeology

### Theoretical Approaches
- **Processual (New) Archaeology**: Hypothesis testing; middle-range theory; systems theory (Binford)
- **Post-Processual**: Interpretive; meaning, agency, ideology; critique of objectivity
- **Cognitive Archaeology**: Symbolic behavior; religion; art origins
- **Archaeology of Identity**: Gender, ethnicity, age, status in material culture
- **Community Archaeology**: Stakeholder engagement; descendant communities; public archaeology

### Heritage & Conservation
- **Cultural Resource Management (CRM)**: Legal compliance; survey and mitigation
- **Conservation**: Artifact preservation; environmental control; consolidation
- **Repatriation**: NAGPRA (US); return of human remains and sacred objects
- **World Heritage**: UNESCO sites; protection of cultural landscapes
- **Digital Heritage**: 3D modeling; virtual museums; open access archives

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Three-Age System | Thomsen | Stone → Bronze → Iron ages |
| Middle-Range Theory | Binford | Bridge between static archaeological record and dynamic past |
| Agency Theory | Various | People as active agents, not passive responders to systems |
| World Systems Theory | Wallerstein | Core-periphery dynamics in ancient trade |

## Important Figures

- **Howard Carter**: Tutankhamun's tomb discovery
- **Heinrich Schliemann**: Troy and Mycenae excavations
- **Mortimer Wheeler**: Stratigraphic excavation method
- **Lewis Binford**: Processual archaeology; middle-range theory
- **Ian Hodder**: Post-processual archaeology; Çatalhöyük excavation
- **Mary Leakey**: Olduvai Gorge; early human fossils

## Applications

- **Cultural Heritage**: Site preservation, museum curation, public outreach
- **CRM/Consulting**: Compliance surveys, mitigation excavations
- **Forensic Archaeology**: Crime scene investigation; mass graves
- **Environmental Archaeology**: Past climates, land use, human-environment interaction
- **Indigenous Heritage**: Collaborative research; traditional knowledge integration
- **Space Archaeology**: Satellite remote sensing; site prospection
