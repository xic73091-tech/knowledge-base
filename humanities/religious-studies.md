# Religious Studies

## Overview

Religious studies is the academic, non-confessional study of religion as a human phenomenon. It examines religious beliefs, practices, texts, institutions, and experiences across cultures and history, using historical, sociological, anthropological, philosophical, and comparative methods.

## Core Concepts

### Major World Religions

#### Abrahamic Religions
- **Judaism**: Torah, Talmud; covenant; monotheism; diaspora; denominations (Orthodox, Conservative, Reform)
- **Christianity**: Bible (Old & New Testament); Trinity; Jesus Christ; sacraments; denominations (Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant)
- **Islam**: Quran, Hadith; Five Pillars; Muhammad; Sunni/Shia split; Sufism

#### Eastern Religions
- **Hinduism**: Vedas, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita; dharma, karma, samsara, moksha; multiple deities; caste system
- **Buddhism**: Four Noble Truths; Eightfold Path; Siddhartha Gautama; Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana; nirvana
- **Confucianism**: Analects; ren (benevolence), li (ritual), filial piety; social harmony
- **Daoism (Taoism)**: Dao De Jing (Laozi); wu wei (non-action); naturalness; yin-yang
- **Shinto**: Kami; shrines; purification; kami-no-michi; Japanese indigenous tradition

#### Other Traditions
- **Sikhism**: Guru Granth Sahib; ten Gurus; equality; langar (communal kitchen)
- **Judaism's Mystical Traditions**: Kabbalah; Zohar; sefirot
- **Indigenous Religions**: Animism; ancestor worship; oral traditions; shamanism
- **African Traditional Religions**: Yoruba, Vodun; orishas; spirit world; syncretism (Santería, Candomblé)
- **Zoroastrianism**: Ahura Mazda; dualism; influence on Judaism, Christianity, Islam
- **Bahá'í Faith**: Unity of religion; progressive revelation; Bahá'u'lláh

### Core Themes

#### Theology & Philosophy of Religion
- **God Concepts**: Monotheism, polytheism, pantheism, panentheism, deism, atheism, agnosticism
- **Theodicy**: Problem of evil; why does God allow suffering? (Augustine, Leibniz, process theology)
- **Proofs of God**: Cosmological (Aquinas), teleological (design), ontological (Anselm), moral
- **Religious Experience**: Mysticism; altered states; numinous (Otto); perennial philosophy
- **Afterlife**: Heaven, hell, reincarnation, nirvana, annihilation; near-death experiences
- **Sacred & Profane**: Durkheim; hierophany (Eliade); sacred space and time

#### Ritual & Practice
- **Ritual**: Liturgy, sacraments, prayer, meditation, sacrifice, pilgrimage
- **Rites of Passage**: Initiation, marriage, funeral; van Gennep's three stages
- **Calendar & Festivals**: Lunar/solar calendars; Christmas, Easter, Ramadan, Diwali, Hanukkah, Vesak
- **Pilgrimage**: Mecca (Hajj), Jerusalem, Varanasi, Santiago de Compostela, Lourdes
- **Asceticism & Monasticism**: Renunciation; monastic rules (Benedictine, Buddhist vinaya)

#### Religion & Society
- **Secularization**: Decline of religion in modern societies; secular societies
- **Religious Revival**: Fundamentalism; Pentecostalism; Islamic revival; New Age
- **Religion & Politics**: Theocracy; state religion; religious freedom; religious nationalism
- **Religion & Science**: Complementary (Gould's NOMA), conflicting (Dawkins), dialogue (Polkinghorne)
- **Interfaith Dialogue**: Common ground; shared values; pluralism (Hick)
- **Gender & Religion**: Women's roles; feminist theology; masculinity in religion

#### Mythology & Symbolism
- **Myth**: Sacred narratives; creation myths; hero's journey (Campbell)
- **Symbolism**: Cross, crescent, om, yin-yang, Star of David; archetypes (Jung)
- **Cosmology**: Creation stories across cultures; cosmogonic myths
- **Comparative Mythology**: Common motifs; flood narratives; dying-and-rising gods

### Academic Approaches
- **Phenomenology of Religion**: Describing religious experience from within (Eliade, Otto)
- **Sociology of Religion**: Durkheim (social function), Weber (Protestant ethic), Berger (sacred canopy)
- **Anthropology of Religion**: Ritual, symbolism, magic vs religion (Evans-Pritchard, Geertz)
- **Psychology of Religion**: James (religious experience), Freud (illusion), Jung (archetypes)
- **Feminist Theology**: Reclaiming women's voices; critique of patriarchy; inclusive language
- **Postcolonial Theology**: Decolonizing religious discourse; indigenous theologies

## Frontiers

- **Religion and Artificial Intelligence**: Can AI be religious? How do algorithmic systems reshape spiritual practice, and what happens when religious authority is mediated by technology?
- **Secularization and Post-Secular Societies**: Berger's secularization thesis is contested by global religious revival; what frameworks best describe the complex relationship between modernity and religion?
- **Cognitive Science of Religion**: Why do humans tend toward religious belief? Exploring the evolutionary, neurological, and psychological origins of religiosity (Boyer, Barrett, Atran)
- **Religion and Climate Ethics**: How do different religious traditions frame environmental responsibility? The growing intersection of faith-based movements and ecological activism
- **Indigenous Religious Freedom**: Tensions between indigenous sacred sites, land rights, and state/market interests; the limits of legal frameworks like the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples

## Applications

- **Journalism**: Reporting on religion; religious freedom issues
- **Diplomacy**: Religious actors in conflict resolution; faith-based organizations
- **Counseling**: Pastoral care; spiritual direction; grief support

## Classic Works

- **"The Elementary Forms of Religious Life"** by Emile Durkheim — Foundational sociological study of religion; argues that religion is a social phenomenon rooted in collective effervescence
- **"The Idea of the Holy"** by Rudolf Otto — Introduces the concept of the numinous; analyzes the non-rational dimension of religious experience as mysterium tremendum et fascinans
- **"The Sacred and the Profane"** by Mircea Eliade — Defines the phenomenology of religion; distinguishes sacred and profane space and time as fundamental categories of religious experience
- **"The World's Religions"** by Huston Smith — Widely read comparative introduction; presents the essential teachings and practices of major world religions with empathy and clarity
- **"A History of God"** by Karen Armstrong — Traces the evolution of the concept of God across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam; accessible yet scholarly
