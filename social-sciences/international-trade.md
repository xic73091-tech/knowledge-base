# International Trade

## Overview

International trade is the exchange of goods and services across national boundaries. It has been a fundamental driver of economic growth, cultural exchange, and geopolitical relationships throughout history. International trade theory examines why countries trade, what they trade, and the effects of trade on domestic industries, workers, and consumers. Understanding international trade is essential for policymakers, businesses, and citizens in an increasingly interconnected global economy.

## Core Concepts

### Trade Theory Foundations
- **Absolute Advantage**: Adam Smith's theory; countries specialize in goods they produce most efficiently
- **Comparative Advantage**: David Ricardo's theory; specialization based on relative efficiency gains
- **Heckscher-Ohlin Model**: Factor proportions theory; resource endowments drive trade patterns
- **Factor Price Equalization**: Trade equalizes factor prices across countries
- **New Trade Theory**: Krugman; economies of scale; monopolistic competition; strategic trade
- **Gravity Model**: Trade volume proportional to GDP; inversely proportional to distance
- **Intra-industry Trade**: Trade in similar products within same industry; horizontal and vertical

### Trade Policy & Instruments
- **Tariffs**: Import taxes; specific vs ad valorem; protective vs revenue tariffs
- **Quotas**: Quantity restrictions; absolute vs tariff-rate quotas
- **Subsidies**: Government payments; production subsidies; export subsidies
- **Non-Tariff Barriers**: Regulations; standards; licensing; customs procedures
- **Trade Facilitation**: Reducing transaction costs; customs modernization; border procedures
- **Export Controls**: Technology restrictions; national security controls
- **Local Content Requirements**: Mandating domestic production percentage

### Trade Agreements & Institutions
- **World Trade Organization (WTO)**: Multilateral trade rules; dispute settlement; trade negotiations
- **Regional Trade Agreements (RTAs)**: Free trade areas; customs unions; common markets
- **Bilateral Investment Treaties (BITs)**: Investment protection; dispute resolution
- **GATT Principles**: Most-favored-nation; national treatment; transparency
- **NAFTA/USMCA**: North American trade agreement; rules of origin; dispute resolution
- **EU Single Market**: Four freedoms; harmonization; common external tariff
- **APEC & ASEAN**: Regional economic cooperation forums

### Trade & Economic Development
- **Trade & Growth**: Export-led growth; import substitution; trade multipliers
- **Developing Country Trade**: Special and differential treatment; capacity building
- **Least Developed Countries (LDCs)**: Preferential access; duty-free quotas
- **Trade Diversification**: Moving up value chains; export complexity
- **Industrial Policy**: Strategic industries; infant industry protection
- **Technology Transfer**: FDI spillovers; licensing; embodied technology in trade
- **Trade & Inequality**: Winners and losers; skill-biased technical change

### Trade & Labor

- **Trade Adjustment Assistance**: Programs for workers displaced by trade
- **Offshoring & Outsourcing**: Global value chains; back-shoring; reshoring

- **Child Labor & Trade**: Ethical sourcing; consumer pressure; international standards
- **Wage Effects of Trade**: Developed vs developing country wage impacts
- **Trade Unions & Globalization**: Collective bargaining; global union networks

### Trade & Environment
- **Trade & Climate Change**: Carbon border adjustments; emissions embedded in trade
- **Environmental Kuznets Curve**: Trade-environment relationship; pollution haven hypothesis

### Balance of Payments & Trade Balance
- **Current Account**: Trade balance; primary/secondary income; current transfers
- **Capital Account**: Capital transfers; acquisition/disposal of non-produced assets
- **Financial Account**: Direct investment; portfolio investment; reserve assets
- **Trade Balance**: Exports minus imports; goods vs services; bilateral balances
- **Current Account Deficits**: Financing; sustainability; crowding out
- **Twin Deficits**: Budget and current account deficits relationship
- **Marshall-Lerner Condition**: Exchange rate effects on trade balance

### Exchange Rates & Trade
- **Nominal vs Real Exchange Rates**: Currency value; purchasing power parity
- **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)**: Law of one price; Big Mac Index
- **Exchange Rate Systems**: Fixed; floating; managed float; currency boards
- **Currency Manipulation**: Undervaluation; trade distorting practices; WTO rules
- **Exchange Rate Pass-Through**: Effect of currency changes on import/export prices
- **J-Curve Effect**: Short-run deterioration; long-run improvement after devaluation
- **Impossible Trinity**: Fixed exchange rates; free capital movement; monetary policy

### Trade Wars & Protectionism

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Absolute Advantage | Adam Smith | Countries should specialize in goods they produce most efficiently |
| Comparative Advantage | David Ricardo | Gains from trade even if one country is less efficient in all goods |
| Heckscher-Ohlin Model | Heckscher & Ohlin | Trade based on factor endowments; capital vs labor abundant countries |
| New Trade Theory | Krugman | Economies of scale; monopolistic competition; strategic trade policy |
| Gravity Model | Tinbergen | Bilateral trade proportional to GDP, inversely proportional to distance |
| Leontief Paradox | Wassily Leontief | US exported labor-intensive goods despite being capital-abundant |
