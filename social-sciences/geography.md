# Geography

## Overview

Geography is the study of places and the relationships between people and their environments. It bridges natural and social sciences, examining both the physical features of Earth and how humans organize, use, and modify space. The two main branches are physical geography and human geography.

## Core Concepts

### Physical Geography
- **Geomorphology**: Landforms and landscape evolution; erosion, tectonics, glaciation
- **Climatology**: Climate classification (Köppen); climate zones; climate change
- **Biogeography**: Distribution of species and ecosystems; biomes
- **Hydrology**: Rivers, lakes, groundwater; watershed management
- **Soil Geography**: Soil types, formation, classification; pedology
- **Coastal & Marine Geography**: Coastlines, coral reefs, sea-level change

### Human Geography
- **Population Geography**: Distribution, density, migration, demographic transition
- **Urban Geography**: City structure (concentric zone, sector, multiple nuclei models); urbanization; megacities
- **Economic Geography**: Location theory (Weber, Christaller); industrial location; global supply chains
- **Cultural Geography**: Cultural landscapes; diffusion; place and identity
- **Political Geography**: Nation-states, borders, geopolitics, territoriality; gerrymandering
- **Development Geography**: Global inequality; core-periphery models; development indicators (HDI, GNI)

### GIS & Spatial Analysis
- **Geographic Information Systems (GIS)**: Spatial data capture, analysis, visualization
- **Remote Sensing**: Satellite imagery; LiDAR; aerial photography
- **Cartography**: Map design; projections (Mercator, Peters); thematic mapping
- **Spatial Statistics**: Spatial autocorrelation; hot spot analysis; geostatistics
- **GPS & Navigation**: Global Positioning System; geolocation

### Environmental Geography
- **Human-Environment Interaction**: Resource use, land cover change, sustainability
- **Natural Hazards**: Risk assessment, vulnerability, disaster management
- **Climate Change**: Impacts on ecosystems, agriculture, human settlements
- **Conservation Geography**: Protected areas, biodiversity corridors, ecosystem services

## Key Theories

| Theory | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|
| Environmental Determinism | Physical environment shapes human culture and behavior |
| Possibilism | Environment offers possibilities; humans choose (Blache) |
| Location Theory | Economic activity follows spatial patterns (Weber, Von Thünen) |
| Central Place Theory | Settlements form hierarchical patterns (Christaller) |
| World Systems Theory | Global economic hierarchy; core, periphery, semi-periphery |
| Critical Geography | Space is socially produced; power shapes landscapes (Harvey, Soja) |

## Important Figures

- **Alexander von Humboldt**: Father of modern geography; biogeography
- **Carl Ritter**: Comparative geography
- **Alfred Wegener**: Continental drift
- **Walter Christaller**: Central place theory
- **David Harvey**: Critical geography; social justice and the city
- **Yi-Fu Tuan**: Humanistic geography; sense of place
- **Doreen Massey**: Relational space; power geometry

## Applications

- **Urban Planning**: Land use, transportation, zoning
- **Environmental Management**: Conservation, resource management, climate adaptation
- **Disaster Risk Reduction**: Hazard mapping, early warning systems
- **Business & Marketing**: Location analysis, market segmentation, logistics
- **Public Health**: Disease mapping, health service accessibility
- **National Security**: Intelligence analysis, border management
