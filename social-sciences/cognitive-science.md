# Cognitive Science

## Overview

Cognitive science is the interdisciplinary study of the mind and its processes — how we perceive, think, learn, remember, speak, and solve problems. It draws on psychology, neuroscience, computer science, linguistics, philosophy, and anthropology to build unified theories of cognition. It is the field most explicitly dedicated to understanding intelligence itself.

## Core Concepts

### Approaches to Cognition
- **Computationalism**: Mind as information processing system; thinking as computation
- **Connectionism**: Cognitive processes as emergent properties of neural networks; parallel distributed processing
- **Embodied Cognition**: Mind shaped by body and environment; cognition is not just brain-bound
- **Enactivism**: Cognition arises through dynamic interaction between organism and environment
- **Predictive Processing**: Brain as prediction machine; perception as controlled hallucination

### Perception & Attention
- **Visual Perception**: Edge detection, object recognition, face processing; bottom-up vs top-down
- **Auditory Perception**: Speech perception, music processing, auditory scene analysis
- **Cross-Modal Integration**: How the brain combines information from multiple senses (McGurk effect)
- **Attention Mechanisms**: Selective attention (Broadbent, Treisman); inattentional blindness; change blindness
- **Perceptual Illusions**: What they reveal about processing assumptions the brain makes

### Memory & Learning
- **Working Memory**: Baddeley's model (phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive, episodic buffer)
- **Long-Term Memory**: Declarative (episodic, semantic) vs non-declarative (procedural, priming, conditioning)
- **Encoding & Retrieval**: Levels of processing; encoding specificity; retrieval practice effect
- **Forgetting**: Decay, interference (proactive, retroactive); forgetting curves
- **Skill Acquisition**: Power law of practice; automaticity; expertise development

### Language & Communication
- **Language Acquisition**: Nativist (Chomsky's universal grammar) vs usage-based (Tomasello) accounts
- **Speech Production & Comprehension**: From thought to articulation; parsing, garden-path sentences
- **Psycholinguistics**: Word recognition, sentence processing, discourse comprehension
- **Bilingualism**: Code-switching; cognitive advantages; bilingual memory
- **Pragmatics**: Context-dependent meaning; implicature; speech acts

### Reasoning & Decision-Making
- **Deductive Reasoning**: Syllogisms, conditional reasoning; mental models theory (Johnson-Laird)
- **Inductive Reasoning**: Generalization from instances; category learning
- **Heuristics & Biases**: Availability, representativeness, anchoring (Kahneman & Tversky)
- **Prospect Theory**: Loss aversion; reference-dependent preferences
- **Bounded Rationality**: Satisficing; ecological rationality (Gigerenzer)

### Problem Solving & Creativity
- **Problem Space Theory**: Initial state, goal state, operators (Newell & Simon)
- **Insight**: Restructuring; "aha" moments; incubation effects
- **Analogical Reasoning**: Structure mapping; using known domains to understand new ones
- **Expertise**: Chunking; pattern recognition; deliberate practice (Ericsson)
- **Creativity**: Divergent thinking; combinatorial search; constraints and freedom

### Concepts & Categorization
- **Classical View**: Necessary and sufficient conditions; prototype theory (Rosch)
- **Exemplar Theory**: Categories represented by stored instances
- **Theory Theory**: Concepts as mini-theories; causal knowledge shapes categorization
- **Conceptual Combination**: How basic concepts combine to form complex ideas
- **Semantic Networks**: Spreading activation; hierarchical organization

### Cognitive Architecture
- **ACT-R**: Adaptive Control of Thought — Rational; modular, production-rule system (Anderson)
- **SOAR**: State, Operator And Result; general problem-solving architecture (Laird, Newell, Rosenbloom)
- **Global Workspace Theory**: Consciousness as global broadcast across specialized modules (Baars)
- **Dual-Process Theory**: System 1 (fast, intuitive) vs System 2 (slow, deliberative) (Kahneman)

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Computational Theory of Mind | Turing, Fodor | Mental processes are computations over representations |
| Connectionism | Rumelhart, McClelland | Knowledge stored in connection weights between simple units |
| Embodied Cognition | Varela, Thompson | Cognition depends on having a body embedded in an environment |
| Dual-Process Theory | Kahneman | Two systems: fast/intuitive and slow/deliberate |
| Predictive Processing | Clark, Friston | Brain minimizes prediction error; perception is inference |

## Important Figures

- **Noam Chomsky**: Universal grammar; revolutionized linguistics and cognitive science
- **Allen Newell & Herbert Simon**: Symbolic AI; problem-solving; physical symbol system hypothesis
- **Daniel Kahneman**: Heuristics and biases; prospect theory; dual-process theory
- **David Marr**: Three levels of analysis (computational, algorithmic, implementational)
- **Douglas Hofstadter**: Strange loops; consciousness and self-reference
- **Patricia Churchland**: Neurophilosophy; eliminative materialism
- **Elizabeth Spelke**: Core knowledge in infant cognition

## Frontiers

- **Artificial General Intelligence**: Can we build systems with human-level cognitive flexibility? What would that require?
- **Consciousness**: What are the neural and computational correlates of subjective experience? Can machines be conscious?
- **Cognitive Development in AI**: How do current AI systems (LLMs) acquire and represent knowledge compared to humans?
- **Collective Cognition**: How do groups, institutions, and cultures process information and make decisions?
- **Cognitive Enhancement**: Pharmacological, technological, and educational methods to improve human cognition — ethical and practical limits

## Applications

- **Artificial Intelligence**: Cognitive architectures inspire AI design; human-AI interaction
- **Education**: Learning science; instructional design; cognitive load theory
- **User Experience**: Interface design based on perception, attention, and memory constraints
- **Clinical Psychology**: Cognitive assessments; understanding cognitive deficits
- **Law**: Eyewitness testimony; decision-making under uncertainty; cognitive biases in jurors
- **Human Factors**: Aviation, medical, and industrial safety; workload management

## Classic Works

- **"The Computer and the Brain"** by John von Neumann — Early comparison of computing machines and biological neural computation
- **"Gödel, Escher, Bach"** by Douglas Hofstadter — Pulitzer-winning exploration of cognition, self-reference, and consciousness through logic, art, and music
- **"Cognitive Science: An Introduction to the Study of Mind"** by Jay Friedenberg & Gordon Silverman — Comprehensive textbook covering all major subfields
- **"Thinking, Fast and Slow"** by Daniel Kahneman — Accessible overview of dual-process theory and cognitive biases from the field's most influential researcher
