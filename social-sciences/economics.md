# Economics

## Overview

Economics is the social science that studies how individuals, businesses, governments, and societies make choices about allocating scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants. It is divided into microeconomics (individual decisions) and macroeconomics (economy-wide phenomena).

## Core Concepts

### Microeconomics
- **Supply & Demand**: Market equilibrium; price determination; shifts vs movements
- **Elasticity**: Price, income, cross elasticity; responsiveness of quantity to price changes
- **Consumer Theory**: Utility maximization; indifference curves; budget constraints
- **Producer Theory**: Profit maximization; production functions; cost curves (fixed, variable, marginal)
- **Market Structures**: Perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition
- **Market Failures**: Externalities (positive/negative), public goods, information asymmetry, moral hazard
- **Game Theory**: Nash equilibrium; prisoner's dilemma; dominant strategies

### Macroeconomics
- **GDP**: Gross Domestic Product; nominal vs real; expenditure and income approaches
- **Inflation & Deflation**: CPI, PPI; causes (demand-pull, cost-push); hyperinflation
- **Unemployment**: Types (frictional, structural, cyclical); natural rate; Phillips curve
- **Business Cycles**: Expansion, peak, contraction, trough; recessions and depressions
- **Monetary Policy**: Central banks (Fed, ECB, PBOC); interest rates; money supply; quantitative easing
- **Fiscal Policy**: Government spending and taxation; multiplier effect; deficit and debt
- **International Trade**: Comparative advantage (Ricardo); tariffs, quotas; trade agreements; balance of payments
- **Exchange Rates**: Fixed vs floating; purchasing power parity; currency markets

### Economic Systems
- **Capitalism**: Private ownership, free markets, profit motive
- **Socialism**: Collective ownership, central planning
- **Mixed Economies**: Combination of market and government intervention
- **Command Economy**: Government controls production and distribution

### Development Economics
- **Economic Growth**: Solow model; technology, capital, labor inputs
- **Human Development Index**: Beyond GDP; health, education, income
- **Inequality**: Gini coefficient; Lorenz curve; poverty traps
- **Institutions**: Property rights, rule of law, governance quality

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Classical Economics | Adam Smith | Invisible hand; laissez-faire |
| Keynesian Economics | John Maynard Keynes | Government intervention to manage demand |
| Monetarism | Milton Friedman | Money supply drives inflation and output |
| Austrian Economics | Hayek, Mises | Spontaneous order; knowledge problem |
| Behavioral Economics | Kahneman, Thaler | Psychological biases in economic decisions |
| Marxist Economics | Karl Marx | Labor theory of value; class conflict |
| New Institutional | Douglass North | Institutions shape economic performance |

- **Adam Smith**: Wealth of Nations; father of modern economics
- **John Maynard Keynes**: General Theory; Keynesian revolution

- **Paul Samuelson**: Mathematical economics; Nobel laureate
- **Amartya Sen**: Welfare economics; capability approach

- **Thomas Piketty**: Capital in the Twenty-First Century; inequality

## Frontiers

- **AI and Labor Markets**: How artificial intelligence and automation reshape employment, productivity, wage distribution, and the nature of work
- **Climate Economics**: Modeling the economic costs of climate change; optimal carbon pricing; green transition pathways and stranded assets
- **Digital Currencies and CBDCs**: How cryptocurrencies and central bank digital currencies may transform monetary policy, financial stability, and inclusion
- **Behavioral Interventions at Scale**: Leveraging nudges and randomized controlled trials in public policy design; scaling evidence-based interventions
- **Secular Stagnation**: Whether advanced economies face persistently low growth, low interest rates, and weak aggregate demand

## Applications

- **Public Policy**: Tax design, healthcare, education funding, climate policy
- **Central Banking**: Interest rate decisions, inflation targeting
- **International Organizations**: IMF, World Bank, WTO
- **Business Strategy**: Market analysis, pricing, competitive dynamics
- **Personal Finance**: Saving, investing, consumption decisions

## Classic Works

- **"The Wealth of Nations"** by Adam Smith — Foundational text of modern economics; introduced the invisible hand and market self-regulation
- **"The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money"** by John Maynard Keynes — Revolutionized macroeconomics; argued for government intervention during downturns
- **"Capital in the Twenty-First Century"** by Thomas Piketty — Data-driven analysis of wealth inequality across centuries; reignited global debate on redistribution
- **"Thinking, Fast and Slow"** by Daniel Kahneman — Accessible overview of cognitive biases and dual-process theory in economic decision-making
- **"The Theory of Moral Sentiments"** by Adam Smith — Often overlooked companion to Wealth of Nations; explores moral and psychological foundations of economic behavior
