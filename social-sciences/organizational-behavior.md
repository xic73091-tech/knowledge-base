# Organizational Behavior

## Overview

Organizational behavior (OB) is the systematic study of how individuals and groups behave in organizations. It examines the impact of organizational structure, culture, leadership, and work design on individual attitudes, motivation, and performance. Understanding organizational behavior helps managers and leaders improve effectiveness, create positive work environments, enhance employee satisfaction, and achieve organizational goals. It bridges psychology, sociology, anthropology, and management to explain and predict human behavior in workplace settings.

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Hierarchy of Needs | Abraham Maslow | Motivation stems from satisfying increasingly higher-level needs |
| Two-Factor Theory | Frederick Herzberg | Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction |
| Expectancy Theory | Victor Vroom | Motivation depends on expectancy, instrumentality, and valence |
| Theory X and Theory Y | Douglas McGregor | Managerial assumptions about human nature affect management style |
| Situational Leadership | Hersey & Blanchard | Effective leadership style depends on follower readiness |
| Organizational Culture | Edgar Schein | Three levels: artifacts, espoused values, basic assumptions |
