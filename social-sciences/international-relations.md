# International Relations

## Overview

International relations (IR) is the study of interactions between sovereign states, international organizations, non-governmental actors, and transnational forces. It examines war and peace, trade and cooperation, diplomacy and law, and the structures of power that shape global politics. IR is both an academic discipline and a practical guide to foreign policy and global governance.

## Core Concepts

### The International System
- **Sovereignty**: Westphalian system; states as primary actors; non-interference principle
- **Anarchy**: No world government; self-help system; security dilemma
- **Balance of Power**: States coalition to prevent any single state from dominating
- **Polarity**: Unipolar (hegemon), bipolar (Cold War), multipolar (pre-WWII); implications for stability
- **Power**: Hard (military, economic), soft (culture, diplomacy), smart (combination), structural (shaping rules)

### Theoretical Traditions
- **Realism**: States seek power and security; national interest; offensive (Mearsheimer) vs defensive (Waltz) realism
- **Liberalism**: Cooperation through institutions, trade, and democracy; democratic peace theory; interdependence
- **Constructivism**: Ideas, norms, and identities shape international politics (Wendt); "anarchy is what states make of it"
- **English School**: International society; states share norms and rules (Bull, Wight)
- **Marxism/Dependency Theory**: Global capitalism creates core-periphery exploitation
- **Feminist IR**: Gendered nature of war, diplomacy, and security; women's experiences in conflict
- **Postcolonialism**: How colonial legacies shape contemporary global politics and knowledge production

### Conflict & Security
- **Causes of War**: Security dilemma, territorial disputes, resource competition, ideology, domestic politics
- **Nuclear Strategy**: Deterrence, mutually assured destruction (MAD), proliferation, arms control
- **Civil Wars**: Ethnic conflict, state failure, insurgency, intervention
- **Terrorism**: Definitions; state vs non-state; motivations; counterterrorism strategies
- **Humanitarian Intervention**: R2P (Responsibility to Protect); sovereignty vs human rights; Kosovo, Libya, Rwanda
- **Cyber Warfare**: State-sponsored attacks; critical infrastructure; attribution challenges

### International Organizations
- **United Nations**: Security Council, General Assembly, ICJ, peacekeeping, reform debates
- **World Trade Organization**: Trade liberalization, dispute settlement, Doha round failures
- **International Monetary Fund / World Bank**: Global financial governance, conditionality, development lending
- **Regional Organizations**: EU (supranational integration), AU, ASEAN, NATO (collective defense)
- **Non-Governmental Organizations**: Amnesty International, Médecins Sans Frontières, Greenpeace; advocacy and service delivery

### International Political Economy
- **Trade Theory**: Comparative advantage (Ricardo); Heckscher-Ohlin; free trade vs protectionism
- **Globalization**: Economic integration; supply chains; capital flows; backlash movements
- **Development**: Modernization theory, dependency theory, world-systems theory; sustainable development goals
- **Sanctions**: Economic coercion; effectiveness; humanitarian costs
- **Global Governance**: Regimes for trade, finance, environment, health; legitimacy and enforcement

### International Law
- **Sources**: Treaties, custom, general principles, judicial decisions, scholarship
- **Sovereign Immunity**: States generally immune from foreign courts
- **Human Rights Law**: UDHR, ICCPR, ICESCR; treaty bodies; universalism vs cultural relativism
- **International Criminal Law**: ICC, ad hoc tribunals; war crimes, genocide, crimes against humanity
- **Law of the Sea**: UNCLOS; territorial waters, EEZ, high seas, deep seabed
- **Law of Armed Conflict**: Geneva Conventions; proportionality, distinction, military necessity

### Foreign Policy Analysis
- **Decision-Making Models**: Rational actor, bureaucratic politics (Allison), groupthink (Janis)
- **Diplomacy**: Negotiation, summitry, public diplomacy, track-two diplomacy
- **Intelligence**: Collection, analysis, covert action; failures and reforms
- **Domestic Sources**: Public opinion, interest groups, political institutions, leader psychology

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Structural Realism | Kenneth Waltz | International anarchy shapes state behavior |
| Democratic Peace | Kant, Russett | Democracies rarely go to war with each other |
| Constructivism | Alexander Wendt | Ideas and identities, not just material power, shape politics |
| Complex Interdependence | Keohane & Nye | Multiple channels of contact; military force less central |
| World Systems Theory | Immanuel Wallerstein | Global capitalism creates core, periphery, semi-periphery |

## Important Figures

- **Thucydides**: History of the Peloponnesian War; realism's intellectual ancestor
- **Hugo Grotius**: Father of international law; freedom of the seas
- **Immanuel Kant**: Perpetual peace; democratic peace theory
- **Hans Morgenthau**: Classical realism; Politics Among Nations
- **Kenneth Waltz**: Neorealism; Theory of International Politics
- **Alexander Wendt**: Social Theory of International Politics; constructivism
- **Hedley Bull**: The Anarchical Society; English School

## Frontiers

- **Multipolarity and Great Power Competition**: How does the rise of China and the relative decline of US hegemony reshape the international order?
- **Climate Governance**: Can international cooperation keep warming below 1.5°C? Who bears the costs?
- **AI and Autonomous Weapons**: Lethal autonomous weapons systems; arms race dynamics; international regulation
- **Global Digital Governance**: Data sovereignty, platform regulation, cyber norms, internet fragmentation
- **Migration and Borders**: Climate refugees, economic migration; sovereignty vs human mobility rights

## Applications

- **Diplomacy**: Treaty negotiation, crisis management, multilateral cooperation
- **Peacekeeping & Peacebuilding**: UN missions, post-conflict reconstruction, transitional justice
- **Trade Policy**: Trade agreements, WTO disputes, economic statecraft
- **Development**: Foreign aid, capacity building, sustainable development
- **Intelligence & Security**: Threat assessment, counterterrorism, cybersecurity
- **Human Rights**: Monitoring, advocacy, accountability mechanisms

## Classic Works

- **"The Peloponnesian War"** by Thucydides — The foundational text of realist thought; analysis of power, fear, and honor in interstate conflict
- **"The Anarchical Society"** by Hedley Bull — Definitive statement of the English School; argues international society exists despite anarchy
- **"Theory of International Politics"** by Kenneth Waltz — Structural realism; how the distribution of power shapes state behavior
- **"Social Theory of International Politics"** by Alexander Wendt — Foundational constructivist work; how ideas and identities constitute the international system
- **"The Tragedy of Great Power Politics"** by John Mearsheimer — Offensive realism; why great powers compete for hegemony
