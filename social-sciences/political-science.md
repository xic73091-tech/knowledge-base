# Political Science

## Overview

Political science is the systematic study of governance, political activities, political thoughts, and political behavior. It deals with the theory and practice of politics at every level — from local to international — and examines power, authority, justice, and the state.

## Core Concepts

### Political Theory
- **Power & Authority**: Weber's types (traditional, charismatic, rational-legal); sovereignty
- **Justice**: Rawls' theory of justice; utilitarian approaches; libertarianism
- **Democracy**: Direct vs representative; liberal democracy; deliberative democracy
- **Political Ideologies**: Liberalism, conservatism, socialism, feminism, environmentalism, populism
- **Social Contract**: Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau; legitimacy of government
- **Liberty**: Positive vs negative liberty; rights and responsibilities

### Comparative Politics
- **Regime Types**: Democracies, authoritarian regimes, hybrid regimes
- **Electoral Systems**: Proportional representation, first-past-the-post, mixed systems
- **Party Systems**: Two-party, multi-party, dominant-party, non-partisan
- **Federalism vs Unitarism**: Division of power between central and regional governments
- **Political Culture**: Civic values, political socialization, trust in institutions
- **State Capacity**: Ability to implement policy, collect taxes, maintain order

### International Relations
- **Realism**: States as primary actors; power politics; national interest
- **Liberalism**: International institutions; cooperation; democratic peace theory
- **Constructivism**: Ideas, norms, and identities shape international politics
- **World Systems Theory**: Core, periphery, semi-periphery (Wallerstein)
- **International Organizations**: UN, NATO, EU, ASEAN, AU
- **Conflict & Security**: War theories, deterrence, arms control, terrorism
- **Globalization**: Economic integration, cultural diffusion, sovereignty challenges

### Public Policy & Administration
- **Policy Process**: Agenda setting → formulation → adoption → implementation → evaluation
- **Public Administration**: Bureaucracy, new public management, governance networks
- **Policy Analysis**: Cost-benefit analysis, stakeholder analysis, program evaluation
- **Regulatory Policy**: Environmental regulation, financial regulation, consumer protection

### Political Behavior
- **Voting Behavior**: Rational choice, party identification, issue voting
- **Public Opinion**: Polling, framing, agenda-setting
- **Political Participation**: Voting, protest, civic engagement, social movements
- **Media & Politics**: Agenda setting, framing, propaganda, fake news

## Key Theories

| Theory | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|
| Social Contract | Government legitimacy from consent of governed |
| Separation of Powers | Executive, legislative, judicial checks (Montesquieu) |
| Realism (IR) | International system is anarchic; states pursue power |
| Liberalism (IR) | Institutions and cooperation can mitigate anarchy |
| Pluralism | Power distributed among competing interest groups |
| Elite Theory | Power concentrated in elite minority (Pareto, Mosca) |

## Important Figures

- **Aristotle**: Classification of governments; political philosophy
- **Niccolò Machiavelli**: The Prince; realpolitik
- **Thomas Hobbes**: Leviathan; social contract
- **John Locke**: Natural rights; limited government; consent
- **Montesquieu**: Separation of powers
- **Max Weber**: Bureaucracy; types of authority; state as monopoly on legitimate violence
- **Hannah Arendt**: Totalitarianism; the human condition
- **John Rawls**: Theory of justice; veil of ignorance

## Applications

- **Government**: Legislative design, executive leadership, judicial review
- **Diplomacy**: Treaty negotiation, conflict resolution, alliance building
- **Political Consulting**: Campaign strategy, polling, media management
- **NGOs & Advocacy**: Policy advocacy, human rights, development work
- **Journalism**: Political reporting, investigative journalism
