# International Development

## Overview

International development is the study and practice of improving the political, economic, and social well-being of people in developing countries. It encompasses a wide range of approaches, theories, and interventions aimed at reducing poverty, promoting sustainable growth, improving health and education outcomes, and enhancing human capabilities. The field draws on economics, political science, sociology, anthropology, and public policy to understand the complex factors that shape development trajectories and to design effective interventions.

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Modernization Theory | Rostow | Countries progress through predictable stages toward economic maturity |
| Capabilities Approach | Amartya Sen | Development as expansion of capabilities and freedoms, not just income |
| Dependency Theory | Frank; Amin | Peripheral economies subordinated to and shaped by core |
| Endogenous Growth | Romer; Lucas | Growth driven by internal factors like technology and human capital |
| Institutional Theory | Acemoglu; North | Institutions shape development trajectories; extractive vs inclusive |
| Sustainable Development | Brundtland Commission | Meeting present needs without compromising future generations |
