# Behavioral Economics

## Overview

Behavioral economics integrates insights from psychology and economics to understand how people actually make decisions, often deviating from the "rational" model assumed by classical economics. It examines the cognitive biases, emotional influences, and social factors that shape economic choices. Behavioral economics has profound implications for policy-making, marketing, finance, and personal decision-making, providing a more realistic understanding of human behavior in economic contexts.

## Core Concepts

### Foundations of Behavioral Economics
- **Rational Choice Theory (vs Behavioral)**: Classical economics assumes perfect rationality; behavioral economics challenges this
- **Bounded Rationality**: Herbert Simon's concept; limits on human cognitive capacity
- **Prospect Theory**: Value function; loss aversion; reference points (Kahneman & Tversky)
- **Behavioral Welfare Economics**: Measuring well-being beyond rational preferences
- **Behavioral Game Theory**: Combining game theory with psychological insights
- **Neuroeconomics**: Brain mechanisms underlying economic decisions

### Cognitive Biases & Heuristics
- **Confirmation Bias**: Seeking information that confirms existing beliefs; ignoring contradictory evidence
- **Availability Heuristic**: Judging probability by ease of recall; recency effect
- **Representativeness Heuristic**: Assessing probability by resemblance to typical case; base rate neglect
- **Anchoring**: Relying heavily on initial information; numerical anchors
- **Hindsight Bias**: "Knew-it-all-along" effect; overestimating predictability after the fact
- **Overconfidence Bias**: Overestimating one's own abilities; confidence intervals too narrow
- **Optimism Bias**: Underestimating risks; overestimating positive outcomes
- **Status Quo Bias**: Preferring current state; resistance to change

### Loss Aversion & Risk Behavior
- **Loss Aversion**: Losses loom larger than gains; pain of losing > pleasure of gaining (2:1 ratio)
- **Endowment Effect**: Valuing owned items more highly; mere ownership increases value
- **Disposition Effect**: Selling winners too early; holding losers too long
- **House Money Effect**: Increased risk-taking with "found" money
- **Break-Even Effect**: Taking risks to avoid losses when close to breaking even
- **Zero-Risk Bias**: Preferring to eliminate one risk even at expense of other risks
- **Myopic Loss Aversion**: Frequent evaluation increases risk aversion

### Time Discounting & Intertemporal Choice
- **Hyperbolic Discounting**: Present bias; preferring smaller immediate rewards over larger later ones
- **Temporal Discounting**: How value of rewards decreases over time
- **Present Bias**: Overweighting immediate rewards; procrastination
- **Procrastination & Self-Control**: Future self-continuity; commitment devices
- **DSM Model**: Dual-self model (Planner vs Doer); Ulysses contracts
- **Life-Cycle Saving**: Intertemporal consumption smoothing; retirement planning
- **Addiction & Instant Gratification**: Immediate rewards; delayed negative consequences

### Social Preferences & Fairness
- **Ultimatum Game**: Offers rejected if perceived as unfair; sense of fairness
- **Dictator Game**: Giving without expectation of reciprocity; altruism
- **Public Goods Game**: Contribution incentives; free-rider problem
- **Trust Game**: Reciprocal trust; return on trust; cultural differences
- **Social Norms**: Conditional cooperation; following perceived norms
- **Reciprocity**: Positive (reciprocating favors) and negative (reciprocating harm)
- **Inequity Aversion**: Discomfort with unequal outcomes

### Mental Accounting
- **Mental Accounting Theory**: Richard Thaler's concept; categorizing money differently
- **Narrow Framing**: Evaluating decisions in isolation rather than holistically
- **Household Accounting**: Managing finances by mental "accounts"
- **Sunk Cost Fallacy**: Continuing behavior due to prior investment rather than future value
- **Payment Decoupling**: Mental separation of payment from consumption
- **Budgeting Perversions**: Overspending in certain categories due to mental accounting

### Choice Architecture & Nudges
- **Choice Architecture**: How choices are presented; defaults; options; information
- **Libertarian Paternalism**: Nudges that preserve freedom while guiding toward better choices
- **Default Effects**: Strong tendency to stick with pre-set options
- **Choice Overload**: Paradox of choice; decision fatigue; too many options
- **Decoy Effect**: Adding inferior option to make target option more attractive
- **Framing Effects**: Different descriptions of same choice lead to different decisions
- **Simplification & Chunking**: Reducing cognitive load; clear communication

### Nudge Theory & Applications
- **Thaler & Sunstein's Nudge**: Choice architecture; nudge unit; libertarian paternalism
- **Nudge Units in Government**: UK's BIT; US OIRA; applying behavioral insights to policy
- **Save More Tomorrow**: Default enrollment in retirement savings; commitment device
- **Opt-Out vs Opt-In**: Organ donation; pension enrollment; defaults matter enormously
- **Smart Disclosure**: Providing information in usable formats; simplifying choices
- **Social Norms Interventions**: Descriptive vs injunctive norms; reducing energy use
- **Gamification**: Points; badges; leaderboards; applying game mechanics to behavior

### Behavioral Factors in Financial Decisions
- **Financial Literacy vs Behavior**: Knowing vs doing; action gaps in financial behavior
- **Excessive Trading**: Disposition effect; overconfidence; attention-seeking
- **Portfolio Underdiversification**: Home bias; mental accounting; reluctance to realize losses
- **Mental Retirement Accounting**: Inadequate retirement preparation; optimism bias
- **Debt Behavior**: Immediate gratification; hyperbolic discounting; credit card usage
- **Herding Behavior**: Following others; market bubbles and crashes; social proof
- **Mental Model of Risk**: Misunderstanding probabilities; unlikely events; tail risks

### Behavioral Marketing & Consumer Behavior
- **Price Psychology**: Charm pricing; 9-ending prices; bundling; anchoring
- **Promotion & Discounts**: Reference prices; percentage vs dollar; scarcity effects
- **Decoy Pricing**: Strategic pricing to guide choices; asymmetric domination
- **Free Offers**: Zero-price effect; freemium models; loss aversion
- **Anchoring in Marketing**: High-anchor prices; MSRP; suggested retail prices
- **Scarcity & Urgency**: Limited availability; countdown timers; FOMO
- **Social Proof**: Testimonials; reviews; user counts; influencer marketing

### Policy & Public Sector Applications
- **Pension Enrollment**: Auto-enrollment; Save More Tomorrow; increasing savings rates
- **Energy Conservation**: Social norm feedback; smart meters; default green energy
- **Health Behavior**: Organ donation; vaccination uptake; healthy eating nudges
- **Tax Compliance**: Simplification; default payment; reminder nudges
- **Environmental Policy**: Green defaults; carbon footprint labels; commitment devices
- **Education Policy**: Early childhood interventions; summer learning loss; feedback nudges
- **Criminal Justice**: Reducing recidivism; cognitive behavioral interventions

### Organizational Behavior & Management
- **Performance Incentives**: Overjustification effect; crowding out intrinsic motivation
- **Goal Setting**: Stretch goals; SMART goals; feedback and commitment
- **Bonus vs Salary**: Non-linear incentives; lottery-like bonuses; fixed vs variable pay
- **Recognition Programs**: Social recognition; non-monetary rewards; sustainability
- **Feedback & Performance Reviews**: Timing; framing; delivery; cognitive biases
- **Decision-Making in Organizations**: Groupthink; information cascades; silos

### Judgment Under Uncertainty
- **Base Rate Neglect**: Ignoring statistical base rates; focusing on specific information
- **Conjunction Fallacy**: Thinking conjunction is more likely than single event
- **Gambler's Fallacy**: Expecting reversal after streak; law of small numbers
- **Hot Hand Fallacy**: Expecting continued streak; misperception of random sequences
- **Clustering Illusion**: Seeing patterns in random data; overinterpreting noise
- **Regression to the Mean**: Overreacting to extreme performance; natural variation
- **Survivorship Bias**: Focusing on successes; ignoring failures; selection bias

### Emotional Factors in Decisions
- **Affect Heuristic**: Using emotions as mental shortcut; mood influencing risk perception
- **Fear & Risk Perception**: Availability and vividness; affect-as-information
- **Anxiety & Risk Aversion**: Worry about negative outcomes; precautionary behavior
- **Anticipated Regret**: Expected pain of missed opportunities; maximizing minimum
- **Somatic Markers**: Body's response to options; gut feelings in decision-making
- **Mood & Decision-Making**: Positive mood broadening; negative mood narrowing
- **Stress & Decision-Making**: Cognitive load; prefrontal cortex function; dual-process

### Behavioral Economics in Personal Finance
- **Savings Behavior**: Auto-escalation; commitment devices; mental accounting for savings
- **Debt Management**: Snowball vs avalanche; credit card psychology; fee avoidance
- **Investment Behavior**: Index fund bias; rebalancing; avoiding behavioral timing errors
- **Budgeting & Spending**: Tracking apps; pre-commitment; spending feedback
- **Retirement Planning**: Auto-enrollment; catch-up contributions; longevity insurance
- **Insurance Decisions**: Probability neglect; dread effects; catastrophic coverage
- **Fraud & Scams**: Overconfidence; authority bias; urgency tactics; vulnerability

### Experimental Methods
- **Lab Experiments**: Controlled environment; controlled incentives; causal inference
- **Field Experiments**: Real-world settings; natural variation; external validity
- **Natural Experiments**: Observing real behavior without intervention; quasi-experiments
- **Revealed vs Stated Preferences**: What people do vs what they say; hypothetical bias
- **Neuroimaging**: fMRI; EEG; studying brain activity during economic decisions
- **Big Data & Behavioral Insights**: Digital traces; passive data; large-scale behavioral patterns

### Behavioral Economics in Technology
- **Persuasive Technology**: Fogg's behavior model; Captology; digital nudges
- **Attention Economics**: Capturing and monetizing attention; engagement optimization
- **Algorithmic Persuasion**: Recommendation systems; filter bubbles; personalization
- **Gamification Design**: Points; badges; leaderboards; meaningful gamification
- **Dark Patterns**: Manipulative design; unethical use of behavioral insights
- **Digital Choice Architecture**: Defaults in apps; notification nudges; defaults matter
- **Social Media & Behavior**: FOMO; social comparison; dopamine loops; addiction

### Ethics in Behavioral Economics
- **Manipulation vs Persuasion**: Ethical boundaries; informed consent; autonomy
- **Transparency**: Disclosure of nudges; algorithmic transparency; explainability
- **Paternalism Concerns**: Whose preferences?; soft paternalism vs manipulation
- **Equity & Fairness**: Differential impacts; vulnerable populations; equity considerations
- **Evidence Standards**: When is evidence sufficient to justify interventions?
- **Sludge**: Mild friction designed to benefit organization at expense of users
- **Regulation of Dark Patterns**: Consumer protection; behavioral law and economics

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Prospect Theory | Kahneman & Tversky | People evaluate losses and gains asymmetrically; loss aversion; S-curve value function |
| Mental Accounting | Richard Thaler | People mentally categorize and treat money differently based on context |
| Nudge Theory | Thaler & Sunstein | Choice architecture that guides behavior while preserving freedom |
| Dual-Process Theory | Kahneman (System 1/2) | Fast intuitive (System 1) vs slow deliberate (System 2) thinking |
| Behavioral Life Cycle | Sheena Iyengar | Psychological life cycle of consumption and saving |
| Ultimatum Game | Güth et al.; Henrich | Sense of fairness dominates pure economic self-interest |
