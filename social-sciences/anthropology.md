# Anthropology

## Overview

Anthropology is the holistic, comparative study of humans — our biology, culture, language, and societies across time and space. It is uniquely broad, encompassing everything from primate evolution to contemporary digital cultures, seeking to understand what it means to be human.

## Core Concepts

### Cultural Anthropology
- **Culture**: Learned, shared, symbolic systems of meaning; includes beliefs, values, norms, practices
- **Ethnography**: Immersive fieldwork; participant observation; thick description (Geertz)
- **Kinship & Family**: Descent systems (patrilineal, matrilineal, bilateral); marriage practices across cultures
- **Rituals & Symbolism**: Rites of passage (van Gennep); symbolic anthropology (Turner)
- **Economic Anthropology**: Reciprocity, redistribution, market exchange; gift economies (Mauss)
- **Political Anthropology**: Band, tribe, chiefdom, state; power and authority in non-Western contexts
- **Medical Anthropology**: Illness vs disease; cultural healing practices; social determinants of health
- **Applied Anthropology**: Using anthropological methods to solve real-world problems

### Biological/Physical Anthropology
- **Human Evolution**: Australopithecus → Homo habilis → Homo erectus → Homo sapiens
- **Primate Studies**: Great apes; behavior, cognition, social structure
- **Forensic Anthropology**: Skeletal analysis for legal purposes
- **Human Variation**: Adaptation to environments; race as social construct vs biological variation
- **Paleoanthropology**: Fossil evidence for human evolution; key sites (Olduvai, Lucy)

### Linguistic Anthropology
- **Language & Culture**: Sapir-Whorf hypothesis; linguistic relativity
- **Sociolinguistics**: Language variation by class, region, ethnicity, gender
- **Language Endangerment**: Language death; revitalization efforts
- **Discourse Analysis**: How language constructs social reality

### Archaeology (see also [Archaeology](../humanities/archaeology.md))
- **Material Culture**: Artifacts, ecofacts, features as evidence of past societies
- **Methods**: Excavation, survey, dating (radiocarbon, stratigraphy)
- **Settlement Patterns**: How past peoples organized space
- **Public Archaeology**: Community engagement, heritage management

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Cultural Relativism | Boas | Understand cultures on their own terms |
| Structuralism | Lévi-Strauss | Universal structures underlying all cultures |
| Interpretive Anthropology | Geertz | Culture as text to be read and interpreted |
| Practice Theory | Bourdieu | Culture reproduced through everyday practices |
| World Systems Theory | Wallerstein | Global economic system shapes local cultures |
| Postcolonialism | Said, Spivak | Critique of colonial knowledge and power |

## Important Figures

- **Franz Boas**: Father of American anthropology; cultural relativism
- **Margaret Mead**: Coming of Age in Samoa; culture and personality
- **Bronislaw Malinowski**: Participant observation; Trobriand Islanders
- **Claude Lévi-Strauss**: Structural anthropology; myth analysis
- **Clifford Geertz**: Interpretive anthropology; thick description
- **Ruth Benedict**: Patterns of Culture; configurational approach
- **Zora Neale Hurston**: African American folklore; ethnographic fiction

## Applications

- **UX Research**: Ethnographic methods in product design
- **International Development**: Community-based approaches; understanding local contexts
- **Healthcare**: Cultural competence; understanding illness behavior
- **Business**: Cross-cultural management; consumer anthropology
- **Museum & Heritage**: Curation, repatriation, cultural preservation
- **Forensics**: Skeletal identification, disaster victim identification
