# Sports Science

## Overview

Sports science (exercise science/kinesiology) is the study of how the human body responds to physical activity and exercise. It integrates physiology, biomechanics, psychology, nutrition, and coaching to optimize athletic performance, prevent injuries, and promote health through physical activity.

## Core Concepts

### Exercise Physiology
- **Energy Systems**: ATP-CP (phosphocreatine), glycolytic (anaerobic), oxidative (aerobic); energy system continuum
- **Cardiovascular Adaptations**: Cardiac output, stroke volume, VO2max, heart rate, blood pressure
- **Respiratory Adaptations**: Ventilation, gas exchange, oxygen delivery
- **Muscle Physiology**: Fiber types (Type I slow-twitch, Type II fast-twitch); hypertrophy; neuromuscular adaptations
- **Endocrine Response**: Hormones (testosterone, growth hormone, cortisol, insulin) and exercise
- **Thermoregulation**: Heat production, dissipation; heat illness; cold stress
- **Detraining & Tapering**: Reversibility principle; pre-competition taper

### Biomechanics
- **Kinematics**: Motion description; position, velocity, acceleration; gait analysis
- **Kinetics**: Forces; ground reaction forces; joint loads; Newton's laws applied
- **Linear & Angular Motion**: Projectile motion; torque; angular momentum
- **Fluid Mechanics**: Drag, lift in swimming, cycling; aerodynamics
- **Injury Mechanics**: ACL injury mechanisms; concussion biomechanics; stress fractures
- **Equipment Design**: Shoe biomechanics; racket design; aerodynamic helmets

### Training Principles
- **Specificity**: SAID principle; train for the specific demand
- **Progressive Overload**: Gradually increasing training stimulus
- **Variation**: Periodization; changing stimuli to prevent plateau
- **Recovery**: Rest, sleep, active recovery; supercompensation
- **Periodization**: Linear, undulating, block; mesocycles, microcycles, macrocycles
- **Strength Training**: Hypertrophy (6-12 reps), strength (1-5 reps), power, endurance (12+ reps)
- **Endurance Training**: Long slow distance, tempo, interval, HIIT; polarized training
- **Flexibility & Mobility**: Static stretching, dynamic stretching, PNF, foam rolling

### Sports Nutrition
- **Macronutrient Needs**: Carbohydrates for performance, protein for recovery (1.6-2.2 g/kg), fat for health
- **Hydration**: Sweat rate; electrolyte replacement; hyponatremia; pre/during/post exercise
- **Pre/Post-Workout Nutrition**: Timing; carbohydrate-protein combinations; glycogen replenishment
- **Ergogenic Aids**: Caffeine, creatine, beta-alanine, nitrate, sodium bicarbonate; evidence-based vs banned
- **Body Composition**: Assessment methods; weight management; making weight (combat sports)
- **Supplements**: Protein powder, BCAAs, multivitamins; quality control; WADA considerations

### Sports Psychology
- **Motivation**: Intrinsic vs extrinsic; self-determination theory; goal-setting (SMART)
- **Mental Toughness**: Resilience, focus, confidence, composure under pressure
- **Arousal & Anxiety**: Inverted-U hypothesis; individual zones of optimal functioning (IZOF)
- **Visualization & Imagery**: Mental rehearsal; performance imagery; process vs outcome
- **Team Dynamics**: Cohesion; leadership; communication; roles; team culture
- **Flow State**: Csikszentmihalyi; optimal experience; challenge-skill balance
- **Burnout & Overtraining**: Psychological and physical fatigue; recovery strategies

### Sports Medicine & Injury
- **Common Injuries**: ACL tears, ankle sprains, rotator cuff, hamstring strains, concussions, stress fractures
- **Injury Prevention**: Warm-up, neuromuscular training, load management, proper equipment
- **Concussion**: Signs, symptoms, sideline assessment (SCAT), return-to-play protocols
- **Rehabilitation**: RICE/POLICE; progressive loading; range of motion; functional exercises
- **Return to Sport**: Criteria-based progression; psychological readiness; re-injury risk
- **Anti-Doping**: WADA code; prohibited substances; testing; TUE (Therapeutic Use Exemption)

### Coaching & Performance Analysis
- **Coaching Styles**: Autocratic, democratic, holistic; transformational coaching
- **Motor Learning**: Skill acquisition; practice design; feedback (intrinsic, extrinsic); transfer
- **Performance Analysis**: Video analysis; notational analysis; GPS tracking; wearable technology
- **Talent Development**: Relative age effect; deliberate practice (Ericsson); early vs late specialization
- **Youth Athletics**: Long-term athlete development (LTAD); maturation; burnout prevention

### Special Populations
- **Youth Athletes**: Growth and maturation; overuse injuries; psychological development
- **Masters Athletes**: Aging and performance; training adaptations; injury prevention
- **Female Athletes**: Triad/RED-S; menstrual cycle effects; pregnancy and exercise
- **Paralympic Athletes**: Classification; adaptive equipment; training considerations
- **Clinical Populations**: Exercise prescription for diabetes, cardiovascular disease, mental health

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| General Adaptation Syndrome | Selye | Alarm → resistance → exhaustion; training stimulus response |
| Deliberate Practice | Ericsson | Expert performance from structured, effortful practice |
| Optimal Arousal | Yerkes-Dodson | Performance peaks at moderate arousal |
| Self-Determination Theory | Deci, Ryan | Autonomy, competence, relatedness drive motivation |

## Important Figures

- **Kenneth Cooper**: Aerobics concept; Cooper test
- **Hans Selye**: Stress and adaptation; General Adaptation Syndrome
- **Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi**: Flow state theory
- **Anders Ericsson**: Deliberate practice; expertise research
- **Tim Noakes**: Central governor theory; exercise physiology
