# Military Science

## Overview

Military science is the study of military strategy, tactics, operations, logistics, technology, and defense policy. It encompasses the theory and practice of organized armed conflict, from ancient warfare to modern hybrid and cyber threats. It draws on history, political science, engineering, psychology, and management.

## Core Concepts

### Strategy & Grand Strategy
- **Grand Strategy**: National objectives; integrating military, diplomatic, economic, informational power
- **Military Strategy**: Linking military operations to political objectives; center of gravity (Clausewitz)
- **Deterrence**: Nuclear deterrence; conventional deterrence; extended deterrence; MAD (Mutually Assured Destruction)
- **Asymmetric Warfare**: Insurgency, guerrilla warfare, terrorism; weaker party exploiting stronger's vulnerabilities
- **Hybrid Warfare**: Blending conventional, irregular, cyber, information warfare
- **Offense-Defense Theory**: Relative advantage of offensive vs defensive strategies

### Operational Art & Tactics
- **Operational Art**: Linking tactics to strategy; campaigns; phasing; culminating point
- **Maneuver Warfare**: Speed, surprise, concentration of force; mission-type orders (Auftragstaktik)
- **Attrition Warfare**: Wearing down enemy through superior resources
- **Combined Arms**: Integration of infantry, armor, artillery, air, engineers
- **Urban Warfare**: Military operations in cities; MOUT; civilian protection
- **Counterinsurgency (COIN)**: Population-centric vs enemy-centric; hearts and minds; clear-hold-build

### Military History (Key Periods)
- **Ancient Warfare**: Phalanx (Greek), legion (Roman), cavalry, siege warfare
- **Medieval Warfare**: Castles, knights, longbow, gunpowder introduction
- **Napoleonic Warfare**: Corps system; mass conscription; decisive battle
- **World War I**: Trench warfare; machine guns; chemical weapons; attrition
- **World War II**: Blitzkrieg; strategic bombing; amphibious operations; nuclear weapons
- **Cold War**: Proxy wars; nuclear deterrence; limited wars (Korea, Vietnam)
- **Post-Cold War**: Gulf War (precision warfare); peacekeeping; counterterrorism; hybrid warfare
- **Contemporary**: Russia-Ukraine conflict; cyber warfare; drone warfare; gray zone operations

### Weapons & Technology
- **Small Arms**: Rifles, pistols, machine guns; caliber, effective range
- **Armor & Vehicles**: Tanks, IFVs, APCs; active protection systems
- **Artillery**: Howitzers, MLRS, mortars; precision-guided munitions
- **Air Power**: Fighter jets, bombers, transport, helicopters, drones (UAVs); air superiority
- **Naval Power**: Aircraft carriers, destroyers, submarines, frigates; power projection
- **Nuclear Weapons**: Fission, fusion; delivery systems (ICBMs, SLBMs, bombers); arms control treaties
- **Cyber Warfare**: Offensive and defensive operations; critical infrastructure protection
- **Space Warfare**: Satellite protection; anti-satellite weapons; space-based ISR
- **Autonomous Systems**: Unmanned ground/aerial/maritime vehicles; AI in targeting; ethical concerns

### Intelligence
- **Intelligence Cycle**: Planning → Collection → Processing → Analysis → Dissemination
- **HUMINT**: Human intelligence; espionage; agents; debriefings
- **SIGINT**: Signals intelligence; communications intercept; electronic intelligence
- **IMINT**: Imagery intelligence; satellite, aerial reconnaissance
- **OSINT**: Open source intelligence; social media, news, public data
- **Counterintelligence**: Protecting against espionage; deception; double agents

### Logistics & sustainment
- **Military Logistics**: Supply chain; maintenance, transportation, medical support
- **Lines of Communication**: Supply routes; vulnerability to interdiction
- **Force Projection**: Deploying military power globally; sealift, airlift, prepositioning
- **Industrial Base**: Defense industry; arms production; mobilization capacity

### Defense Policy & Civil-Military Relations
- **Civilian Control of Military**: Democratic accountability; Chain of command
- **Defense Budget**: Procurement, personnel, operations, R&D; military-industrial complex
- **Alliance Systems**: NATO, bilateral alliances; burden-sharing; collective defense (Article 5)
- **Arms Control**: Nuclear treaties (NPT, New START); conventional arms control; export controls
- **Just War Theory**: Jus ad bellum (right to war), jus in bello (conduct in war); proportionality, discrimination
- **Peacekeeping**: UN missions; peace enforcement; stabilization operations

### Military Organization
- **Branches**: Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, Space Force
- **Ranks & Structure**: Enlisted, NCOs, officers; command structure; joint operations
- **Special Operations**: Special forces; unconventional warfare; direct action; counterterrorism
- **Reserves & National Guard**: Reserve components; mobilization; homeland defense

## Important Figures

- **Sun Tzu**: Art of War; strategic deception; ancient Chinese strategist
- **Carl von Clausewitz**: On War; friction; trinity of people, army, government
- **Napoleon Bonaparte**: Corps system; decisive battle; operational art
- **Helmuth von Moltke**: Mission-type orders; staff system; Prussian military reform
- **Dwight D. Eisenhower**: Supreme Allied Commander; D-Day; Cold War strategy
- **Vo Nguyen Giap**: Vietnamese insurgency; asymmetric warfare

## Frontiers

- **AI in Military Decision-Making**: Autonomous targeting, predictive logistics, and command support — where should humans remain in the loop?
- **Autonomous Weapons & Ethics**: Lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS); international debates on banning or regulating "killer robots"
- **Cyber Warfare Doctrine**: Establishing norms for state-sponsored cyber operations; attribution challenges; threshold for armed attack in cyberspace
- **Hypersonic Weapons & Arms Control**: Hypersonic glide vehicles and scramjet missiles destabilizing existing deterrence frameworks; new treaty challenges
- **Cognitive Warfare**: Targeting the mind through information operations, deepfakes, and social media manipulation; blurring war and peace

## Applications

- **Military Service**: Active duty, reserves, national guard
- **Defense Policy**: Strategic planning, budgeting, procurement
- **Intelligence Community**: Analysis, collection, operations
- **Defense Industry**: Weapons development, cybersecurity, aerospace
- **Diplomacy**: Arms control, alliance management, conflict resolution
- **Security Studies**: Academic research, think tanks, policy analysis
- **Humanitarian Assistance**: Disaster relief, refugee support, peacekeeping

## Classic Works

- **"On War"** by Carl von Clausewitz (1832) — Foundational text of Western military theory; war as a continuation of politics; friction; the trinity
- **"The Art of War"** by Sun Tzu (5th century BC) — Ancient Chinese treatise on strategy; deception, intelligence, and winning without fighting
- **"Strategy: The Indirect Approach"** by B. H. Liddell Hart (1954) — Argues that the most effective strategy avoids direct confrontation in favor of dislocation
- **"The Influence of Sea Power upon History"** by Alfred Thayer Mahan (1890) — Defined naval strategy and the link between sea control and national power
- **"Strategy in the Missile Age"** by Bernard Brodie (1959) — Seminal work on nuclear deterrence strategy and the revolution in warfare brought by atomic weapons
