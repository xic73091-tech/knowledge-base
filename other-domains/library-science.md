# Library & Information Science

## Overview

Library and information science (LIS) is the field concerned with the collection, organization, preservation, and dissemination of information and knowledge. It encompasses traditional librarianship, archival science, information architecture, and digital curation. In an era of information overload, LIS professionals are essential for ensuring that knowledge is findable, accessible, trustworthy, and preserved for future generations.

## Core Concepts

### Information Organization
- **Cataloging**: Descriptive cataloging (title, author, physical description) and subject cataloging
- **Classification Systems**: Dewey Decimal (DDC), Library of Congress (LCC), Universal Decimal (UDC)
- **Metadata**: Descriptive, structural, administrative; Dublin Core, MODS, METS
- **Subject Analysis**: Indexing, abstracting, controlled vocabularies, thesauri (LCSH, MeSH)
- **Linked Data & Semantic Web**: RDF, ontologies, knowledge graphs; connecting information across systems

### Reference & Information Services
- **Reference Interview**: Understanding user needs; question negotiation; reference transactions
- **Information Literacy**: Ability to find, evaluate, and use information effectively; ACRL Framework
- **Research Assistance**: Database searching, citation management, systematic reviews
- **Reader's Advisory**: Matching readers with materials; genre knowledge; discovery services
- **Community Engagement**: Programming, outreach, partnerships; libraries as community hubs

### Collection Development & Management
- **Selection Criteria**: Relevance, quality, demand, diversity, budget; collection development policies
- **Acquisition Models**: Firm orders, approval plans, demand-driven acquisition (DDA), patron-driven
- **Weeding & Deselection**: CREW method; maintaining relevant, current collections
- **Digital Collections**: E-books, databases, streaming media; licensing vs ownership
- **Special Collections**: Rare books, manuscripts, local history; unique materials

### Digital Libraries & Repositories
- **Digital Preservation**: Format migration, emulation, bit-level preservation; OAIS reference model
- **Institutional Repositories**: Collecting and providing access to institutional scholarly output
- **Digital Curation**: Selection, organization, and long-term maintenance of digital assets
- **Full-Text Search**: Indexing, ranking algorithms, relevance feedback; Elasticsearch, Solr
- **Digital Rights Management**: Copyright, licensing, fair use, Creative Commons

### Archival Science
- **Archival Principles**: Provenance (respects des fonds), original order, collective control
- **Appraisal**: Deciding what to preserve; evidential, informational, and intrinsic value
- **Arrangement & Description**: Creating finding aids; hierarchical description (fonds, series, files, items)
- **Preservation**: Environmental control, disaster planning, digitization, conservation treatment
- **Access**: Reading rooms, digitized collections, born-digital access; balancing access and privacy

### Information Architecture & UX
- **Information Architecture**: Organizing, labeling, and structuring information environments
- **Navigation Design**: Menus, breadcrumbs, search, faceted navigation; findability
- **User Experience (UX)**: User research, personas, usability testing; designing for diverse users
- **Taxonomy & Ontology**: Controlled vocabularies, hierarchical structures, knowledge organization systems
- **Accessibility**: WCAG compliance; screen reader compatibility; inclusive design

### Research Methods in LIS
- **Bibliometrics**: Citation analysis, h-index, journal impact factor, co-citation analysis
- **User Studies**: Surveys, interviews, ethnography, observation; understanding information behavior
- **Information Behavior**: How people seek, use, and share information; models (Wilson, Kuhlthau)
- **Systematic Reviews**: PRISMA guidelines; comprehensive, reproducible literature synthesis
- **Action Research**: Practitioner-led inquiry; improving services through evidence

### Ethics & Intellectual Freedom
- **Intellectual Freedom**: Freedom to read; opposing censorship; ALA Library Bill of Rights
- **Privacy**: Confidentiality of patron records; surveillance concerns; data ethics
- **Copyright & Fair Use**: Balancing access with rights holder interests; open access movement
- **Diversity & Inclusion**: Representing diverse voices in collections; equitable access; decolonizing classification
- **Digital Divide**: Ensuring access for underserved communities; broadband equity; digital literacy

## Key Theories

| Theory | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Information Poverty (Chatman) | Marginalized communities have limited access to information due to social norms |
| Sense-Making (Dervin) | People construct meaning through gaps in their understanding; information bridges gaps |
| Information Ground (Fisher) | Places where people share information unintentionally in the course of other activities |
| Five Laws of Library Science (Ranganathan) | Books are for use; every reader their book; every book its reader; save time; library is growing organism |

## Important Figures

- **S.R. Ranganathan**: Father of library science in India; Five Laws; colon classification
- **Melvil Dewey**: Dewey Decimal Classification; modern library professionalism
- **Shiyali Ramamrita Ranganathan**: Faceted classification; library science theory
- **Jesse Shera**: Social epistemology; libraries as knowledge institutions
- **Caroline Warehouse**: Information science pioneer; documentation theory
- **Brenda Dervin**: Sense-Making methodology; user-centered information research

## Frontiers

- **AI in Libraries**: Automated cataloging, recommendation systems, chatbots, AI-assisted research — how does the librarian's role change?
- **Open Science**: Open access, open data, preprints; transforming scholarly communication
- **Digital Preservation at Scale**: Preserving the web, social media, born-digital cultural heritage; petabyte challenges
- **Data Literacy**: Teaching citizens to find, evaluate, and use data; libraries as data literacy hubs
- **Indigenous Knowledge**: Preserving and providing access to indigenous knowledge systems on their own terms; respecting cultural protocols

## Applications

- **Academic Libraries**: Research support, information literacy instruction, scholarly communication
- **Public Libraries**: Community services, early literacy, digital inclusion, workforce development
- **Special Libraries**: Corporate, law, medical, government; specialized collections and services
- **Archives**: Historical preservation, records management, digital archiving
- **Information Management**: Knowledge management in organizations; taxonomy design; content strategy
- **Publishing**: Editorial, metadata, digital platforms; open access publishing

## Classic Works

- **"The Organization of Information"** by Joudrey & Taylor — Comprehensive introduction to cataloging, classification, and metadata
- **"Foundations of Library and Information Science"** by Rubin — Standard textbook covering the field's history, principles, and practice
- **"Introduction to Information Science"** by Bawden & Robinson — European perspective on information science foundations
- **"Information Architecture"** by Rosenfeld, Morville & Arango — The classic guide to organizing and structuring information environments
- **"The Atlas of New Librarianship"** by R. David Lankes — Reframing librarianship around facilitating knowledge creation
