# Communication & Media Studies

## Overview

Communication studies examines how people create, share, and interpret messages through various channels. It spans interpersonal, organizational, mass, and digital communication, and intersects with journalism, public relations, rhetoric, and media studies. It is both a theoretical discipline and a practical profession.

## Core Concepts

### Communication Theory
- **Shannon-Weaver Model**: Sender → message → channel → receiver → feedback; noise
- **Transactional Model**: Simultaneous sending and receiving; context-dependent
- **Semiotics**: Signs, symbols, meaning-making; denotation and connotation (Saussure, Peirce)
- **Framing**: How presentation shapes interpretation (Entman, Goffman)
- **Agenda-Setting**: Media tells people what to think about, not what to think (McCombs & Shaw)
- **Cultivation Theory**: Long-term media exposure shapes worldview (Gerbner)
- **Uses & Gratifications**: Audiences actively choose media to meet needs

### Rhetoric & Persuasion
- **Aristotle's Appeals**: Ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion), logos (logic)
- **Rhetorical Situation**: Exigence, audience, constraints (Bitzer)
- **Narrative Paradigm**: Humans are storytelling animals; good reasons vs narrative probability (Fisher)
- **Propaganda & Manipulation**: Techniques; media literacy; critical analysis
- **Public Speaking**: Speech organization; delivery; audience analysis; visual aids

### Journalism
- **News Values**: Timeliness, proximity, impact, conflict, novelty, prominence, human interest
- **Journalism Ethics**: Accuracy, fairness, independence, accountability, minimizing harm
- **Investigative Journalism**: In-depth reporting; source protection; public interest
- **Digital Journalism**: Online-first; data journalism; multimedia storytelling; social media reporting
- **Citizen Journalism**: User-generated content; blogs; social media as news source
- **Press Freedom**: Fourth estate; media independence; press freedom indices

### Mass Communication
- **Media Effects**: Agenda-setting, framing, priming, cultivation, third-person effect
- **Media Ownership**: Concentration; conglomerates; impact on content diversity
- **Advertising**: Persuasion techniques; media planning; brand communication
- **Entertainment Media**: Television, film, gaming, streaming; narrative transportation
- **Global Media**: Cultural imperialism; contra-flows; media globalization

### Public Relations
- **PR Functions**: Media relations, crisis communication, corporate communication, public affairs
- **PR Models**: Press agentry, public information, two-way asymmetric, two-way symmetric (Grunig)
- **Crisis Communication**: Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT); response strategies
- **Stakeholder Management**: Identifying, prioritizing, engaging stakeholders
- **Reputation Management**: Building, protecting, restoring organizational reputation

### Organizational Communication
- **Internal Communication**: Employee engagement; intranets; town halls; cascading messages
- **Leadership Communication**: Vision, change management, storytelling, authenticity
- **Communication Networks**: Formal and informal channels; gatekeepers; grapevine
- **Intercultural Communication**: High-context vs low-context (Hall); cultural dimensions (Hofstede)
- **Conflict Communication**: Styles (avoiding, accommodating, competing, compromising, collaborating); mediation

### Digital & Social Media
- **Social Media Platforms**: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, WeChat, Weibo
- **Content Strategy**: Content creation, curation, distribution; platform-specific strategies
- **Influencer Marketing**: Types (mega, macro, micro, nano); authenticity; metrics
- **Viral Communication**: Memes; shareability; network effects; echo chambers
- **Misinformation & Disinformation**: Fake news; fact-checking; media literacy; platform responsibility
- **Algorithmic Media**: Filter bubbles; recommendation systems; attention economy
- **Digital Ethics**: Privacy, consent, data use, platform governance, AI-generated content

### Media History
- **Print Revolution**: Gutenberg press; newspapers; pamphlets; mass literacy
- **Broadcast Era**: Radio (1920s); television (1950s); shared cultural experiences
- **Cable & Satellite**: 24-hour news; niche channels; fragmentation
- **Internet Age**: Web 1.0 → 2.0 → social media; democratization of publishing
- **Mobile & Streaming**: Smartphones; on-demand content; attention fragmentation

## Key Theories

| Theory | Key Figure | Core Idea |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Agenda-Setting | McCombs & Shaw | Media influences what topics people consider important |
| Cultivation | Gerbner | Heavy TV viewing shapes perceptions of reality |
| Uses & Gratifications | Katz, Blumler | Audiences actively choose media to satisfy needs |
| Spiral of Silence | Noelle-Neumann | Minority opinions suppressed; majority amplified |
| Medium is the Message | McLuhan | The medium shapes society more than its content |
